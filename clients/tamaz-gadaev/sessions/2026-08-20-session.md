# Session — 2026-08-20

Confidential — see `../../README.md`. Raw transcript: `../../../sources/transcripts/clients/tamaz-gadaev/2026-08-20-session.vtt` (Russian, WEBVTT). Session opened with a short grounding/breathing exercise, described by Vlad as a "condensed session" following on directly from the prior one.

## Opening thread: sleep deprivation is becoming more noticeable to him

Tamaz reported that since the last session, he's started noticing more directly when he's sleep-deprived — citing a day where he felt low mood and apathy, traced it to poor sleep the night before (asleep normally, but waking around 6:30am regardless), and described the negative signal ("this is a bad day") as something he's getting better at registering rather than pushing through. Vlad reinforced this explicitly as progress worth registering with his nervous system, calling it important groundwork for the current phase of the work.

## The core tension: what keeps him up

Tamaz named two things that reliably interfere with going to bed on time:

1. **Work he hasn't finished.** A felt need to not show up the next day "with empty results" — described as partly a social pressure tied to leading a team, not wanting to arrive without progress to report.
2. **Wanting to enjoy the day a bit longer** — not wanting to sleep because something is engaging, or wanting unstructured time before ending the day.

He distinguished a **long-term, rational part** of himself that understands the cost of under-sleeping from an **in-the-moment part** that overrides it nightly — later reframed in-session as a "two wolves" conflict (see below).

## The "fixing a loss" pattern

Tamaz described a self-imposed accounting: if he judges that he "did nothing" on a given day, he registers it as a loss — with an added social dimension, since as team lead he doesn't want to show up having accomplished nothing. Vlad asked how often, by this internal accounting, he actually reaches "satisfied" — Tamaz initially recalled a past estimate of roughly 3-in-10, but revised this in-session, saying the real number is currently lower and that the situation has changed materially since he first framed it that way (more tracks in the startup are currently going well, roughly "60/40").

**A useful moment:** Vlad asked directly whether anyone had ever actually required him to justify an unproductive day. Tamaz said no — and located the standard as internally generated: "a set of internal standards or expectations... how I imagine it would be, ideally." He described this standard as net-positive overall (helps him model reliability and follow-through for his team, especially under real deadline pressure) but with a **false-positive failure mode**: the same drive that helps him push through legitimate crunch also fires when pushing through would actually cost him — specifically, on nights when he needs to recover.

## The "two wolves" reframe and the false-positive test

Vlad introduced the frame directly: two competing internal drives, one wants to keep working, one knows he needs rest. Tamaz's own proposed test for telling them apart in the moment: stop, close his eyes, and talk to himself — check whether the anxious part can be reassured ("it's fine, nothing bad will happen if I sleep, I can promise to finish this tomorrow and actually protect the time for it").

They discussed calibrating this test against real thresholds Tamaz named for himself:
- **3 nights in a row under 5 hours** — starts losing functional capacity ("loses all ability"). Recovery needs roughly 1.5 days, including one full low-output day with ~9 hours of sleep.
- **5 days at 6 hours** — also starts to erode him.
- **2–3 weeks at 7 hours** — similarly erosive over a longer horizon.
- He estimated he could tolerate **up to about 2 weeks** of a bad sleep pattern for a genuinely justified push (e.g., a hard startup deadline) before it becomes a problem regardless of justification. Beyond that, "not worth it" by his own account.

He was clear that his actual concern is the **unjustified** version of the pattern — sacrificing sleep with no real payoff — rather than deliberate short-term pushes for a real deadline, which he considers an acceptable, bounded trade.

## The evening-vs-morning distortion

Vlad introduced the observation that problems reliably look larger in the evening than they do in the morning, and asked Tamaz to find language for this that would function as a real counterweight in the moment it's needed — not just an abstract fact.

## Homework agreed

1. **An 8pm (or similar) "evening close-out" session, roughly 5 minutes.** Trigger: not a fixed clock reminder, but tied to something concrete — Tamaz proposed anchoring it to his Whoop buzzing/vibrating at a set time, since abstract time-based cues don't reliably work for him (mornings are already packed with back-to-back meetings, so there's no natural anchor point there anymore — this replaces an old habit he used to have of logging a sleep/mood table right after waking, which no longer fits his schedule). The 5 minutes: pause, decide how he wants to end the day, and — if the anxious/pushing part is active — explicitly reassure it and make a concrete promise about when the unfinished work will actually get done.
2. **Revive the morning recovery-data check.** He has a small existing habit of checking his Whoop recovery score first thing and reacting to it (pleased if green/high HRV, disappointed if not) — described as "gamified," low-friction, and something he'd let lapse. Agreed to reinstate it as a low-resistance anchor for re-engaging with sleep data daily.
3. **A 1pm sleepiness check-in.** Discussed as a way to reinforce noticing his own resourced state during the day, not just failure signals — logging when he notices he is *not* sleepy as a small deliberate win ("I remember what sleepy feels like, and I'm glad I'm not, that means I can actually work right now"), tied to a second Whoop-style reminder rather than a fixed clock time.

## Identity work: what "sleep" means to the resourced version of him

Near the end, Vlad asked Tamaz to fully inhabit the "gives WiFi" identity — operating at real capacity, energizing the people around him — and then asked what sleep means to that version of himself. Tamaz's answer: that version doesn't consciously think about sleep at all; sleep is simply accepted as an "enabler," something to be grateful to, not something actively managed or resisted. He also noted a real effect where missing one night makes gratitude/awareness of this increase temporarily, but it fades if a bad pattern continues past a single night.
