# Instagram/TikTok content idea bank — entheogen.expert

50 ideas from the "INSTA Strategy" document (2026-08-30), organized by the seven content pillars defined in `../../platforms/instagram/strategy.md`. **None of these are filmed. This is the idea layer, not the draft layer** — compare to `drafts/tiktok-entheogen-series-03.md`, which holds finished, ready-to-post scripts. Pull a script from any entry here on request, following the fully-worked template in the final section.

Per idea: **Hook** (spoken opener) · **Overlay** (on-screen cover text) · **Core** (the actual idea/mechanism/distinction) · segment(s) it targets · R(isk)/C(ommercial relevance) level from the source analysis.

## Pillar 1 — Insight is not yet change (10 ideas)

1. **Three weeks later, nothing has changed.** Hook: "It has been three weeks. Nothing in your life is different." Overlay: MOST COMMON OUTCOME. Core: insight has no delivery mechanism attached to it, so it decays like any unrehearsed information; name the gap as structural, not personal. Segment: Stalled. R: Low · C: Highest. *This is the series anchor — see the full worked-out script in the final section.*
2. **Name one thing you do differently.** Hook: "You can still recite what you understood. Name one thing you do differently because of it." Overlay: RECALL IS NOT CHANGE. Core: recall and behavior are separate systems; recall is cheap. Segment: Stalled, Faded. R: Low · C: High.
3. **Insight is information. Behavior is a system.** Hook: "You did not come back short on understanding. You came back short on structure." Overlay: UNDERSTANDING ≠ STRUCTURE. Core: two columns — what an insight gives you vs. what a system gives you; the gap is the whole problem. Segment: Stalled. R: Low · C: High.
4. **Telling the story is not doing the work.** Hook: "You have told that story eleven times. That is not integration. That is a very good story." Overlay: THE STORY IS NOT THE WORK. Core: narration can substitute for action and feel identical from the inside; name the substitution without shaming, then give the test (does anyone in your life notice a difference). Segment: Faded, Returner. R: Low · C: High.
5. **Knowing why has never stopped anyone.** Hook: "You now understand exactly why you do it. You are still doing it. That is not a flaw in you." Overlay: WHY IS NOT HOW. Core: insight into causation is a poor predictor of behavior change on its own; separate explanation from intervention. Segment: Stalled. R: Low · C: High.
6. **The insight did not fade. The conditions never changed.** Hook: "It did not wear off. You went back to the exact same week." Overlay: SAME WEEK, SAME RESULT. Core: environment as the variable nobody adjusts — one concrete example (same calendar, same sleep, same people, same outcome). Segment: Stalled, Faded. R: Low · C: High.
7. **What did you actually try to change in the first two weeks.** Hook: "Answer honestly. In the fourteen days after, what did you actually do differently?" Overlay: FOURTEEN DAYS. Core: most people change nothing concrete and expect a delayed effect anyway; that's the diagnosis, not the failure. Segment: Stalled, Returner. R: Low · C: Highest.
8. **You did not waste it.** Hook: "You did not waste it. You have an insight with nothing attached to it. Those are different problems." Overlay: NOT WASTED. UNATTACHED. Core: relief first, mechanism second, one action third — for the permission-seeking hidden-demand layer. Segment: Faded, Stalled. R: Low · C: High.
9. **The distance between "I saw it" and "I do it."** Hook: "Seeing it and doing it are not the same skill. Nobody told you otherwise, and the whole field is built on pretending they are." Overlay: TWO DIFFERENT SKILLS. Core: perception versus practice, two-column format. Segment: Stalled. R: Low · C: High.
10. **Monday, not the mountaintop.** Hook: "The experience was Saturday. The work is Monday. Almost all the attention goes to Saturday." Overlay: INTEGRATION STARTS MONDAY. Core: attention allocation as the diagnosis, then one Monday-sized action. Segment: Stalled, Re-doser. R: Low · C: High.

## Pillar 2 — Meaning without certainty (6 ideas)

11. **It meant something. That does not make it true.** Hook: "It meant something. That does not mean it was true. Both can hold, and you do not have to pick this month." Overlay: MEANINGFUL ≠ ACCURATE. Core: significance and accuracy are separate claims, neither settles the other. Segment: Destabilized. R: Low · C: High.
12. **You do not have to decide what reality is by Friday.** Hook: "You are trying to resolve a question about reality inside three weeks. Nobody has ever needed to do that." Overlay: NO DEADLINE. Core: lower the tempo, give permission to hold it open, name what usually happens when people close it too fast. Segment: Destabilized. R: Low · C: Medium.
13. **Two people, same experience, two conclusions.** Hook: "Two people have the same experience. One concludes the universe is conscious. One concludes their marriage is over. The experience did not decide that." Overlay: THE EXPERIENCE DOES NOT INTERPRET ITSELF. Core: priming and context as the interpretive layer — a caution, not a debunk. Segment: Destabilized, Audience. R: Low · C: Medium. Strongest share format in this pillar.
14. **The rush to explain it.** Hook: "The urge to explain what happened is not always insight. Sometimes it is anxiety with better vocabulary." Overlay: CLOSURE IS NOT UNDERSTANDING. Core: distinguish sitting with something from resolving it prematurely. Segment: Destabilized. R: Low · C: Medium.
15. **Your partner is not dismissing you.** Hook: "Your partner is not dismissing you. They are hearing words that mean nothing to them and watching you change." Overlay: TRANSLATE, DO NOT RECRUIT. Core: three sentences that work with a skeptical person, one that never does. Segment: Destabilized, Returner. R: Low · C: High. Strongest send-to-a-specific-person format on the account.
16. **Significance without a metaphysics.** Hook: "You can take it completely seriously and commit to none of the metaphysics. That option exists and nobody offers it." Overlay: SERIOUS WITHOUT DOCTRINE. Core: the third position between belief and dismissal — the entire brand position sits in this gap. Segment: Destabilized. R: Low · C: Medium.

## Pillar 6 — Preparation without fantasy (6 ideas — numbered 17–22 in source order)

17. **Having done it before does not protect you.** Hook: "You have done this before, so you assume you're safer. The research does not support that as clearly as you'd like." Overlay: EXPERIENCE IS NOT PROTECTION. Core: state the finding, state its limits, do not extrapolate — prior experience does not predict lower risk. Segment: Researcher, Re-doser. R: Medium · C: Medium.
18. **Four months of reading is not preparation.** Hook: "You have read about this for four months. That is not preparation. That is a different activity with a similar feeling." Overlay: READING ≠ PREPARING. Core: past a point, research becomes avoidance with a productive appearance; name what preparation actually consists of at the level of questions, not logistics. Segment: Researcher. R: Low · C: Medium.
19. **Questions worth answering honestly first.** Hook: "Five questions worth answering honestly before you go. None of them are about what to expect." Overlay: ASK YOURSELF FIRST. Core: questions only, no advice, no logistics — routes to the self-assessment asset. Segment: Researcher. R: Medium · C: Medium.
20. **Categories, not advice.** Hook: "There are categories of people for whom the evidence is thin and the caution is high. I will name the categories. I will not tell you what to do." Overlay: SCREENING IS NOT A FORMALITY. Core: contraindication categories (psychotic disorders, bipolar disorder, trauma history explicitly flagged in the literature as under-researched) plus an honest statement that the research is incomplete. Segment: Researcher. R: Medium · C: Low.
21. **If your main reason is that your life is not working.** Hook: "If the honest reason is that your life is not working, that's worth examining before, not after." Overlay: THE MOTIVE SHAPES THE AFTERMATH. Core: an unexamined motive tends to shape the interpretation afterward, and the situation is still there in the morning. Segment: Researcher. R: Medium · C: Medium.
22. **Comfort and support are risk variables, not luxuries.** Hook: "The things people treat as atmosphere show up in the risk data as variables." Overlay: SETTING IS A VARIABLE. Core: absence of physical comfort and social support is a documented risk factor, not aesthetic preference. Segment: Researcher. R: Medium · C: Low.

## Pillar 4 — Difficult experiences and referral literacy (8 ideas — numbered 23–30, all permanently CTA-free)

23. **When this is not a coaching problem.** Hook: "Some of what people bring me is outside what I do. Here is exactly where that line is." Overlay: OUTSIDE COACHING. Core: name the categories, point to licensed local care, state that worsening or loss of function means urgent. Segment: Shaken, Wrong Room. R: Medium · C: None by design.
24. **One in four reported something lasting a week or more.** Hook: "About one in four reported something lasting more than a week. You are not unusual. You may still need someone qualified." Overlay: NOT UNUSUAL. Core: normalization plus the sample limitation plus the referral, all three, every time. Segment: Shaken, Stalled. R: Medium · C: Low.
25. **Misdiagnosis is a documented risk.** Hook: "If you go for help, there is a documented risk of the wrong label. That is a reason to describe it precisely, not to avoid help." Overlay: DESCRIBE IT PRECISELY. Core: the finding, then the practical implication — precise description improves the odds of correct diagnosis. Segment: Shaken, Wrong Room. R: Medium · C: None.
26. **How to describe what happened to a clinician.** Hook: "You're worried about what happens if you tell a doctor. Here's how to say it so you're heard and not filed under the wrong heading." Overlay: SAY IT PRECISELY. Core: describe symptoms, timeline, and function — not substance narrative, not interpretation. Segment: Shaken, Wrong Room. R: Medium · C: None. Pure utility, no return expected — highest trust-per-second in this pillar.
27. **Flatness has more than one explanation.** Hook: "Flatness after an intense experience has more than one explanation. Only one of them is mine to work with." Overlay: MORE THAN ONE EXPLANATION. Core: list the possibilities (contrast effect, depressive process, dissociation) without assigning one, then route. Segment: Shaken, Stalled. R: Medium · C: Low.
28. **If the world still looks slightly wrong.** Hook: "If weeks later the world still looks slightly wrong, stop asking the internet and stop asking me." Overlay: LICENSED. LOCAL. NOW. Core: short, plain, no mechanism theater, straight to referral. Segment: Shaken, Wrong Room. R: **High** · C: None. Under 30 seconds; publish regardless of performance — the refusal is the service.
29. **Difficult does not automatically mean harmful.** Hook: "Most people who had a hard time still called it useful afterward. That is a real finding, and it is not permission to romanticize it." Overlay: BOTH THINGS ARE TRUE. Core: hold both — reassurance for the frightened, correction for the romanticizers (the 88%-regarded-as-growth finding). Segment: Shaken, Destabilized. R: Medium · C: Low.
30. **Stigma is keeping people out of care.** Hook: "People are avoiding the help they need because of how they think they'll be treated. The avoidance is doing more damage than the disclosure would." Overlay: AVOIDANCE HAS A COST. Core: name the mechanism (documented stigma effect), then the practical route to care. Segment: Shaken, Wrong Room. R: Medium · C: None.

## Pillar 5 — Before another experience (6 ideas — numbered 31–36)

31. **Before you book another one, answer this.** Hook: "Before you book another one, answer one question. What did you do differently after the last one?" Overlay: ONE QUESTION. Core: the variable that changed between people it stuck for and people it didn't is usually what happened in the following weeks, not the experience itself. Segment: Re-doser. R: Medium · C: **Highest.** Owns the highest-value segment.
32. **If the third did less than the first.** Hook: "If the third one did less than the first, more is unlikely to be the variable you're missing." Overlay: DIMINISHING RETURNS ARE INFORMATION. Core: treat the pattern as data, not failure — no mention of amount, direction, or substance. Segment: Re-doser. R: Medium · C: High.
33. **What that budget would buy instead.** Hook: "The money for the next one buys about three months of the boring part. That is not a pitch, it's arithmetic." Overlay: ARITHMETIC, NOT A PITCH. Core: the cleanest, most honest commercial argument available — name that Vlad benefits from this framing and let them decide anyway. Segment: Re-doser. R: Medium · C: **Highest.**
34. **Wanting another one is not a red flag by itself.** Hook: "Wanting to do it again is not a red flag. Doing it more often while your life gets smaller is a different conversation." Overlay: FREQUENCY UP, FUNCTION DOWN. Core: give the distinction so they can self-assess without shame. Segment: Re-doser. R: Medium · C: High.
35. **A structure problem does not respond to a bigger dose.** Hook: "You're solving a structure problem with a substance. Those are different categories and one of them doesn't answer the other." Overlay: WRONG CATEGORY. Core: category-error framing, no amounts, no comparisons, no direction. Segment: Re-doser. R: Medium · C: High. **Most likely idea in the bank to drift into dose talk — control tightly.**
36. **The fourteen days you skipped.** Hook: "There were fourteen days after the last one where this actually got decided. What happened in them?" Overlay: THE WINDOW. Core: reconstruct what those days actually looked like; routes to The 14 Day Return lead magnet. Segment: Re-doser, Returner. R: Low · C: High.

## Pillar 3 — Evidence versus story (8 ideas — numbered 37–44)

37. **Two studies, opposite conclusions.** Hook: "One study says the intensity of the experience predicts the outcome. Another says it does not. Both are peer reviewed." Overlay: THE FIELD DISAGREES WITH ITSELF. Core: present both (2022 mystical-experience study vs. 2025 crossover study), refuse to resolve it, explain why refusing is the accurate response. Segment: all, especially Audience. R: Low · C: Medium. The translator role in its purest form.
38. **That study had twenty-eight people in it.** Hook: "The study behind that caption had twenty-eight participants. It's still interesting. It's not what the caption said it proved." Overlay: CHECK THE N. Core: teach sample-size literacy as a recurring, repeatable format. Segment: Enthusiast, Destabilized. R: Low · C: Low.
39. **No visions does not mean it did not work.** Hook: "You got nothing. No visions, no revelation. That says less about the outcome than you've been told." Overlay: SPECTACLE IS NOT THE MEASURE. Core: honest presentation including the contradicting 2022 finding — the real disappointment cluster. Segment: Stalled, Re-doser. R: Low · C: High.
40. **The neuroplasticity claim is doing more work than the evidence supports.** Hook: "The plasticity window is the most repeated claim in this field and the least carefully stated." Overlay: OVERSTATED, NOT FALSE. Core: grant what's true, isolate the inference that doesn't follow, name what actually determines outcome — plasticity/entropy increases appear to settle back toward baseline; durable change to a neurobiological baseline is not well demonstrated. Segment: Enthusiast, Stalled. R: Low · C: Medium. The single most over-claimed mechanism in the space — expect pushback.
41. **Why these surveys overstate.** Hook: "The scary numbers and the impressive numbers come from the same kind of sample, and that sample overstates both." Overlay: SELF-SELECTED. Core: teach one methodological idea properly — the large prevalence surveys are self-selected online samples with heavy demographic skew. Segment: Enthusiast, Shaken. R: Low · C: Low.
42. **Integration is widely believed to help. It is not proven.** Hook: "I sell integration coaching. I am also not going to tell you it's proven, because it isn't." Overlay: PLAUSIBLE, NOT PROVEN. Core: state the honest evidential status of his own product — disclosing against his own interest is the differentiator nobody else will copy. Segment: all. R: Low · C: Medium. **Top-ranked idea in the bank by strategic value.**
43. **Access is expanding faster than support.** Hook: "Roughly sixteen thousand people have been through Oregon's programme. The support layer for what comes after has not grown at anything like that rate." Overlay: THE GAP IS STRUCTURAL. Core: context, not commentary — no policy opinion, two figures on screen (Oregon ~16,000 clients, Colorado 34 licensed centers, FDA pipeline). Segment: all. R: Low · C: Medium.
44. **"Science shows" is doing a lot of work in that caption.** Hook: "Every time you see 'science shows' in a caption about this, ask which science, how many people, and measuring what." Overlay: WHICH SCIENCE. HOW MANY. MEASURING WHAT. Core: three questions given as a permanent filter for the whole field. Segment: Enthusiast. R: Low · C: Low.

## Pillar 7 — Coaching, therapy, and the right next step (6 ideas — numbered 45–50)

45. **Ask any coach what they refer out.** Hook: "Before you pay any integration coach, ask what they refer out. If the answer takes more than five seconds, keep looking." Overlay: ASK WHAT THEY REFUSE. Core: give the buyer the standard, then be measured against it publicly. Segment: Buyer, Destabilized. R: Low · C: **Highest.** Highest-conversion idea for the fastest-closing segment.
46. **Therapy works on why. Coaching works on Monday.** Hook: "Therapy and coaching are not competitors. They're answering different questions, and confusing them costs people time." Overlay: DIFFERENT QUESTIONS. Core: two columns, clean, no superiority claim in either direction. Segment: all. R: Low · C: High.
47. **What a short certification does not give anyone.** Hook: "There is an accredited integration-coaching certification selling for under a hundred dollars with thousands of students. That's the market you're choosing from." Overlay: THE CREDENTIAL IS NOT THE SIGNAL. Core: give buyers a better filter without naming or attacking a specific program. Segment: Buyer. R: Low · C: High.
48. **This may be coaching. This may be clinical.** Hook: "Same sentence, two different situations. One is mine. One is not." Overlay: SAME WORDS, DIFFERENT ROOM. Core: three paired examples showing where the line falls between coaching and clinical territory. Segment: all. R: Medium · C: Medium.
49. **Comment reply: "can you help with this."** Hook: screenshot of a real comment, read aloud, answered as an adult. Overlay: the comment itself. Core: a self-generating, ongoing format — rotate between a qualified-question reply and a boundary-question reply so the audience learns both responses. Segment: all. R: Medium · C: High. Highest trust-per-second of any format on the account.
50. **What I do not do.** Hook: "Things I do not provide. I do not tell anyone what to take. I do not diagnose. I do not treat. I refer out and I say so." Overlay: WHAT I DO NOT DO. Core: pin permanently — qualifies every other video on the account. Segment: all. R: Low · C: **Highest, indirectly.**

## Fully-worked example — idea #1, "Three weeks later, nothing has changed"

The only idea in this bank scripted all the way out in the source document, since it's the anchor of pillar 1 and the whole calendar leans on it. **Provided as the source's own template — not new work generated for this request.** Use this structure when a script is requested for any other idea above.

**Primary script (45 seconds, ~130 words).** [Hook] Three weeks later, nothing has changed. [Recognition] You understood something real. You could still explain it now, word for word. And your life looks exactly the same as it did before. [Mechanism] Here's what I think is happening. An insight is information. Behavior is produced by conditions. You came back to the same calendar, the same sleep, the same people, the same week. Unchanged conditions reproduce the old behavior no matter how clearly you understood it. [Example] You understood you were living on other people's expectations. Then Monday arrived, and the calendar was still full of other people's expectations. [Boundary] Separate thing: if what's happening is not stalled change but flatness that won't lift, or the world still looking slightly wrong, that's not a coaching question — that goes to a licensed clinician where you live. [Next step] Pick one behavior, not one belief — something another person would notice inside a week. [CTA] Save this and read it back on a bad week.

**20-second version:** Three weeks later, nothing has changed. You understood something real. Your life looks identical. Insight is information. Behavior comes from conditions. You went back to the same calendar, the same week, the same people. Same conditions, same behavior. Pick one thing another person would notice inside a week — not a belief, a behavior. Save this for a bad week.

**60-second version** adds a calibration line before the mechanism ("I want to be careful here — I don't have a study telling me how often this happens. What I have is that it's the single most common thing people write to me about, and that's worth saying plainly rather than dressing up as research") and a softer close for the stalled-specific case.

**Spoken hook:** "Three weeks later, nothing has changed." Five words to the tension — no wind-up, no "so a lot of people ask me," no throat-clearing. Core tension lands before second two.

**Overlay text:** INSIGHT IS NOT YET CHANGE — compresses rather than repeats; spoken hook gives the situation, overlay gives the diagnosis. Cover frame carries this text, never a mid-sentence transcript fragment.

**Caption:** "Three weeks later, nothing has changed. This is the most common thing people write to me about after a significant experience, and almost nobody says it out loud, because saying it feels like admitting you wasted something. You did not waste it. You have an insight with nothing attached to it. Those are different problems and only one of them is about you. Insight is information. Behavior comes from conditions. If the calendar, the sleep, the demands, and the people are unchanged, the behavior tends to be unchanged too. That is not a willpower failure. It's what happens when nothing in the environment was asked to change. One caveat I'll keep repeating: if what you're describing is not stalled change but flatness that won't lift, or the world still looking slightly wrong weeks later, that's not a coaching question — that's a licensed clinician where you live. For the stalled version, start smaller than you want to. One behavior, not one belief. Something someone else in your life would notice inside a week. Education, not medical advice. I am not a therapist or a clinician."

**Hashtags** — three principles: no substance names ever, including the obvious ones; no recovery/crisis tags (pull an audience Vlad isn't equipped to serve and attract moderation attention); weight toward behavior-change rather than topic. Core set on every video in this series (4–5): #integration #behaviorchange #habitchange #nervoussystem #afterthepeak. Rotating set, 2–3 per post: #insight #selfawareness #personalgrowth #harmreduction #evidencebased #meaningmaking #identityshift. **Never:** any compound name; any retreat/venue/country tag; #healing #awakening #consciousness #plantwisdom and similar (anything that would make an evangelist audience feel found). Keep total under 8; on Instagram, put them in the first comment, not the caption, so the caption reads as writing.

**CTA:** "Save this and read it back on a bad week." Reason-bound save, not a bare request — that's what makes it work. No link, no DM invitation, no offer; this video's only job is to be recognized and kept. The offer comes later in the series, from ideas 7 and 31. Secondary option if testing comment engagement instead of saves: "Name one thing you do differently. One sentence." Do not run both — one cognitive job, one ask.

**Boundary note — three things doing deliberate work in this script.** The clinical separation is inside the video, not just the caption (people who need it may not read captions, so it's stated as a different situation rather than a warning, which keeps it from functioning as alarm). No claim that integration or coaching fixes this — the script names the gap and gives one action; it does not say working with Vlad closes it, because that's not demonstrated and claiming it would undercut the whole position. No prevalence claim dressed as research — the 60-second version states explicitly that this is practice observation, not a study; that calibration line is not padding, it's what separates this from every other integration account, and should stay in whenever runtime allows. **Comment handling for this video specifically:** it will surface distress in the replies, because "nothing changed" is also what some people say when the actual situation is flatness or dissociation — watch for that. Public reply, short, points to licensed local support, no DM invitation, no offer. Do not move an acute conversation into DMs.

**Evidence tier.** Mechanism claim (insight doesn't automatically convert to behavior): Tier D, moderate — supported indirectly by the literature defining integration as the process by which an experience translates into daily-life change, which presupposes the translation often fails; not a measured prevalence. Prevalence claim ("most common thing people write to me about"): Tier E, practitioner observation only — no study cited, none should be implied; the 60-second version states this openly, don't let the shorter cut imply otherwise. Clinical-boundary claim: Tier D, strong — persisting negative effects after psychedelic experiences are documented across multiple surveys, and DPDR misdiagnosis is a documented risk. Net evidential posture: honest, calibrated, no overreach — the script never claims a mechanism is proven or that acting on it produces a specific outcome.

**Platform/medical/legal risk.** Platform: very low — no compound named, no dosing, no sourcing, no logistics, no imagery; nothing here would register as drug content to a classifier; anchor the series with this one specifically because of that. Medical: low to moderate, entirely in the comments — the video makes no clinical claim and diagnoses nothing, but invites disclosure from people whose situation isn't the one described; mitigated by the in-video boundary and disciplined comment handling. Legal: none identified — no advice, no jurisdiction-specific content, no encouragement.

**Two alternative hooks** to A/B against the primary, logged in the same tracking sheet as Series 02: (A) reflective-question variant, "Name one thing you do differently" — tests whether a direct question outperforms a statement of situation, higher friction at second one, potentially higher comment quality. (B) contradiction variant, "It did not wear off. You went back to the same week." — denies the explanation the viewer has already reached, creates tension without alarm, slightly harder to land, higher ceiling if it does.

**Two alternative overlays:** (A) THE GAP IS STRUCTURAL — leads with the reframe, better paired with the recognition hook since it answers the implied "why." (B) SAME WEEK, SAME RESULT — leads with the mechanism in compressed form, best paired with alternative hook B. Both stay under five words and compress rather than restate.

**Comment reply that becomes the next video.** Likely comment: "Six months later and I still talk about it more than I act on it. How do you tell the difference between processing and avoiding?" Reply: "Good question, and a hard one to answer from the inside. One rough test: processing usually changes what you do next, even slightly. Avoiding tends to leave the behavior identical while the explanation gets more sophisticated. What has actually changed in six months, apart from how well you can describe it?" **The follow-up video this becomes:** comment-reply format (idea 49, pillar 7), screenshot on screen, read aloud, answered to camera — this is pillar 1 idea 4 in the library, "telling the story is not doing the work." That video will outperform this one on trust-per-second and costs nothing to produce, because the audience wrote the brief.
