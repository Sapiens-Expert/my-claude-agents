# Pain-aware activity support

## 1. Confirm scope

Establish the coaching goal, known diagnosis/current care, clinician restrictions, competence, and whether exercise prescription or rehabilitation belongs with a qualified professional.

## 2. Screen for change and risk

Ask about acute deterioration, trauma, systemic illness, hot/swollen joint, neurological change, unexplained loss of function, and other red flags. Escalate rather than experiment when indicated.

## 3. Define valued function

Choose a meaningful activity outcome and current baseline. Avoid making posture, range, pain elimination, weight, or a proprietary movement score the goal unless it clearly serves the person's function and care plan.

## 4. Design the smallest tolerable experiment

With appropriate clinical clearance, select a modest activity dose, context, support, adaptation, and stop/modify rule. Change one main variable at a time.

## 5. Observe response

Track activity, symptoms during and after, next-day response, function, recovery, confidence, and adverse effects. Do not interpret a temporary response as diagnosis, damage, cure, or disease progression.

## 6. Adapt or refer

Progress, hold, reduce, substitute, or seek professional review according to predefined response and functional criteria. Respect preference and informed refusal; persistence is not always the correct objective.

## Boundary

This supports adherence and learning around an appropriate plan. It is not physiotherapy, medical exercise prescription, pain treatment, manual therapy, or rheumatology care.
