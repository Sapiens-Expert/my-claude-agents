# Motivational interviewing

Use partnership, acceptance, compassion, and evocation. Ask open questions, reflect, affirm accurately, summarize, and elicit the client’s own reasons for change. Do not use MI as covert persuasion or pretend competence beyond training.

## Processes

1. **Engaging:** build a working relationship and understand the person's perspective.
2. **Focusing:** develop and maintain a mutually agreed direction.
3. **Evoking:** elicit the person's own language for change while exploring ambivalence.
4. **Planning:** strengthen commitment and form a plan when readiness emerges.

Move forward and backward as needed. Do not force planning because a session is ending.

## Practice rules

- Reflect more than interrogate; use summaries to organize meaning and choice.
- Listen for both change talk and sustain talk without selectively ignoring either.
- Respond to discord as information about the relationship, focus, pace, or autonomy—not as client pathology.
- Ask permission before offering information or advice, then return to the client's interpretation.
- Avoid the righting reflex, debate, manufactured discrepancy, and questions designed to corner agreement.
- Distinguish MI-informed communication from competent/fidelity-assessed MI practice. Reading the method does not establish proficiency.
