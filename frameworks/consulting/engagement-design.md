# Consulting engagement design

## Conceptual agreement

Before a proposal, align with the economic buyer on:

- business objectives and why they matter now;
- measures of progress and completion;
- expected value, including uncertainty and non-financial value;
- scope boundaries, stakeholder roles, constraints, and risks;
- decision process, timing, and commercial terms.

## Proposal architecture

1. Situation and decision context.
2. Outcomes and measures.
3. Approach and workstreams.
4. Client and consultant responsibilities.
5. Deliverables and capability transfer.
6. Timeline, review cadence, assumptions, and change control.
7. Options and fees tied to scope/value logic rather than hours disguised as value.

## Delivery cadence

- Kickoff: confirm questions, evidence access, governance, and working norms.
- Weekly: decisions, learning, risks, dependencies, and next tests.
- Milestones: synthesize evidence and obtain explicit decisions.
- Close: measure outcomes, document unresolved risks, transfer artifacts, and agree follow-up.

## Ethical constraints

- Never invent financial value to support a fee.
- Avoid contingent incentives that compromise independent judgment.
- Surface conflicts of interest and limits of expertise.
- Do not create artificial dependence or withhold client-owned knowledge.
