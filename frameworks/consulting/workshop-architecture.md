# Workshop architecture

## Core sequence

### 1. Contract

Define the business outcome, output, participants, sponsor, facilitator role, decision authority, constraints, confidentiality, and evidence needed.

### 2. Prepare

Research customers/system/context, interview stakeholders, identify conflicts and accessibility needs, pre-read only what is necessary, and build an agenda with buffers and fallback routes.

### 3. Enter

Make purpose, rules, roles, agenda, decision method, and psychological/operational boundaries explicit. Activate relevant prior knowledge and clarify what will happen to the output.

### 4. Frame

Build a shared fact base. Separate facts, interpretations, assumptions, constraints, and unknowns. Define the problem before solving it.

### 5. Diverge

Use individual reflection, pairs/small groups, structured inquiry, visual mapping, scenarios, or games to expand perspectives and options.

### 6. Converge

Apply agreed criteria, test tradeoffs, invite dissent, combine or reject options, and state who makes the final call.

### 7. Commit

Translate choices into owners, authority, resources, milestones, measures, dependencies, risks, and the next review.

### 8. Transfer

Document decisions, distribute outputs, rehearse required behavior, capture unresolved questions, and run follow-up at the cadence appropriate to the work.

## Quality gates

- Every activity changes understanding, choice, capability, or commitment.
- Content blocks are shorter than the participant work they enable.
- No “consensus” claim without a stated decision rule and recorded dissent.
- No action item without an owner and next observable event.
- No learning claim without a transfer or performance measure.
- Sensitive health, personnel, commercial, or client discussion has an explicit privacy rule.
