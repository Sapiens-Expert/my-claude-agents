# Product validation loop

## 1. Frame the decision

Name the decision this evidence will inform, its deadline, constraints, owner, and stop condition.

## 2. Define the customer progress

Specify person/context, current behavior, desired outcome, alternatives, urgency, and access route. Separate buyer, user, beneficiary, and blocker where they differ.

## 3. Rank assumptions

List desirability, usability, feasibility, viability, safety/regulatory, privacy, and channel assumptions. Rank by consequence if false and uncertainty.

Map dependencies across customer, proposition, business-model and responsibility assumptions. Test “idea killers”—uncertain assumptions whose failure invalidates downstream work—before polishing or scaling dependent components.

## 4. Select evidence

Choose the least expensive ethical test capable of changing the decision: interview, concierge/manual delivery, prototype task, paid diagnostic, deposit, pilot commitment, or repeated-use test.

If a seed launch funds or co-develops the first version, disclose experimental status, minimum viable delivery, schedule, feedback/data rights, fair price, support and refund. Launch response tests a bounded offer/channel condition; it does not establish retained value or business-model fit.

## 5. Predefine the readout

Record sample, success/failure thresholds, qualitative signals, costs, timebox, and what will happen under pass, mixed, or fail results.

Use the Test Card and Learning Card in the [value-proposition evidence brief](../../templates/value-proposition-evidence-brief.md). Record denominator, expected reliability, privacy/ethics review, alternative explanations and what remains untested. Passing one test never validates the entire proposition.

## 6. Run and observe

Capture behavior, friction, context, objections, workarounds, value realization, adverse effects, support load, and drop-off. Do not coach participants toward the desired result.

## 7. Decide

Deepen, revise, narrow, pause, or stop. Update the business model and evidence register; never reinterpret a failed threshold as success after the fact.

## 8. Test repeatability

Only after initial value is demonstrated, test acquisition, delivery, retention, economics, safety, and founder/team capacity across enough cases to justify scale.
