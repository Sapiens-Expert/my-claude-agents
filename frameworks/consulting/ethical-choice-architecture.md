# Ethical choice architecture

## 1. Define welfare and authority

Identify the chooser, designer, sponsor, affected parties, intended welfare outcome, conflicts of interest, and who has authority to decide.

## 2. Diagnose behavior in context

Observe the current journey. Map capability/ability, opportunity/environment, motivation, prompts, friction, incentives, information, social norms, emotions, and structural constraints. Do not select a nudge before diagnosis.

## 3. Generate intervention levels

Consider information, interface/process, service, incentive, policy, resource, capability, and structural changes. Include a no-intervention option.

## 4. Run the autonomy and harm gate

Test transparency, comprehension, reversibility, opt-out cost, privacy, accessibility, vulnerable-state exploitation, distributional impact, dependency, regret, and sponsor benefit.

## 5. Pre-register the test

Define population, intervention, comparator, welfare outcome, behavior metric, guardrails, subgroup analysis, time horizon, failure threshold, and stop condition.

## 6. Pilot and inspect

Measure comprehension, behavior, outcome, persistence, unintended consequences, and who is excluded or burdened. Conversion alone cannot pass the test.

## 7. Decide and govern

Adopt, revise, reject, or sunset. Document rationale, owner, monitoring, complaint/appeal route, review date, and how a chooser can reverse the outcome.
