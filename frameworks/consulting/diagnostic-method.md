# Consulting diagnostic method

## Output

A defensible statement of the challenge, its material drivers, the evidence and uncertainty behind it, and the few actions most likely to change the outcome.

## Sequence

### 1. Contract

- Name the economic buyer, stakeholders, decision to be made, deadline, constraints, and access to evidence.
- Agree objectives, observable measures, and value before prescribing scope.

### 2. Frame

- Write a provisional problem statement.
- Distinguish observed facts, stakeholder interpretations, assumptions, and unknowns.
- Define the system boundary and time horizon.

### 3. Structure

- Build an issue tree or causal map appropriate to the problem.
- Use MECE as a checking discipline, not a claim that reality fits a perfect tree.
- Rank branches by potential impact, uncertainty, and cost of learning.

### 4. Test

- Gather the minimum sufficient mix of quantitative data, documents, observation, and interviews.
- Actively seek disconfirming evidence and alternative explanations.
- Update confidence rather than defending the opening hypothesis.

### 5. Diagnose

- State the central challenge and the few key drivers.
- Identify reinforcing constraints, bottlenecks, and decisions already made by default.
- Show what evidence would change the conclusion.

### 6. Respond

- Define a guiding policy that concentrates effort.
- Specify coherent actions, owners, sequence, resources, risks, and explicit non-priorities.
- Connect each action to a driver and an observable outcome.

### 7. Transfer

- Deliver the decision narrative, evidence pack, implementation map, and review cadence.
- Confirm internal ownership and capability transfer.

## Quality test

A diagnosis fails if it is merely a goal, slogan, long task list, template output, or restatement of the client’s symptoms.
