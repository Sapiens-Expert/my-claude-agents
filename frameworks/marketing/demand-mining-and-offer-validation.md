# Demand mining and offer validation

Use repeated questions, enquiries, objections and delivery patterns to generate responsible offer hypotheses. Signals reveal possible demand; they do not prove need, competence, willingness to pay, repeatability or customer value.

## 1. Capture the signal

Record the exact question or observed request, source/context, date, audience/account, frequency, current workaround, stakes, sensitivity and whether the person gave permission for follow-up. Remove or protect personal data and never mine health, trauma or psychedelic engagement for sales targeting.

## 2. Classify the evidence

Distinguish:

- curiosity or content interest;
- repeated informational need;
- contextual advice need;
- execution/service need;
- repeatable workflow;
- explicit budget or purchase inquiry;
- delivery/outcome evidence from existing customers.

Likes, followers, comments and hypothetical enthusiasm remain weak evidence.

## 3. Check authority and responsibility

Confirm capability, evidence, scope, conflicts and referral boundaries. Choose learner, practitioner or expert language accurately. If free information, an existing product, a referral or “not qualified” is the responsible answer, do not manufacture an offer.

## 4. Match the smallest valid response

| Signal pattern | Candidate response | Required validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated simple question | FAQ, post, checklist or template | Frequency, comprehension and public usefulness |
| Repeated contextual question | Paid consultation or diagnostic | Competence, standalone value, scope and willingness to pay |
| Repeated execution need | Defined service | Delivery process, acceptance criteria, margin, capacity and outcomes |
| Repeated standardized workflow | Product, course or tool | Generalizability, usability, support, completion and retained value |
| Budget mismatch | Smaller independently useful scope | Outcome integrity; no manipulative downsell |
| Complementary product | Disclosed referral/affiliate | Due diligence, fit, conflict and customer recourse |

## 5. Run the smallest ethical test

State audience, problem, outcome, scope, evidence, price, effort, alternatives, limitations and non-fit. Predefine behavioral evidence and stop/change thresholds. Do not use scarcity, social proof or audience access to compensate for weak value.

## 6. Evaluate delivery and economics

Measure problem comprehension, qualified inquiry, commitment, activation, outcome, completion/retention, refund, support burden, contribution margin, acquisition effort, founder capacity, concentration, complaints and harm.

## 7. Decide the offer state

Continue learning, publish free guidance, test consultation, standardize service, productize, partner/refer, narrow, pause or stop. Expansion follows repeated delivery and customer-outcome evidence—not audience size.
