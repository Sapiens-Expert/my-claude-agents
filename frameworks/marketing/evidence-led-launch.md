# Evidence-led launch

Use to coordinate research, education, a time-bounded offer, delivery and learning. A launch is a delivery-backed experiment—not proof of a durable business.

## 1. Establish readiness

Define audience/problem evidence, offer maturity, decision owner, price/terms, claims/proof, safe delivery capacity, accessibility, privacy, support, refund/cancellation, timeline, economics and stop/go thresholds. Apply the [capacity-first campaign framework](capacity-first-campaign.md) before creating urgency or accepting commitments.

Prelaunch questions test language, priorities and unresolved needs. Feedback does not promise inclusion, authorize sensitive-data reuse or validate demand.

## 2. Publish a disclosed education sequence

Each unit must stand alone as useful and disclose that a related offer will follow:

1. **Problem/opportunity:** what is changing, for whom, compared with what and why it matters.
2. **Method/evidence:** how the proposed approach works, supporting evidence, uncertainty and limitations.
3. **Application/fit:** effort, experience, objections, risks, alternatives and poor fit.
4. **Offer bridge:** explicit transition from education to commercial invitation.

Do not withhold material price, risk, eligibility, effort or limitations until checkout. Value creates no reciprocity debt.

## 3. Open clearly

State the offer, scope, deliverables, maturity/experimental status, price and total cost, dates, verified capacity, eligibility, proof, exclusions, support, privacy, accessibility and cancellation/refund. Provide a calm decision path and easy refusal. Opening does not authorize aggressive retargeting or new channels.

## 4. Close only on a real change

A deadline is legitimate only when an auditable operational condition changes: cohort start, allocation, actual capacity, contractual partner term or real price period. State what happens after close. Prohibit resetting countdowns, invented demand, escalating fear/shame and messages sent after the represented final notice.

## 5. Deliver before celebrating

Onboard, fulfil, support, recover failures and handle refunds. Monitor activation, completion, customer outcome, non-use, adverse effects, complaints, accessibility, support burden and team load before interpreting revenue.

## 6. Review the full launch

Record audience exposure, permission, application/fit, orders, revenue, refunds, chargebacks, fulfilment cost, contribution margin, cash timing, retention, customer outcome, complaints, partner effects, cannibalization, demand pulled forward and team recovery. Compare with always-on, smaller-cohort or no-launch alternatives where feasible.

## Variant gates

### Seed cohort

Use a small informed cohort to co-develop only when a minimum viable curriculum/service, experiment status, delivery calendar, feedback/data rights, fair price, support and refund are explicit. Participants are not buying a mature product. Their feedback is compensated or recognized according to the agreement.

### Permissioned internal launch

Use an existing audience only within the original relationship purpose, channel and frequency expectations. Relevance and opt-out govern segmentation.

### Partner/JV launch

Require a written agreement covering role, commission and incentives, claims and prohibited claims, approved/current assets, commercial disclosure, contact frequency, suppression/unsubscribe, complaints, data controller/processor responsibilities, attribution, payment/refund effects, records and termination. A partner's permission cannot be silently transferred.

### New-business launch

Treat response as evidence about message, offer and acquisition in that context. Continue testing retention, delivery, unit economics, safety and stakeholder fit before claiming a validated business.
