# Capacity-first campaign

Use when demand may exceed a bounded ability to deliver. The objective is excellent, fair delivery with a manageable demand buffer—not manufactured oversubscription.

## 1. Plan from real capacity

Define the capacity unit and period, binding constraint, quality threshold, support and accessibility load, contingency reserve, recovery time, offer, audience, evidence of need, price/obligations, campaign dates, fit criteria and delivery owner.

`real capacity = maximum feasible throughput - quality/safety reserve - support/contingency load`

The real number is a planning estimate with assumptions, not a scarcity claim until verified.

## 2. Build value and collect signals

Educate the market and invite low-friction, consented signals. Distinguish:

`exposure -> engagement -> explicit interest -> qualified conversation/application -> informed commitment -> delivered value`

Each step supports only the next bounded inference. Likes are not leads; a waitlist is not purchase intent or permission for repeated outreach. Publish enough information for informed nonparticipation.

## 3. Release transparently

Open a clearly described window. State actual quantity/capacity, dates, price, total obligations, eligibility, allocation method, cancellation/refund terms and what happens when capacity fills. Never fabricate countdowns, queues, cohort limits, stock or competing demand.

## 4. Follow through and allocate fairly

Diagnose mutual fit through relevant, permissioned communication. Use an allocation rule appropriate to the context:

- first-come with accessibility and outage safeguards;
- transparent lottery;
- needs-based priority;
- disclosed merit/fit criteria;
- safety or continuity priority.

Record the rule, reviewer, accommodations, conflicts, appeal/correction route and alternatives/referrals. Avoid opaque favoritism, wealth/status theatre and criteria that proxy protected traits.

## 5. Deliver and recover

Provide promised onboarding, service, support, accessibility and failure recovery within capacity. Monitor outcome, non-use, complaint, refund, workload, poor fit, adverse effect and referral. Scarcity without delivery excellence is under-supply, not brand value.

## 6. Learn before repeating

Compare signals, applications, commitments and delivery outcomes; retain negative evidence; thank participants; and use only consented, representative proof. Review acquisition/fulfilment cost, contribution margin, working capital, retention, repeat rate, opportunity cost, team recovery and excluded/underserved groups.

Decide whether to adjust price, capacity, offer, access, allocation, campaign frequency or stop. Diagnose chronic excess demand as possible underpricing, inequity, insufficient capacity or poor forecasting—not automatically success.

## Truthful-scarcity gate

A capacity/time claim is publishable only when its quantity, cause, owner, start/end, reset behavior and evidence are real and auditable. The constraint must not be created solely to impair deliberation. Explain reasonable alternatives and never hide essential health, safety or eligibility information behind the deadline.

## Contact and recovery budget

Set maximum campaign frequency, contact frequency, follow-up attempts, support load and team recovery interval. Respect opt-outs and nonresponse across channels. Urgency does not reset consent.
