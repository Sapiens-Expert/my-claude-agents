# Ethics, consent, and power

## Consent standard

Consent must be informed, specific, comprehensible, voluntary, ongoing, reversible, and possible to refuse without retaliation. Altered states can reduce capacity to negotiate new boundaries; material permissions must be discussed in advance and rechecked later.

## Power safeguards

- Name role, competence, limits, interests, fees, confidentiality, records, supervision, and complaint routes.
- Avoid dual relationships, dependency, exclusivity, secrecy, spiritual authority, and claims of privileged access to truth.
- No sexual or romantic conduct with clients; no sexualized touch or interpretation.
- Touch is never assumed, necessary by default, or newly negotiated during impairment. The safest coaching default is no touch beyond ordinary emergency/safety needs.
- Do not use client labor, stories, referrals, loyalty, or vulnerability for commercial or reputational gain.
- Maintain consultation/supervision and refer conflicts or needs beyond competence.

## Cultural safeguards

Distinguish respect, learning, and reciprocity from extraction or invented lineage. Do not present Indigenous or ceremonial practices as interchangeable techniques, claim authorization not held, or strip practices from community, history, and legal context.

## Accountability

Document concerns, preserve evidence, protect the affected person, use appropriate reporting/complaint pathways, and do not resolve serious harm through private reputation management.
