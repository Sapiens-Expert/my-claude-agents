# LinkedIn objective and role router

Use before changing a profile, page, content plan, search workflow or outreach motion. Choose one primary job for the initiative; secondary jobs may coexist only when their audiences, evidence and next steps remain coherent.

| Primary job | Audience decision | Required trust surface | Core motion | Meaningful outcome | Critical safeguard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Employment | Employer/recruiter assesses role fit | Truthful role proposition, readiness and work evidence | Search, selective applications, referrals and contextual contact | Qualified interviews and suitable offers | Vacancy verification, controlled résumé data, accessibility and truthful identity/location |
| Independent service | Buyer assesses whether to explore or hire | Problem scope, method, proof, fit, terms and contact route | Useful contribution, discovery and permissioned conversation | Qualified enquiries and good-fit clients | Claims, review authenticity, price/scope clarity and non-fit |
| Company presence | Stakeholder validates the organization | Verified page, positioning, proof and active ownership | Publish, respond, recruit and route stakeholder action | Trust, qualified action and operational continuity | Admin succession, employee autonomy, claim ownership and maintenance |
| Social selling | Account stakeholder evaluates relevance | Buyer-relevant expertise and substantiated proof | Research, listen, contribute, connect and progress with permission | Account learning, qualified conversation and assisted revenue | One contact record, no pretext/quotas and sensitive-signal exclusion |
| Recruiting | Candidate decides whether to engage | Credible recruiter/employer and transparent role | Lawful sourcing, contextual outreach and candidate care | Diverse qualified slate and fair hire | Job-related criteria, privacy, accessibility and anti-discrimination |

## Routing procedure

1. State the decision LinkedIn should help another person make.
2. Select the primary job and accountable owner.
3. Define audience, proof, content, contact path, outcome and non-goals.
4. List secondary jobs and test for promise, authority, privacy or CTA collisions.
5. Separate company/personal surfaces or campaigns where one coherent route is impossible.
6. Apply the relevant evidence, relationship, hiring, sales and platform-verification controls.
7. Review qualified outcomes, burden, confusion and harm; reroute or stop when the job changes.

Do not optimize generically. A profile built simultaneously as résumé, sales page, company brochure, recruiter identity and personal manifesto usually weakens comprehension and creates conflicting calls to action.

Output: use the objective section in the [LinkedIn operating brief](../../templates/linkedin-operating-brief.md).
