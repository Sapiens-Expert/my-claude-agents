# Purpose-to-practice alignment

Use to define a legitimate purpose, translate it into governing behavior and test whether products, incentives and outcomes support it. Purpose supplies direction and constraints; it does not replace strategy, competence or evidence.

## 1. Purpose: bounded stakeholder commitment

> We exist to **[create defined benefit/change]** for **[people/system]** because **[evidenced need and value commitment]**, while respecting **[rights, safety, exclusions and limits]**.

Test:

- Who participated in defining it, benefits from it and bears cost or risk?
- Does the organization have competence and legitimate standing?
- Which customers, claims, practices, partners or revenues would violate it?
- Which outcomes and adverse effects would support, challenge or retire it?
- Can stakeholders contest or revise it without challenging a founder's identity?

Purpose may be inherited, discovered, chosen or co-created. It is a revisable commitment, not necessarily one timeless essence.

## 2. Principles: observable HOW

For each principle define the protected value, required behavior, prohibited behavior, owner, affected stakeholders, competence/resources, evidence, tradeoffs, exception rule and escalation. Abstract nouns such as integrity or innovation are insufficient until they govern a real choice.

## 3. Practice and proof: observable WHAT

Audit products, services, pricing, policies, partnerships, employment, incentives, content, claims and resource allocation through:

`claimed alignment -> operational evidence -> stakeholder outcome -> contradiction/adverse evidence -> corrective decision`

A campaign, founder story or donation cannot compensate for conflicting operations.

## 4. Strategy and decision screen

For each material decision ask:

1. Which purpose outcome does this advance?
2. Which principles enable or constrain it?
3. Who benefits, decides, pays and may be harmed?
4. Does it fit the diagnosed challenge and coherent strategy?
5. Can the organization deliver it competently and sustainably?
6. What evidence supports it and what would falsify it?
7. Does it clarify or confuse the brand/project portfolio?
8. What pilot, review, rollback or exit is available?

Coherence is not rigidity. Record justified exceptions, duration, affected stakeholders and learning.

## 5. Trust audit

Assess trust through competence, reliability, honesty, fair process, workload/pay, distribution of benefit, privacy/security boundaries, protected voice/dissent, grievance/remedy and accountable oversight. Shared belief, enthusiasm, loyalty, conversion or retention alone does not establish trust.

## 6. Scale and adoption

Treat early adopters and diffusion stages as hypotheses tied to a specific behavior and social system. Record relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability, users, buyers, gatekeepers and affected nonusers. Scale after use, safety, retention and outcome evidence—not a universal tipping percentage or purpose resonance.

## 7. Split-risk and succession

Purpose is drifting when founder stories replace decision rules, incentives contradict stated principles, offers lack alignment decisions, dissent becomes disloyalty, stakeholder outcomes worsen while internal metrics improve or purpose campaigns compensate for weak value.

Preserve source history without mythologizing it; distribute stewardship; authorize revision; embed purpose in strategy, incentives, hiring and product gates; audit contradictions independently; and assess successors on competence, ethics, stakeholder legitimacy and stewardship. No founder is the sole interpreter.

## 8. Communication order

Purpose -> method/principles -> proof/offer is one useful sequence. Lead with product, evidence or urgent action when audience context requires it. Any “megaphone” scales only after clarity, operational proof and harm controls.
