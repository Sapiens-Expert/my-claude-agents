# Brand concept validation

Use to move a brand strategy into cross-functional prototypes, test interpretation and behavior, and decide whether to preserve, revise, pilot or stop.

## 1. Contract the concept

State the business/customer decision, audience and context, intended identity and position, category cues, meaningful differentiation, distinctive assets, promise/evidence, protected boundaries, owner and falsification condition.

If symbolic or archetypal language may help creative exploration, use the optional [meaning-pattern creative lens](meaning-pattern-creative-lens.md). Keep it subordinate to functional truth, audience evidence and category comprehension; never use it to type people or manufacture a permanent brand essence.

## 2. Design collaboration

| Dimension | Required decision |
| --- | --- |
| Accountability | Who owns customer/business outcome and final risk? |
| Stewardship | Where do evidence, assets and decision history remain? |
| Expertise | Which strategic, creative, operational, user and specialist perspectives participate? |
| Decision rights | Who proposes, critiques, approves, escalates or stops? |
| Integration | Which brief, prototype, evidence and asset system connects the work? |
| Conflicts/IP | How are incentives, confidentiality, ownership and reuse governed? |
| Continuity | What documentation, onboarding and succession preserve learning? |

Use prototypes as boundary objects. Make promise, journey, content, visual/verbal assets, product behavior, accessibility and edge cases inspectable before polishing.

## 3. Test through complementary methods

| Test | Question | Limit/control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swap | Could a competitor replace the identifier without incongruity? | Genericity diagnostic, not effectiveness proof |
| Hand | Is the source recognizable and fitting when the main identifier is hidden? | Familiarity can masquerade as quality; test varied audiences |
| Concept | What do people understand, expect, value or reject? | Avoid leading explanation; sample real segments and alternatives |
| Field | What happens in the real decision/use context? | Establish baseline, exposure, ethics and outcome measures |
| Qualitative | Why and under what conditions do interpretations form? | Seek counterexamples and document researcher influence |
| Quantitative | How common, different or stable are the patterns? | Require valid measures, sampling and uncertainty |
| Experiment | What changes because of the concept or experience? | Predefine guardrails, power, attribution and external-validity limits |

## 4. Evaluate the concept

| Criterion | Decision test |
| --- | --- |
| Distinctiveness | Noticeable and correctly attributable among real alternatives |
| Relevance | Fits audience job, situation and category expectation |
| Memorability | Retrieved at relevant category entry points |
| Extendibility | Survives formats, languages, accessibility and likely evolution |
| Depth | Supports legitimate levels of engagement without confusion |
| Credibility | Claims, behavior and delivery support expectations |
| Accessibility | Can be perceived, understood and used |
| Integrity | Respects rights, autonomy, privacy and affected stakeholders |
| Viability | Can be delivered and maintained at acceptable economics/capacity |

Set thresholds and tradeoffs for the actual job and risk. Do not sum the criteria into a universal brand score.

## 5. Decide and cultivate

Record preserve, revise, pilot, scale or stop; name owners, evidence thresholds and review dates. Define protected and adaptable elements, train contributors through examples and real decisions, listen to customers/workers/partners, monitor recognition and delivery, correct failures and preserve institutional memory without fossilizing founder taste.

Radical novelty is optional. Distinct category membership and recognizable assets may be more valuable than difference in some contexts. Fear inside the team is not evidence of innovation.
