# Brand-strategy diagnostic

## 1. Contract the decision

Define the business decision, growth problem, scope, market/geography, time horizon, stakeholders, evidence access, and implementation authority.

## 2. Diagnose the market system

- Category/subcategory definition and demand dynamics.
- Customer situations, alternatives, criteria, barriers, and language.
- Competitor positions, distinctive assets, availability, capabilities, and likely moves.
- Cultural, regulatory, technological, channel, and economic changes.
- Business economics, product truth, delivery capability, and constraints.

## 3. Audit the brand

- Current image versus intended identity.
- Mental/physical availability and distinctive assets.
- Equity, experience, proof, trust, and negative associations.
- Portfolio roles, overlaps, contradictions, and investment.
- Internal culture/behavior alignment and delivery gaps.

## 4. State the challenge

Write a precise diagnosis separating symptoms, causes, assumptions, unknowns, and decisive constraints. Name what evidence would change it.

## 5. Choose the strategy

Select a coherent approach to audience/category, position, value proposition, availability, experience, identity, assets, architecture, narrative, and growth. State trade-offs and non-priorities.

When audience comprehension, retrieval, attribution or comparative meaning is a diagnosed constraint, use the [evidence-led positioning framework](positioning-design.md). A positioning line cannot substitute for product value, proof, access or delivery.

When the existing problem–solution frame itself blocks comprehension, adoption or value creation, assess the [category-design framework](category-design.md). Keep established-category improvement and focused positioning as live alternatives; a new label is not category evidence.

Before prescribing any of these, use the [relevance diagnostic](relevance-diagnostic.md) to separate category selection, brand retrieval, qualification, conditional preference, action availability, delivery and economics.

## 6. Operationalize

Translate the strategy into product/service behavior, customer experience, innovation, sales, distribution, content, identity, culture, governance, budget, and sequencing.

Use the [brand identity system](brand-identity-system.md) to separate observed image, committed identity and active position; define boundaries; and map intended meaning to operating behavior and evidence.

Before committing to a polished expression, use [brand-concept validation](brand-concept-validation.md) to establish cross-functional decision rights, prototype the promise and experience, run swap/hand/concept/field tests and evaluate distinction, relevance, memory, extension, depth, credibility, accessibility, integrity and viability. Novelty is optional; fear or executive excitement is not validation.

## 7. Measure and learn

Set brand, behavioral, commercial, and implementation measures with baselines, owners, review cadence, and decision thresholds.

Use the [brand-growth diagnostic](brand-growth-diagnostic.md) when deciding among qualified penetration, mental availability, action availability, distinctive assets, retention or delivery interventions. Apply consumer-market laws only after checking transfer conditions.

## Quality test

A brand strategy is incomplete if it is only a logo, platform, purpose statement, campaign, persona, workshop output, or positioning sentence without business choices and coherent implementation.
