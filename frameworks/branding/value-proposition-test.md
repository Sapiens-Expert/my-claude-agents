# Value-proposition test

## 1. Define the customer situation

Specify the audience, triggering situation, desired progress, current alternative, decision participants, constraints, and consequences of action and inaction. Separate observed evidence from assumptions.

Create distinct profiles where user, buyer, budget holder, approver, administrator, beneficiary, referrer or affected nonuser has materially different jobs, risks or power. For every job, pain or gain retain exact source/date, actor/context/sample, interpretation, current behavior/alternative, importance and uncertainty. Never infer a sensitive emotional job merely because it would support the offer.

## 2. Map value

Describe the relevant jobs, pains, gains, risks, effort, and decision criteria. Map the offer's mechanism, scope, pain relievers, gain creators, price, proof, limitations, and non-fit cases. Do not force every customer observation into the offer.

## 3. Frame the market honestly

State the category customers use now and any proposed reframing. Explain why the distinction improves choice. Do not claim a new category, leadership, uniqueness, or inevitability without market evidence.

## 4. Write the hypothesis

For `[audience]` in `[situation]`, `[offer]` helps achieve `[specific progress]` through `[credible mechanism]`, compared with `[actual alternative]`, supported by `[proof]`, within `[scope and limitations]`.

## 5. Test risky assumptions

Test problem importance, comprehension, differentiation, trust, willingness to commit, delivery, retained value, economics, and safety. Prefer observed behavior and meaningful commitment over stated enthusiasm.

Assess five separate gates:

1. **Problem–solution fit:** evidenced important jobs/pains/gains plus a plausible response.
2. **Product–market fit:** behavioral evidence of delivered and retained value in the defined context.
3. **Business-model fit:** repeatable acquisition, delivery, revenue/cost and capacity evidence.
4. **Responsible-delivery fit:** safety, legality, privacy, accessibility, competence and referral.
5. **Stakeholder/ecosystem fit:** effects on workers, partners, affected nonusers, community and externalities.

No gate has a universal threshold. Define it for the proposition, segment, context, time horizon and risk.

## 6. Decide

Adopt, revise, narrow, reposition, or stop using predefined evidence thresholds. Record segment differences, harms, complaints, non-fit, and what would overturn the decision.

## Quality test

A strong value proposition is relevant, comprehensible, differentiated, credible, deliverable, ethically presented, and supported by customer behavior. A canvas or compelling sentence alone is an artifact, not evidence of fit.
