# Meaning-pattern creative lens

Use these patterns only to generate and critique symbolic or narrative possibilities. They are culturally contingent interpretive hypotheses—not personality types, audience segments, clinical constructs or universal psychology.

## Pattern map

| Pattern | Constructive promise | Shadow/pre-mortem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Innocent | Simplicity, safety, renewal | Denial, nostalgia, purity claims |
| Explorer | Freedom, discovery, self-direction | Alienation, irresponsibility, restless consumption |
| Sage | Knowledge, truth, judgment | Superiority, detachment, analysis paralysis |
| Hero | Competence, courage, protection | Overwork, enemy creation, shame, domination |
| Outlaw | Liberation from harmful norms | Destruction, illegality, nihilism, glamorized harm |
| Magician | Integration, possibility, transformation | Pseudoscience, miracle promises, manipulation |
| Regular Person | Inclusion, equality, solidarity | Conformity, anti-expertise, exclusion of difference |
| Lover | Intimacy, beauty, devotion | Objectification, dependency, boundary erosion |
| Jester | Play, pleasure, perspective | Trivialization, cruelty, irresponsibility |
| Caregiver | Care, service, protection | Paternalism, martyrdom, dependency, burnout |
| Creator | Expression, imagination, craft | Perfectionism, impracticality, self-indulgence |
| Ruler | Order, stewardship, quality | Authoritarianism, elitism, status anxiety, rigidity |

People, groups and brands may express several motives across contexts. Never infer a pattern from age, gender, ethnicity, role, diagnosis, consumption or platform behavior.

## Meaning hypothesis

For each candidate record:

1. **Audience situation:** observed job, tension, hope or concern.
2. **Functional truth:** what the offer demonstrably enables or protects.
3. **Meaning hypothesis:** constructive promise that may help interpretation.
4. **Agency and role:** who acts; whether the brand is tool, guide, setting, community or service.
5. **Evidence:** interviews, observation, language, behavior and counterexamples.
6. **Expression:** voice, imagery, pace, environments, rituals, product/service behavior.
7. **Organizational proof:** culture, incentives, policies, capabilities and outcomes.
8. **Shadow:** pattern-specific harm, cliché or manipulation risk.
9. **Cultural/access review:** symbolism, history, identity, appropriation and accessibility.
10. **Validation:** evidence that would support, complicate or reject the hypothesis.

## Congruence audit

Check strategy, offering, culture, power, people, operations, communication and stakeholder outcomes. External symbolism cannot repair an inferior, unsafe or contradictory offering. Ensure affected people can contest the story without being cast as disloyal or “off-brand.”

## Ethical gate

Reject or revise when the story:

- exploits trauma, illness, loneliness, insecurity, status or spiritual yearning;
- casts people as helpless, impure, broken, dangerous or inferior;
- stereotypes, tokenizes or appropriates cultural material;
- glamorizes overwork, illegality, domination, dependency or miracle transformation;
- conceals labor, environmental, clinical or social harm;
- borrows the meaning of a cause without material contribution and accountability;
- implies psychological, spiritual or clinical authority the brand does not possess.

In executive healthspan and psychedelic contexts, never use Magician, Hero or Caregiver symbolism to imply healing, rescue, exceptional invulnerability or clinical authority.

## Research and decision

Use qualitative inquiry to generate hypotheses and quantitative/behavioral evidence to examine prevalence, difference or consequences where needed. Test comprehension, agency, category fit, correct attribution, cultural effects and harm. Record use, revision or rejection; do not lock a permanent “core archetype.”
