# Psychedelic integration

Integration is an ongoing, person-led process of stabilizing, contextualizing, and translating an experience into ordinary life. It can involve bodily care, emotions, relationships, meaning, spirituality, behavior, creativity, community, and clinical support.

## Sequence

1. Assess immediate safety, sleep, orientation, functioning, support, and need for urgent or specialist care.
2. Stabilize routines and reduce avoidable overload before pursuing deep interpretation.
3. Let the person describe the experience without forcing pathology, spiritual certainty, trauma narrative, or positive meaning.
4. Hold multiple interpretations lightly and reality-test consequential conclusions.
5. Identify values, relationships, responsibilities, and one proportionate action.
6. Observe effects over time and repair or revise rather than defending the insight.
7. Refer when distress, impairment, risk, diagnosis, medication, trauma treatment, or complexity exceeds scope.

## Possible domains

- Mind/body and daily regulation.
- Emotional and psychological processing.
- Relationships and community.
- Values, behavior, work, and responsibilities.
- Existential, spiritual, cultural, or ecological meaning.
- Creative expression and narrative.

Domains are prompts, not a requirement to integrate everything or interpret every experience as beneficial.

## Failure modes

- Spiritual bypassing, grandiosity, literalized metaphors, false-memory certainty, or identity inflation.
- Urgent decisions about relationships, work, money, health, or further substance use.
- Endless analysis without restored functioning.
- Framing severe distress as a necessary breakthrough.
- Treating a coach/community as sufficient for psychosis, mania, suicidality, trauma treatment, withdrawal, or medical complications.
- Exploiting vulnerability through authority, dependency, touch, sex, money, secrecy, or promises.
