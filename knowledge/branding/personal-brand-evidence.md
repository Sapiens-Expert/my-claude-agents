# Personal-brand evidence and reputation

A personal brand is not a persona, profile or promise of total control over reputation. It is the interaction between truthful identity, demonstrated capability, bounded authority, strategic position, recognizable expression and what relevant people actually perceive through conduct and evidence.

## Six-layer model

| Layer | Governing question | Acceptable evidence | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Who is this person professionally across contexts? | Biography, roles, values, language, geography and boundaries | Invented or fragmented persona |
| Capability | What can they currently do? | Work, methods, education, practice and dated outcomes | Skills asserted without level, recency or proof |
| Authority | What can they credibly teach, advise on or decide? | Verified credentials, publications, results, peer/client evidence and labeled lived experience | Authority transferred across unrelated domains |
| Position | What useful association should be foregrounded for this audience now? | Audience problem, category, alternative, value and relevant proof | A slogan substitutes for strategic choice |
| Expression | What makes the identity recognizable and understandable? | Voice, visuals, stories, artifacts, behavior and consistent attribution | Distinctiveness without truth or relevance |
| Reputation | What do relevant people actually notice, believe and report? | Audience research, referrals, direct retrieval, independent mentions and observed behavior | Self-presentation mistaken for external perception |

The first five layers can be governed. Reputation can be influenced but only observed externally. Profile completeness, views, followers, endorsements and platform scores do not establish competence or reputation.

## Live, prove, then describe

1. Define the useful association and its strategic purpose.
2. Inventory real work, qualifications, conduct, results and limitations.
3. Gather external language about strengths, risks and distinctive contribution.
4. Select a small core identity, relevant proof and explicit nonclaims.
5. Express it consistently across profile, portfolio, content and interaction.
6. Measure correct recognition, trust, opportunity quality, misunderstanding and harm.
7. Revise when evidence, role or audience changes—not merely when a platform changes its fashion.

## Evidence rules

- Every public association needs proof, scope, provenance, review date and a nonclaim.
- Education and certification establish completed learning, not automatic competence.
- Recommendations need consent, relationship context and specific scope; reciprocal endorsement is weak evidence.
- Client/employer achievements require correct attribution and confidentiality clearance.
- Personal experience must be labeled and cannot establish general efficacy.
- Professional identity remains portable beyond any employer, platform or project.
- Vulnerability and personal disclosure are optional, never a requirement for authenticity.

## Personal–organization boundary

Personal and organizational brands may reinforce one another without becoming interchangeable. State the affiliation and source of each claim; preserve the individual’s voice; use organizational facts and assets correctly; make advocacy voluntary; preclear only material risk; define ownership of work and content; and plan for role change or exit.

For VladOS, Vlad may selectively endorse O!Sapiens and Sapiens OS. Entheogen Expert requires explicit domain separation: commercial strategy, coaching experience or personal psychedelic history does not create clinical authority.
