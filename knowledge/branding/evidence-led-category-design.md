# Evidence-led category design

Category design is the coordinated attempt to define or reshape a problem–solution frame, align an organization and ecosystem around it, and help an audience understand when and why the frame matters. It is justified only when a strategy diagnosis shows that the existing category structure materially obstructs comprehension, adoption or value creation.

Category design does not mean inventing vocabulary around an ordinary offer. A legitimate category corresponds to a real, consequential problem; a meaningfully different solution logic or decision criterion; credible delivery; sufficient demand and economics; and a frame that improves buyer choice beyond the sponsoring company.

## Place in the strategy stack

- **Coherent strategy** determines the pivotal challenge and guiding policy.
- **Value innovation** tests whether buyer utility and cost structure are substantively redesigned.
- **Category design** may define the problem and solution class through which that value becomes understandable.
- **Positioning** determines the credible association the brand can earn within or relative to that frame.
- **Brand growth** builds memory, access, assets, experience and reach over time.

Improving an established category, occupying a focused position or doing nothing must remain live alternatives. A new category is a strategic hypothesis, not evidence of superiority.

## Category legitimacy

Test a candidate category on six dimensions:

1. **Problem reality:** who experiences the problem, in which situations, with what frequency, severity and consequences?
2. **Behavioral distinction:** does the category change how people evaluate, buy, use or organize solutions?
3. **Substantive value:** does the solution create meaningful utility rather than semantic novelty?
4. **Comprehension and usefulness:** does the frame reduce confusion and enable better decisions?
5. **Viability:** are demand, delivery capability, economics, regulation and ecosystem participation plausible?
6. **Integrity:** are problem, urgency, evidence, scope, risks and alternatives represented truthfully?

An apparently empty category may be empty because demand is weak. Validate the problem and behavior before investing in ownership language.

## From/to transition

A from/to map specifies the change in behavior, belief, workflow or decision criteria the category proposes. Describe the current state fairly, including where it remains appropriate. State who benefits, what must change, costs and barriers, and evidence for the transition. Avoid false binaries, caricatured incumbents and invented enemies.

## Point of view

A point of view is a strategic narrative linking:

- the observed problem and stakes;
- why current approaches are insufficient in defined situations;
- the insight enabling a better future;
- the proposed category and its boundaries;
- the solution mechanism and evidence;
- non-fit, limitations and action.

It should guide product, capability, investment, sales and partnership choices—not merely generate campaign copy. Emotional force cannot exceed evidential support.

## Operating and ecosystem alignment

The category promise must be reflected in the product roadmap, offer taxonomy, use cases, business model, capabilities, proof, vocabulary, sales, service and incentives. Map customers, substitutes, partners, suppliers, practitioners, regulators, media and affected communities. Record incentives, dependencies, conflicts, excluded groups, interoperability and governance.

A useful ecosystem creates reciprocal value. Dependence engineered solely around one vendor or personality is not proof of category health.

## Concentrated activation

A coordinated public milestone can focus resources and learning, but attention is only an intermediate observation. Replace irreversible “burn the boats” logic with a bounded activation: clear audience and causal hypothesis, readiness gates, budget, owners, pre-mortem, rollback conditions and follow-through. Measure the chain from comprehension and problem recognition through qualified demand, adoption, retention, outcomes and reputation.

## Category development and governance

Track category-language adoption beyond prompted exposure, correct problem recognition, association and attribution, number and quality of participating actors, buyer behavior, retained value, delivery outcomes, concentration and stakeholder effects. Separate category growth from the focal company's share. Preserve dissent and update or abandon the frame when evidence contradicts it.

Market leadership may create network, ecosystem, capital or talent feedback loops in some markets. Specify the actual mechanism and monitor saturation, pricing power, access, competition and public harm. “Category king” is not a self-awarded credential.

## Do not infer

- Category leaders normally capture a fixed majority of market value.
- Creating a name creates demand, market ownership or defensibility.
- Different is categorically better than a superior established-category offer.
- Customers are irrelevant to category discovery.
- A high-attention launch establishes adoption.
- One company controls how a category evolves.
- Dominance is inherently beneficial to buyers or society.
