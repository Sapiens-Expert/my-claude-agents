# Brand architecture

Brand architecture assigns roles, ranges and relationships so audiences understand offerings and the organization allocates equity, authority and investment deliberately. The portfolio—not an individual name—is the unit of decision.

The aim is the smallest coherent set of brand assets needed to enable business strategy while preserving relevance, clarity, cumulative memory, appropriate separation and future flexibility.

## Common relationship options

- Branded house: one master brand is the primary driver across offers.
- Sub-brand: a named offer modifies the master while drawing substantial credibility from it.
- Endorsed brand: the offer has its own identity with visible reassurance from another brand.
- House of brands: separate brands lead in separate markets with limited visible connection.
- Hybrid: different relationships coexist for explicit strategic reasons.

## Decision criteria

- Audience/category overlap and different buying contexts.
- Desired associations and risk of contradiction.
- Existing equity and credibility transfer.
- Need for focus, autonomy, differentiation, or reputational separation.
- Cross-selling potential and customer navigation.
- Legal/ownership constraints and future divestment/partnership options.
- Cost of building and maintaining distinct identities.
- Channel, geography, language, and organizational accountability.

## Required roles

For every brand and offer, declare target, frame, promise, proof, personality/relationship, driver role, endorser role, strategic importance, investment level, and migration rules.

### Product-defining roles

For each offer, record one customer-facing stack:

1. **Master brand:** the primary reference and usually the main driver of choice.
2. **Subbrand or product brand:** modifies the master only when a distinct promise or navigation need justifies it.
3. **Descriptor:** plainly states what the offer is; prefer this over a decorative new brand.
4. **Endorser or corporate brand:** lends explicit credibility and accountability.
5. **Branded differentiator:** names a meaningful feature, method or service only when value and ownership are evidenced.
6. **Alliance or co-brand:** introduces external equity or capability under governed terms.

If a name does not improve recognition, qualification, navigation, proof, accountability or strategic control, it is probably unnecessary.

### Portfolio roles

| Role | Resource logic | Required evidence | Review trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current power | Sustains material current demand and contribution | Penetration/access, equity, economics and durability | Relevance or contribution structurally declines |
| Future power | Plausible future scale or strategic value | Validated demand, milestones, capability and economics | Learning/adoption thresholds are missed |
| Linchpin | Controls an important relationship or capability indirectly | Counterfactual portfolio dependency | Dependency changes or simpler option appears |
| Silver bullet | Improves another brand's image or credibility | Measured transfer and attribution | Weak transfer or harmful spillover |
| Flanker | Protects a strategic position or tier | Incremental defense net of cannibalization and complexity | Defense is unnecessary or uneconomic |
| Energizer | Adds relevant attention or associations | Incremental reach, fit, transfer and durability | Novelty decay or weak attribution |
| Cash generator | Funds priorities with limited growth investment | Contribution and minimum viable customer/equity support | Harm, accelerated decline or better exit path |

Roles are time- and market-specific hypotheses, never permanent honors.

## Portfolio objectives

Evaluate the net system on:

- **relevance:** qualification for meaningful buying situations;
- **differentiation:** verified value or capability that changes choice;
- **energy:** incremental correct attention and memory, not novelty alone;
- **leverage:** bidirectional value transfer net of cost and risk;
- **clarity:** audience comprehension, navigation, ownership and attribution;
- **synergy:** shared reach, assets, proof and economics net of fragmentation, cannibalization and complexity.

## Scope and authority transfer

Every brand needs a legitimate range across offer, audience, category, price, channel, geography and evidence domain. Model associations and claims moving in both directions. A personal, corporate or partner endorsement must not transfer clinical, scientific, psychedelic, tax or health authority without domain-specific evidence.

## Brand addition gate

A new brand, subbrand, program or differentiator name is justified only when it:

- represents a durable audience promise, category, capability, evidence domain or necessary risk boundary;
- improves correct recognition, navigation or accountability beyond a descriptor;
- can be funded through establishment and maintenance;
- avoids misleading authority transfer and excessive fragmentation;
- has an owner, role, scope, measures and sunset trigger.

Novelty, executive preference, domain availability or a new offer alone are insufficient.

## Extensions and alliances

For an extension, ask whether the existing brand helps the extension, whether the extension helps or harms the existing brand, whether a new brand is necessary, and whether the system can support it. Include fit, retrieval, new category entry points, channel access, cannibalization, investment, quality, price/service economics and future options.

For an alliance, define each party's role and prominence, reciprocal customer value, association transfer, claims, quality, IP, data, approvals, incidents, termination and post-exit use. External brands are governed dependencies, not decorative borrowed credibility.

## Consolidation and retirement

Weak equity, duplicate roles, confusion, underinvestment, poor fit, risk or excessive maintenance can justify maintaining, investing, merging, migrating, demoting to descriptor, pausing, licensing, selling or retiring an asset. Retirement requires customer/search migration, contract and archive preservation, communication, redirects, impact monitoring and an appropriate reversibility window.

Every new brand receives sunset criteria when created.

## Failure modes

- New names compensate for weak strategy or internal politics.
- One brand stretches across contradictory promises or unsafe audiences.
- Multiple brands duplicate investment without distinct roles.
- Endorsement is assumed to transfer credibility without testing.
- The portfolio is documented from the organization chart rather than customer decisions.
- Branded features and programs proliferate because attention is mistaken for durable value.
- Consolidation deletes useful equity, records or customer access without managed migration.
