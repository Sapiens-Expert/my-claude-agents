# Coherent strategy and strategic learning

## Core proposition

A strategy is a testable theory of how coordinated choices and actions will overcome a consequential challenge. It is neither aspiration nor a list of initiatives.

The minimum structure is:

`evidence → diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent actions → observations → revision`

This structure is adapted from Richard Rumelt's strategy kernel and strengthened through comparison with value innovation, positioning, category design, brand growth, brand architecture, product discovery and execution sources.

## 1. Evidence frame

Before diagnosing, define:

- the decision, scope and time horizon;
- stakeholders and whose outcome counts;
- customer situations, alternatives and behavior;
- capabilities, economics and constraints;
- competitors and likely responses;
- environmental, regulatory and technological change;
- evidence quality, missing data and uncertainty.

Evidence is not a ceremonial preface. It constrains the diagnosis and determines what can be claimed.

## 2. Diagnosis

A diagnosis selects the critical structure of the situation. It should explain why the desired outcome is difficult and distinguish symptoms from plausible drivers.

A useful diagnosis:

- names one consequential challenge or a tightly connected challenge system;
- identifies the bottleneck, conflict, asymmetry or change that matters most;
- states causal logic in plain language;
- distinguishes facts, interpretations and assumptions;
- identifies competing diagnoses;
- names evidence that would weaken or overturn it;
- accounts for stakeholder and system effects.

The diagnosis is not required to be certain. It is required to be explicit enough to test.

## 3. Guiding policy

The guiding policy defines the overall method for addressing the diagnosed challenge. It channels decisions without pretending to specify every task.

It should state:

- where effort will concentrate;
- the source of leverage or value;
- the audience, market, system or capability in scope;
- decision rules and boundaries;
- important exclusions and trade-offs;
- how likely competitive or environmental responses will be handled;
- what must remain adaptable.

Positioning, category framing, value innovation and brand choices live here only when they address the diagnosis. They are not substitutes for one.

## 4. Coherent action system

Actions are coherent when they implement the policy and reinforce rather than undermine one another. Specify:

- pivotal and proximate objectives;
- actions started, stopped and sequenced;
- resource concentration and opportunity cost;
- capabilities to build, buy, partner or remove;
- dependencies and weakest links;
- owners, authority and timing;
- leading observations, outcome measures and guardrails;
- review triggers, repair options and exit conditions.

Activity volume is not coherence. A shorter action set with clear reinforcement is usually more strategic than a comprehensive wish list.

## Strategic power lenses

Use these as hypotheses, not mandatory ingredients:

| Lens | Question | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leverage | Where can focused effort create disproportionate change? | Wrong pivot or insufficient force |
| Proximate objective | What high-value obstacle is feasible to resolve next? | Symbolic or remote target |
| Chain-link system | Is performance capped by a binding weak link? | Improving a nonbinding component |
| Design/fit | Which activities become stronger together? | Brittle overfit |
| Focus | Where does capability create disproportionate value? | Segment too narrow or changing |
| Advantage | What valuable asymmetry can be built or renewed? | No customer value or easy substitution |
| Dynamics | What structural change and second-order effects matter? | Trend extrapolation |
| Anti-inertia | What routines or dependencies block adaptation? | Erasing legitimate constraints |
| Anti-entropy | What proliferation is dissolving coherence? | Centralization that suppresses learning |

## Market and brand corrections

Rumelt's kernel governs strategic logic, but complementary sources correct blind spots:

- **Blue Ocean Strategy:** test whether buyer value and cost structure can be changed together; a strategy must create value, not merely exploit an opponent.
- **Positioning/category design:** diagnose the audience's existing mental frame and alternatives; strategic coherence does not guarantee comprehensibility or adoption.
- **How Brands Grow:** test claims about loyalty, differentiation and segmentation against behavioral market evidence; do not infer growth from compelling narrative alone.
- **Aaker's brand work:** connect strategic choice to identity, portfolio roles, distinctive assets and organizational behavior.
- **Product discovery:** resolve critical assumptions with the smallest responsible test before irreversible scaling.

## Learning discipline

Treat strategy as a hypothesis:

1. write the predicted causal chain;
2. list necessary assumptions and base rates;
3. construct the strongest competing diagnosis;
4. run a create–destroy review or premortem;
5. select the smallest decision that reduces consequential uncertainty;
6. predefine observations and decision thresholds;
7. compare results with prediction;
8. update the diagnosis before adding activity;
9. preserve superseded logic in the decision log.

Strategic experiments may be slow, confounded, irreversible or harmful. Use pilots only when risk, consent, reversibility and stakeholder effects are acceptable.

## Strategy quality test

A strategy is not ready if any answer is missing:

1. What is the consequential challenge?
2. What evidence supports that diagnosis?
3. What alternative explanation might be true?
4. What guiding approach addresses the cause rather than the symptom?
5. What will receive less attention or stop?
6. Why should the actions reinforce one another?
7. Which assumption could invalidate the strategy?
8. What observation will cause revision?
9. Who bears costs or risks?
10. Who owns implementation and review?

## Do not infer

- A crisp kernel does not guarantee a correct diagnosis.
- Focus is not automatically superior to a portfolio approach.
- Competitive advantage is not the only legitimate objective.
- Military analogy does not justify zero-sum or deceptive behavior.
- Historical success cases do not isolate causal mechanisms.
- Contrarian judgment is not insight without evidence.
- Coherence must not suppress dissent, resilience or ethical constraints.
