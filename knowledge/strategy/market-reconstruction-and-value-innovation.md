# Market reconstruction and value innovation

Market reconstruction is an optional strategic response to convergence: competitors invest in similar factors, buyers see declining meaningful difference, or category conventions exclude substantial demand. It is not a default definition of strategy. A prior diagnosis must show why redesigning the value curve is more promising than improving execution, focusing, building capability, competing directly or exiting.

## Value innovation

Value innovation seeks a material increase in buyer utility while changing the cost structure. Differentiation and lower cost are therefore design constraints, not slogans. A proposal qualifies only when it specifies what will be eliminated and reduced—not merely what will be added—and validates the resulting utility, willingness to pay or access, unit economics and delivery quality.

## Strategy canvas

A strategy canvas compares the factors on which alternatives invest and the level offered on each factor. Build it from observed buyer behavior and alternative use, not internal feature labels.

1. Define the decision, buyer/user, use situation and relevant alternatives, including substitutes and non-consumption.
2. Express competitive factors in buyer language.
3. Separate measured levels, researched perceptions and managerial estimates.
4. Plot the current offer and alternatives; do not manufacture precision from ordinal judgments.
5. Draft a proposed curve with focus, divergence and a clear, truthful tagline.

The canvas is a diagnostic and communication model. It does not prove demand, causality or defensibility.

## Four actions and ERRC

Ask which category factors should be eliminated, reduced below convention, raised above convention and created. Record the evidence, buyer effect, cost effect, stakeholder effect and dependency for every choice. The eliminate–reduce–raise–create grid is valuable because it exposes trade-offs and prevents innovation from becoming feature accumulation.

## Six paths for boundary exploration

Explore alternatives across industries; strategic groups; buyer, user and influencer roles; complementary products and services; functional versus emotional orientation; and changes over time. These are search directions, not proof that an opportunity exists. Each resulting idea remains a hypothesis.

## Noncustomers and demand barriers

Research three practical distances from the current market: people about to leave or using reluctantly, people who consciously reject the category, and people who have never considered it. Seek common barriers without erasing important differences among segments. Non-use caused by affordability, accessibility, safety, culture, privacy or principled refusal must not be reinterpreted automatically as latent demand.

## Five decision gates

1. **Utility:** Does the design remove a consequential barrier across discovery, purchase, delivery, use, maintenance, complements or disposal?
2. **Access and price:** Can the intended population access it, and is the price credible relative to alternatives and willingness to pay?
3. **Cost and quality:** Can it be delivered sustainably at the target economics without shifting costs into unsafe quality, labor, privacy or environmental harm?
4. **Adoption:** What barriers face employees, partners, regulators, communities and users? Are explanation, participation, remedy and capability adequate?
5. **Public and ethical value:** Who benefits, who bears risk, and are truthfulness, autonomy, inclusion, health, data protection and externalities governed?

These extend the book's value, profit and people propositions with an explicit public/ethical proposition.

## Test and renewal

Precommit the critical assumptions, leading observations, guardrails and failure thresholds. Test the riskiest links with noncustomer interviews, alternative observation, price/access evidence, cost models and bounded pilots. Review the value curve as alternatives imitate, customer priorities change or costs drift. “Uncontested” space is temporary and should never suspend competitive or environmental monitoring.

## Do not infer

- A visually divergent curve establishes demand or profitable growth.
- Category creation is superior to competition in every diagnosis.
- Famous cases establish typical effect sizes or durable causal advantage.
- Noncustomers are homogeneous or should all be converted.
- Lower cost is beneficial when burdens are transferred to workers, partners, users or the public.
