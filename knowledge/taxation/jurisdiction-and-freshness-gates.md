# Tax jurisdiction and freshness gates

## Minimum fact pattern

Do not analyze an operational tax question without identifying:

- person or entity and legal form;
- citizenship only where legally relevant;
- residence position and potentially competing residences;
- relevant tax year and transaction dates;
- jurisdictions and applicable treaty;
- income, asset, service, ownership, control, and permanent-establishment facts;
- family, employment, director, shareholder, beneficial-owner, and related-party facts where relevant;
- elections, registrations, filings, notices, and deadlines already made;
- whether the question is planning, reporting, controversy, or remediation.

## Verification gates

- Confirm the rule as of the relevant event and filing date, not only today's rule.
- Check primary national sources in the authoritative language; translations and manuals may be informational.
- Confirm treaty entry into force, covered taxes, protocol changes, and multilateral-instrument effects.
- Distinguish OECD/UN models and guidance from enacted domestic law.
- Confirm EU measures and Pillar Two implementation country by country.
- Treat rates, thresholds, forms, deadlines, exemptions, special regimes, blacklists, and administrative procedures as volatile.
- Obtain written advice from an appropriately qualified adviser for material decisions, cross-border structures, eligibility, filings, disputes, or penalty exposure.

## Red flags for immediate referral

Competing residence claims; missed filings; audits or notices; uncertain permanent establishment; controlled-company or beneficial-ownership issues; related-party pricing; crypto or opaque asset history; inheritance/gifts; trusts; exit tax; sanctions; aggressive avoidance; potential evasion; or any request to conceal facts or fabricate substance.

Never recommend backdating, sham residence, nominee arrangements, artificial substance, undeclared accounts/income, document destruction, or misleading a tax authority.
