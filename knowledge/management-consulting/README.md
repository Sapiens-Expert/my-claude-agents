# Management consulting

## Purpose

This node synthesizes the Management Consultant library into a practical consulting capability. The books provide methods and vocabulary; owning them does not prove that a method has been applied successfully.

## Consulting system

1. **Contract the problem.** Identify the economic buyer, desired business outcomes, success measures, value, constraints, and decision rights.
2. **Structure the diagnosis.** Separate symptoms from the central challenge; form testable hypotheses; decompose the problem without material overlap or omission; prioritize key drivers.
3. **Choose a strategic response.** Define a guiding policy and a small set of coherent actions. Make explicit what will not be pursued.
4. **Design implementation.** Translate the response into owners, operating cadence, leading indicators, milestones, dependencies, and feedback loops.
5. **Build adoption.** Involve affected people, design the choice environment, communicate trade-offs, and combine care with direct challenge.
6. **Measure and adapt.** Track outcomes rather than activity alone; review evidence; stop, revise, or scale deliberately.
7. **Transfer capability.** Leave the client with decisions, artifacts, operating routines, and internal ownership rather than consultant dependence.

## Diagnostic lenses

| Lens | Core question | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategy | What is the decisive challenge, and where must resources concentrate? | Market structure, customer behavior, capabilities, economics |
| Customer/value | What creates differentiated value, and what can be eliminated, reduced, raised, or created? | Interviews, alternatives, willingness to pay, journey data |
| Operating system | Where do ownership, process, cadence, information, or automation break down? | Workflow, handoffs, cycle time, defects, rework |
| People/culture | Which environment, incentives, management behavior, or role fit shapes performance? | Employee research, selection data, manager practice, retention |
| Execution | Are objectives measurable, owned, time-bounded, and reviewed? | OKRs, milestones, leading indicators, decision log |
| Commercial model | Are scope, value, fees, acquisition, delivery, and retention mutually viable? | Unit economics, pipeline, capacity, outcomes, renewal |

## Guardrails

- Do not substitute a fashionable framework for primary client evidence.
- Do not present MECE, OKRs, Blue Ocean, mindset, culture, or nudges as universal solutions.
- Separate descriptive case studies from causal evidence.
- Behavioral design must preserve autonomy, transparency, and an easy way to opt out.
- Value-based pricing requires an agreed outcome/value logic; it does not justify fabricated ROI.
- Culture and performance recommendations must account for jurisdiction, accessibility, power, and psychological safety.
