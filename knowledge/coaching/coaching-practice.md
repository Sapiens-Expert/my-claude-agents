# Coaching practice

## Working definition

Coaching is a contracted, collaborative process that helps a client clarify desired change, examine patterns and context, generate choices, act, and learn. It is not diagnosis, psychotherapy, medical care, consulting disguised as questioning, or responsibility transfer from client to coach.

## Relationship foundation

- Establish purpose, scope, roles, confidentiality/limits, communication, challenge preferences, data use, fees, and exit/referral conditions.
- Treat the client as capable of agency while recognizing real constraints, vulnerability, power differences, and occasions when expertise or referral is needed.
- Build a designed alliance that can be revisited when trust, focus, expectations, or working style change.
- Listen for content, emotion, values, ambivalence, context, discrepancies, and what is not yet clear; do not claim intuition as fact.
- Use accurate empathy and curiosity without manufacturing intimacy or positioning the coach as uniquely insightful.

## Conversation architecture

1. **Engage:** establish working connection and understand the client's perspective.
2. **Focus:** agree which change or decision is being explored now.
3. **Evoke:** draw out the client's own meaning, reasons, need, ability, and ambivalence.
4. **Explore:** identify desired future, existing resources, exceptions, constraints, patterns, and options.
5. **Choose:** decide whether to act, gather evidence, accept a tradeoff, or pause.
6. **Plan:** define a specific next experiment, context/cue, support, measure, and review.
7. **Learn:** review what happened without moralizing; adapt environment, strategy, or goal.

This is a flexible map, not a mandatory script.

## Useful practitioner lenses

- Co-Active: designed alliance, whole-person attention, fulfillment, balance, process, listening, curiosity, and self-management. Treat “naturally creative, resourceful, and whole” as a stance, not a reason to ignore illness, oppression, missing skills, or referral needs.
- Solution-focused: preferred future, exceptions, resources, signs of progress, scaling, and small next steps. Do not use solution focus to bypass grief, conflict, risk, structural barriers, or root-cause work that matters.
- Positive provocation: questions can challenge assumptions and expand options. A clever question is not automatically useful; obtain permission, time it well, and observe impact.
- Focus design: protect attention through scheduling, environment, boundaries, recovery, and reduction of switching. Popular “deep work” prescriptions must fit role, disability, caregiving, collaboration, and workload reality.
- Growth mindset: abilities can develop through effective effort, strategy, feedback, and support. Do not reduce performance to attitude or blame people for structural constraints.

## Coach interventions

Use reflections, summaries, open questions, affirmations grounded in evidence, reframes offered tentatively, information with permission, feedback tied to observation, experiments, accountability, and referral. Advice is acceptable when contracted and clearly labeled; do not hide it inside leading questions.

## Measurement

Measure the client's stated outcome, behavior/process signals, fit and alliance, experiment completion/learning, adverse effects, scope/referral events, and sustainability. Session satisfaction and compelling insight are insufficient outcome evidence.

## Failure modes

- Righting reflex: arguing for change faster than the client does.
- Questioning without reflection, focus, or genuine listening.
- Premature action planning before engagement and motivation.
- Overinterpreting trauma, nervous-system state, personality, resistance, or unconscious motives.
- Turning coaching into performance pressure, surveillance, dependency, or guru authority.
- Applying business-scaling systems to a person's life without checking values, health, and capacity.
- Using health, longevity, or productivity material to imply treatment or guaranteed performance.
