# Discovery and qualification

## Discovery map

| Area | Questions to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Context | What changed, why now, and what system are we discussing? |
| Current state | What happens today, how often, for whom, and with what measurable effect? |
| Problem | What is not working, what causes it, and what has already been tried? |
| Consequences | What does the problem affect operationally, financially, strategically, and personally? |
| Desired state | What would be observably different, by when, and why is it valuable? |
| Gap | How large and important is the distance between current and desired state? |
| Change readiness | What makes action likely or unlikely? What happens if nothing changes? |
| Buying system | Who uses, evaluates, approves, pays, can block, and can coach the process? |
| Decision | What criteria, evidence, process, resources, risks, and timing govern the choice? |
| Fit | Can this offer responsibly produce the required outcome within scope? |

SPIN’s situation/problem/implication/need-payoff sequence and Gap Selling’s current/future-state model are question architectures, not scripts. Use only the questions needed to understand the buyer.

## Qualification test

An opportunity is qualified when there is evidence of:

- meaningful problem or opportunity;
- identifiable consequences and desired outcome;
- plausible fit with the offer;
- access to the required stakeholders and information;
- credible decision process, timing, and resources;
- reciprocal commitments and a dated next step.

Budget or authority alone is not qualification. A friendly contact is not automatically a champion, and platform activity is not proof of pain.

## Exit conditions

Disqualify or nurture when the problem is immaterial, the offer is unsafe or mismatched, the stakeholder system cannot be accessed, there is no buyer-owned action, or the economics/timing make responsible delivery unlikely.
