# Ethical influence and recipient protection

People often use social cues to reduce decision effort. Reciprocity, liking, social proof, authority, scarcity, commitment/consistency and unity can be genuinely informative. The same efficiency creates vulnerability when a cue is fabricated, exaggerated, selectively presented, irrelevant or combined with others to overwhelm reflection.

Increased compliance is not evidence of comprehension, informed consent, fit, benefit or ethical legitimacy.

## Recipient-centered sequence

1. **Relationship and safety:** make identity, intent, relevance and conflicts clear. Warmth, cooperation and shared purpose may arise naturally but must not be manufactured.
2. **Decision quality:** provide relevant evidence, alternatives, limitations, price, uncertainty and scope. Social proof and authority are supporting evidence, not substitutes for merits.
3. **Autonomous action:** make a proportional next step feasible. Use only genuine constraints and self-authored commitments with reflection, revision and exit.
4. **Outcome review:** measure comprehension, voluntariness, fit, delivery, benefit, adverse effects, complaints and regret—not conversion alone.

## Seven mechanisms and protection tests

| Cue | Legitimate use | Misuse | Protection test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reciprocity | Give useful value without obligation; exchange real concessions | Engineered debt, surprise ask, inflated anchor | Can the recipient keep the value and decline without guilt or penalty? |
| Liking | Be human, cooperative and genuinely appreciative | False similarity, flattery, halo or parasocial capture | Does the offer remain legible when messenger and merits are separated? |
| Social proof | Report relevant verified behavior/outcomes with context | Fake reviews, cherry-picking, popularity-as-truth | Are source, denominator, selection and comparison group clear? |
| Authority | Show relevant qualifications, sources, scope and conflicts | Prestige symbols or borrowed credibility outside expertise | Is authority current, domain-specific, verifiable and trustworthy? |
| Scarcity | Disclose genuine material capacity or deadline early | Resetting countdowns, hidden inventory, competition theater | Is the constraint real, stable and safe to deliberate under? |
| Commitment | Support private, voluntary, revisable self-chosen goals | Low-ball, sunk-cost, public identity or micro-commitment traps | Can the person revise, pause or exit without shame or disproportionate loss? |
| Unity | Enable cooperation around real shared purpose | In-group pressure, enemy creation, loyalty tests | Are dissent, privacy, plural identity and equal dignity protected? |

## Evidence requirements

The taxonomy combines evidence of varying quality and is not an exhaustive behavior model. Before consequential use, review current evidence for the specific mechanism, population, medium and outcome. Do not infer an individual's motive or susceptibility from a response.

Social proof requires provenance, denominator, selection method, outcome definition, time period and relevance. Majority behavior never establishes individual benefit. Authority must be separated across education, coaching, clinical care, science, law/tax and lived experience.

## Tactic stacking

Combining gifts, personal similarity, testimonials, credentials, countdowns, public commitments and “our tribe” language is a red flag even when each item is literally true. Evaluate cumulative cognitive load, vulnerability, action stakes and time pressure. High-stakes decisions require more deliberation, not more cues.

## Context-specific prohibitions

- No manufactured scarcity, fear or urgency in health, psychedelic, financial or emotionally dependent decisions.
- No spiritual, clinical, scientific or professional authority transfer beyond actual scope.
- No public commitment or community belonging used to inhibit reconsideration or referral.
- No fake consensus, engagement pods, undisclosed selection or automated false familiarity.
- No inference or targeting of illness, trauma, altered states, loneliness, financial distress or identity vulnerability without explicit lawful justification.

## Defensive literacy

When a cue feels salient: pause; name it; separate messenger from merits; verify evidence and alternatives; ask what changed in the underlying value; assess reversibility; and revisit prior commitments without treating consistency as a virtue in itself.

## Do not infer

- The seven principles produce uniform or guaranteed effects.
- A truthful cue is automatically ethical or relevant.
- Compliance reveals stable preference or motive.
- Popularity establishes correctness, safety or fit.
- Shared identity authorizes conformity or exclusion.
