# Product management

## Product model

A product is a repeatable way to help a defined customer make progress under real constraints. Product management connects customer evidence, value proposition, delivery capability, business model, distribution, risk, and learning.

## Core questions

1. Who has the problem, in what situation, and how costly/urgent is it?
2. What do they do now, including workarounds and non-consumption?
3. What outcome do they seek, and what blocks adoption or success?
4. Which assumptions about desirability, usability, feasibility, viability, safety, and distribution could invalidate the model?
5. What is the smallest ethical test that produces behavioral evidence?
6. Can the organization deliver value repeatedly with acceptable economics and risk?

## Evidence ladder

Weak to strong:

- opinions, trend interest, likes, and hypothetical enthusiasm;
- problem interviews and observed workarounds;
- prototype use and task completion;
- time, data, reputation, access, or money committed;
- repeated use, renewal, referral, and measurable outcome;
- repeatable acquisition, delivery, retention, and unit economics across relevant segments.

A waitlist, deck, community size, or successful launch does not by itself validate retained value.

Interpret evidence through explicit fit gates: plausible problem–solution, observed product–market value, viable business model, responsible delivery and stakeholder/ecosystem effects. These accumulate at different rates and have no universal pass threshold. A proposition cannot compensate for failed safety or legitimacy with strong conversion.

## Business-model components

Map customer segments, value propositions, channels, relationships, revenue, key resources/activities/partners, and costs as linked hypotheses. Add risk, evidence, owner, and next test; a completed canvas is not a business.

Recurring-revenue models require recurring value. Subscription or membership fit depends on an ongoing job, usage cadence, onboarding, habit/integration, service burden, churn, expansion, and trust. Community is a product capability only when members receive value from relationships and shared participation—not merely from being called a community.

## Distribution

Treat channels as experiments with audience fit, economics, speed, control, saturation, and attribution. Test several plausible routes cheaply, then concentrate on the channel that produces qualified behavior. Viral, launch, influencer, affiliate, paid, content, partnership, community, and sales-led playbooks are context-specific tools.

## Offer and persuasion boundary

An offer combines outcome, scope, delivery, proof, price, risk allocation, and fit. Improve actual value and clarity before adding urgency, scarcity, bonuses, guarantees, or status. Never fabricate constraints, exploit health fear, conceal effort/limitations, or guarantee outcomes outside control.

Repeated audience questions, objections, enquiries and delivery patterns can seed offer discovery through [demand mining](../../frameworks/marketing/demand-mining-and-offer-validation.md). Classify curiosity, informational need, contextual advice, execution need, standardized workflow and actual commitment separately. Questions are clues, not validation; choose free guidance, consultation, service, product, referral or no offer according to authority, customer benefit and evidence.

Creator monetization is a business model, not a follower milestone. Services, products, consultations, affiliates, sponsorships and collaborations require their own value, delivery, rights, disclosure, consumer-protection, concentration and economic controls. Paid engagement is legitimate only as transparently identified moderation or content work—never purchased applause or artificial consensus.

Capacity-constrained offers should use [capacity-first campaigns](../../frameworks/marketing/capacity-first-campaign.md): calculate a quality/safety reserve before promotion, distinguish weak interest from informed commitment, verify scarcity, allocate transparently and evaluate fulfilment, access, workload and recovery. Excess demand is a diagnostic signal, not inherently a product or pricing success.

## Metrics

Use one outcome-linked primary metric plus diagnostic measures across acquisition, activation, value realization, retention, revenue, referral, cost, safety, and support burden. Objectives clarify direction; key results are evidence of progress, not task lists or targets detached from customer value.

## Failure modes

- Solution certainty before problem evidence.
- Asking customers to predict purchases instead of observing commitments.
- Building a broad platform before proving one high-value use case.
- Confusing launch revenue with retention or product-market fit.
- Adding tiers, channels, communities, and automations faster than the team can learn or deliver.
- Optimizing conversion at the expense of fit, consent, outcomes, refunds, trust, or founder capacity.
- Treating popular business books and founder case studies as general causal evidence.
