# Evidence-preserving message design

Message design helps a valid idea earn attention, become understandable, support warranted belief, matter to an audience and enable appropriate action. Memorability and retelling are transmission outcomes—not evidence of truth, importance, learning or benefit.

## Two-gate standard

### Gate 1: validity and safety

Before improving rhetorical force, confirm that:

- claims are accurate, current and supported at the stated strength;
- scope, uncertainty, exceptions, alternatives and material risks remain visible;
- sources, commercial interests and authority boundaries are clear;
- the intended action is proportionate, voluntary and reversible where possible;
- health, psychedelic, psychological, tax/legal and financial claims pass their domain review.

A memorable message that fails this gate is a communication risk.

### Gate 2: communication quality

Then test whether the core is unmistakable; surprise produces useful understanding; a novice can picture the mechanism or action; evidence can be inspected; emotion respects dignity; and any story represents reality fairly and enables a suitable next step.

## SUCCESs as a diagnostic

| Lens | Design question | Useful method | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simple | What single idea should govern understanding or action? | Core plus compact explanation, governing intent, generative analogy | Slogan or omitted conditions |
| Unexpected | Which valid expectation should change, and why? | Relevant contrast, mystery or resolved knowledge gap | Clickbait, gimmick or anxiety gap |
| Concrete | What can the audience see, do, count or recognize? | Specific actor/action, example, prototype or scenario | Anecdote mistaken for prevalence or proof |
| Credible | Why should the claim be believed and checked? | Relevant source, human-scale evidence, transparent test | Prestige, false precision or cherry-picking |
| Emotional | Why does this matter to this audience? | Empathy, meaningful consequence or aspiration | Fear, shame, status or identity pressure |
| Stories | What situation supports rehearsal or possibility? | Challenge, connection, creativity or springboard story | Hero myth, survivorship or invented causality |

Not every message needs every lens. Forced surprise, emotion or narrative can reduce clarity and trust.

## Layered message architecture

Simplicity should compress access, not erase evidence:

1. **Core:** one accurate decision-relevant sentence.
2. **Concrete explanation:** mechanism, example or scenario a novice can reconstruct.
3. **Evidence:** source, population, denominator, comparison, uncertainty and date.
4. **Boundary:** non-fit, risks, alternatives, exceptions and referral conditions.
5. **Action:** the smallest appropriate next step with consent and reversibility.

The short form may link to deeper layers, but high-stakes limitations must remain salient at the point of decision.

## Curse-of-knowledge checks

Expertise can obscure missing context. Test whether an intended reader can accurately paraphrase the core, identify who and when it applies, distinguish example from evidence, and take the intended action. If everything appears equally important, choose one governing intent and layer the rest. If teams interpret strategy differently, add observable behavior and nonexamples.

## Evidence translation

Human-scale statistics and analogies can improve comprehension only when the original denominator, time frame, comparison and uncertainty remain accessible. Concrete cases should be labeled as illustration, mechanism evidence, representative pattern or exceptional example. Vividness cannot upgrade evidence class.

## Story provenance

Record whether a story is documented, personal, client-derived, anonymized, composite or hypothetical. Obtain consent where required, minimize identifying detail and disclose selection. Narrative coherence does not establish causation or typicality. Track harmful retellings and meaning drift after publication.

## Correction and “unsticking”

When correcting a sticky falsehood, avoid unnecessary repetition of the myth. Lead with the accurate explanatory model, identify the misunderstanding briefly, provide evidence and a usable alternative. Test current correction research before high-consequence campaigns.

## Measurement

Assess attention, accurate comprehension, correct delayed recall, source/brand attribution, warranted belief, appropriate behavior, outcomes and harm separately. A message can score well on one and fail another.

## Do not infer

- SUCCESs is a validated scoring equation or guarantees transmission.
- Memorability equals learning, truth or behavior change.
- Stories are superior to statistics for warranted belief.
- Emotion is necessary for every message.
- Retelling preserves evidence, scope or accuracy.
