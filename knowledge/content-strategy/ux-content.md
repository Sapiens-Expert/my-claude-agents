# UX content and microcopy

UX content helps a person understand the system, decide confidently, complete a task, recover from problems, and know what will happen next.

## Component standard

| Component | Required qualities |
| --- | --- |
| Page title/heading | Names the topic or task in the user’s language; supports scanning and navigation |
| Button/link | Describes the action or destination; distinct options do not use ambiguous identical labels |
| Field label/help | States what is needed, format/reason where relevant, and privacy implications before error |
| Error | Identifies what happened, preserves entered work, explains recovery, avoids blame |
| Empty state | Explains the state and offers an appropriate next action without fake urgency |
| Confirmation | States what happened, material consequences, timing, and how to undo or get help |
| Permission/consent | Makes purpose, data use, consequences, alternatives, and revocation understandable |
| Notification | Timely, relevant, controllable, and clear about required versus optional action |

## Voice and tone

Voice remains recognizable; tone adapts to risk, emotional state, task, and urgency. In errors, health contexts, payment, privacy, and difficult experiences, prioritize calm clarity over personality or humor.

## Research and measurement

- Use support logs, search terms, interviews, usability tests, task observation, and analytics.
- Test comprehension, completion, error/recovery, confidence, accessibility, and downstream consequences—not clicks alone.
- Diagnose before A/B testing; a statistically higher conversion may still be worse if it increases regret, misunderstanding, or unsafe use.

## Ethical boundary

No hidden costs, confirm-shaming, forced continuity, disguised advertising, obstructed cancellation, misleading defaults, artificial scarcity, or playful design that conceals consequences.
