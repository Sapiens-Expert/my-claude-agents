# Editing and editorial design

## Editing stack

Edit from highest consequence to lowest:

1. **Purpose and truth:** audience/task, intended outcome, thesis, evidence, safety, rights, privacy, and factual accuracy.
2. **Developmental structure:** scope, omissions, argument/story order, causality, pacing, redundancy, and conclusion.
3. **Content design:** reader questions, findability, headings, navigation, channel/context, accessibility, and maintenance.
4. **Line editing:** clarity, cohesion, emphasis, rhythm, tone, transitions, abstraction, and sentence/paragraph shape.
5. **Copyediting:** grammar, usage, consistency, terminology, numbers, citations, names, and house style.
6. **Proofreading:** final typos, formatting, links, captions, page/display problems, and introduced errors.

Polishing sentences before resolving purpose or structure creates expensive rework.

## Reader-task architecture

- Define what the reader is trying to understand, decide, or do under realistic attention constraints.
- Lead with the result, relevance, or orienting context needed to continue.
- Arrange information in the reader's likely question/action order.
- Make the document navigable through descriptive titles, headings, summaries, labels, links, tables, and progressive disclosure.
- Remove content that serves the author's process but not the reader's task.
- Make responding or taking the next step proportionate and easy without hiding consequences.

Less is not automatically clearer. Preserve the context, definitions, uncertainty, and instructions required for safe understanding.

## Sentence and paragraph principles

- Put familiar context before new or complex information when possible.
- Make actors and actions visible; use nominalization/passive voice only with purpose.
- Keep subject and main verb close enough to follow.
- Place important or surprising material where emphasis is strongest.
- Use paragraph openings to orient and endings to land the point or transition.
- Prefer concrete language, but retain necessary technical precision.
- Vary sentence length and structure to match meaning; “short sentences only” is not a style system.
- Read aloud for rhythm and ambiguity, then verify visually for scanning.

## Editorial judgment and style

Usage references and style manuals resolve consistency questions; they do not replace audience, evidence, voice, or context. Maintain a small decision log for recurring names, capitalization, punctuation, numbers, terminology, citations, accessibility, and brand-specific exceptions.

Rules may conflict across Chicago, Garner, Strunk-style guidance, house conventions, platform constraints, and regional English. Choose deliberately, document the choice, and prioritize comprehension over rule performance.

## Data storytelling

1. State the decision/question and audience before selecting a chart.
2. Choose the simplest accurate visual encoding for the comparison.
3. Remove non-informative clutter without removing uncertainty, scale, source, denominator, or context.
4. Direct attention through hierarchy and annotation rather than decorative effects.
5. Separate observation from interpretation and recommendation.
6. Provide accessible text alternatives and never imply causality from visual sequence alone.

## Story and book models

Narrative frameworks such as hero's journey or Story Grid can diagnose stakes, change, choice, and coherence. They are optional lenses, not universal formulas. Nonfiction books should solve a bounded reader problem, make a credible promise, earn trust, and connect chapters to an observable transformation in understanding or action.

Book usefulness is not the same as bestseller potential, funnel performance, or authority signaling.

## Maintenance

Content is a maintained product. Record owner, source/claims, version, publication context, last review, next review, dependencies, and retirement criteria. Correct or retire material when facts, rules, evidence, offers, platforms, or brand strategy change.

## Failure modes

- Copyediting a weak argument into polished misinformation.
- Removing caveats because they interrupt flow.
- Treating personal style preference as objective correctness.
- Applying fiction formulas to scientific or client material until causality is distorted.
- Making charts persuasive by suppressing baselines, uncertainty, or non-response.
- Editing someone else's voice into generic brand prose.
- Endless revision without a decision, audience test, risk reason, or stop condition.
