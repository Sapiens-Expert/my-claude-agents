# Customer-centered message hierarchy

A customer-centered message makes it easy to identify who is deciding, what progress matters, which observable problem the offer addresses, why the brand is an appropriate resource, what participation requires and what outcome is reasonably supported. It is a communication hypothesis produced after strategy, audience research, product validation and claim review.

Story structure can prioritize information; it does not prove that every purchase is a heroic transformation or that clear copy causes growth.

## Adapted message elements

| Element | Required question | Evidence | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Decision-maker | Who is making which decision in what situation? | Audience/job and buying-system research | Allow multiple users, buyers and stakeholders with conflicting needs |
| Progress | What relevant outcome or task matters now? | Past behavior, direct language and category entry point | Do not invent aspiration or reduce the person to one desire |
| Problem | What observable barrier exists and how is it experienced? | Behavior, consequences, alternatives and causal uncertainty | No shame, diagnosis, villain or manufactured injustice |
| Resource/guide | Why is this brand appropriate to help? | Capability, qualifications, method, proof, empathy and limits | Guide is a relational stance, not clinical, moral or outcome authority |
| Plan | What are the real steps and responsibilities? | Delivery process, prerequisites, effort, duration, risks and referral | Preserve complexity material to consent or outcomes |
| Action | What is the smallest appropriate next step? | Readiness and journey evidence | Voluntary, proportionate and reversible; no false urgency |
| Stakes | What realistically follows from action, delay or alternatives? | Base rates, time horizon and pathways | No fear amplification or false buy-versus-fail dichotomy |
| Outcome | What observable result is plausible, for whom and when? | Outcome definition, variability and evidence | No guarantee, rescue or identity completion |
| Transformation | What capability or identity may develop? | Longitudinal evidence and participant language | Optional, non-shaming and never required for belonging |

## Brand role

The audience is a participant or decision-maker, not a fictional character whose emotions the brand controls. The brand can act as a bounded resource, specialist, facilitator, provider or guide. Select the role from brand identity and actual authority. Empathy is demonstrated through listening, design and conduct—not a scripted phrase.

## Problem integrity

Separate the external situation, the person's reported experience and any broader ethical meaning. Do not infer an internal or philosophical problem simply because it makes copy emotionally stronger. In health and coaching, normal stress, aging, uncertainty or nonpurchase must not be framed as brokenness requiring rescue.

## Plan and action clarity

A short process can reduce ambiguity, but it must expose prerequisites, responsibilities, time, effort, price/cost, uncertainty, safety gates and stop/referral conditions when material. Transitional actions—guides, assessments, workshops or calls—must provide genuine standalone value and obtain specific permission.

## Stakes calibration

Describe benefits and costs neutrally. Include no-action and alternative pathways. Match salience to probability, severity, reversibility and evidence. High-stakes health, psychedelic, financial or identity decisions require cooling-off and referral rather than intensified loss framing.

## Campaign deployment

A validated message can guide a one-line explanation, profile, landing page, educational asset, follow-up sequence, testimonial and referral request. Each channel requires its own context, accessibility, consent and measurement. Repetition supports comprehension only while the message is accurate, relevant and not fatiguing.

Testimonials require consent, privacy, truthful editing, selection disclosure and no implication of typicality. Referrals must not create social debt or share another person's contact or sensitive information without permission.

## Relationship to layered messaging

This hierarchy decides **what customer decision information belongs in the message**. The evidence-preserving message system decides **how to express it without losing evidence and boundaries**. Use both: core, concrete explanation, evidence, boundary and proportionate action.

## Do not infer

- Every message needs all narrative elements.
- The customer is always a single hero or wants the brand as guide.
- Every offer solves internal or philosophical pain.
- Negative stakes and transformation increase ethical effectiveness.
- Clear copy alone produces revenue, qualified demand or retained value.
