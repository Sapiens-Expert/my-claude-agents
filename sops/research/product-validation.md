# Product validation

1. Open a brief with the decision, customer hypothesis, riskiest assumption, evidence already held, and evidence missing.
2. Recruit from the real target context; record selection bias and relationship effects.
3. Ask about specific past behavior, current alternatives, consequences, budgets, authority, and failed attempts. Avoid pitching during problem research.
4. Select a test that requires an appropriate commitment. For health-adjacent products, minimize data and avoid diagnostic implication or unsafe self-experimentation.
5. Define thresholds and a timebox before launch. Include negative indicators: confusion, non-use, refund, adverse event, privacy concern, excessive support, or wrong-customer pull.
6. Store raw observations separately from synthesis. Distinguish quote, observation, interpretation, and decision.
7. Review results against the predefined threshold and document the choice: continue, modify, narrow, pause, or stop.
8. Do not automate, add tiers, or scale acquisition until the value and delivery loop is repeatable enough to justify it.

For a proposed new category, validate the underlying problem and behavior before testing terminology. Compare the new frame with established-category and focused-position alternatives; measure comprehension, qualified demand and adoption rather than prompted enthusiasm. Keep category growth distinct from the sponsoring company's performance.
