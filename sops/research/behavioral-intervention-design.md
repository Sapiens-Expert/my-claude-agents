# Behavioral intervention design

1. Define the behavior, population, context, welfare outcome, sponsor goal, and conflicts of interest.
2. Observe current behavior and journey; separate stated intention from actual constraints and alternatives.
3. Build a constraint map across ability/capability, opportunity/environment, motivation, prompts, friction, incentives, norms, energy, and structure.
4. Review primary evidence for the selected mechanism and target population. Popular-book examples cannot pass this step alone.
5. Generate interventions at multiple levels, including service/structural changes and doing nothing.
6. Apply autonomy, transparency, privacy, accessibility, reversibility, vulnerability, distribution, and harm gates.
7. Predefine outcome, behavior, comprehension, guardrail, subgroup, persistence, and stop measures.
8. Pilot proportionately; inspect adverse and unintended effects as actively as desired behavior.
9. Decide, document, monitor, and sunset when welfare, evidence, context, or consent no longer supports the intervention.
