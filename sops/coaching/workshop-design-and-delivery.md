# Workshop design and delivery

## Before

1. Confirm sponsor, audience, problem, desired output, decision rights, scope, price, success evidence, and follow-up.
2. Interview representative participants and review relevant data. Record contradictions and missing stakeholders.
3. Write participant-centered objectives using observable verbs.
4. Design backward from the final artifact or behavior. Select the smallest set of activities that produces it.
5. Build timing with breaks, transitions, buffer, accessibility, language, remote/hybrid needs, materials, room/technology, and a failure fallback.
6. Send an invitation that states purpose, preparation, expectations, privacy, and how outputs will be used.

## During

1. Contract the room: purpose, roles, rules, confidentiality, agenda, decision method, and permission to challenge.
2. Check understanding and energy without forcing personal disclosure.
3. Alternate concise input with individual thinking, group work, practice, feedback, and synthesis.
4. Track participation and power. Intervene on domination, withdrawal, scope drift, unsafe disclosure, and false consensus.
5. Maintain a visible decision/action/parking record. State changes to agenda or scope explicitly.
6. Close with retrieval/reflection, decisions, owners, dates, measures, risks, unresolved questions, and the next contact.

## After

1. Send a concise record distinguishing decisions, recommendations, hypotheses, and open issues.
2. Protect sensitive notes and delete unnecessary personal data.
3. Run immediate process QA, then delayed transfer/outcome checks.
4. Capture reusable methods without copying client content or third-party proprietary frameworks.
5. Update the workshop core only when evidence shows what to retain, remove, or test.

## Minimum measures

- Output completed and accepted by the decision owner.
- Participant ability to perform the target task where learning is involved.
- Commitments completed at the agreed checkpoint.
- Relevant business/behavior indicator movement, with attribution limits stated.
- Qualified follow-up, diagnostic, or referral only when appropriate to the workshop purpose.
