# Coaching scope and referral

1. At intake, state that coaching is not psychotherapy, diagnosis, medical care, crisis support, or medication guidance; agree confidentiality and its safety/legal limits.
2. Record the coaching goal, current supports, relevant care, practitioner competence, jurisdiction, emergency location/contact process, and foreseeable scope risks without collecting unnecessary clinical detail.
3. When concern emerges, slow down. Describe observable information and the client's report; do not infer a disorder, trauma history, recovered memory, attachment type, or hidden part.
4. Ask direct, calm questions needed to determine immediate safety, functional impact, existing professional support, and whether continuing the session is appropriate. Do not conduct a pseudo-clinical assessment beyond training.
5. If danger may be imminent, follow the person's local emergency/crisis pathway and applicable law/contract. Do not leave them dependent on asynchronous messages or routine follow-up.
6. If non-immediate clinical or medical needs exceed scope, explain the boundary and recommend the appropriate qualified professional. Offer a warm handoff with consent when feasible.
7. Decide and communicate whether coaching pauses, ends, narrows, or continues alongside care. Never make access to coaching conditional on sharing clinical records beyond what is necessary.
8. Document facts, exact safety statements where material, consultation, referrals offered, consent, decisions, and follow-up. Protect the record as sensitive health information.
9. Use supervision or professional consultation without exposing identity unnecessarily and without presenting coaching supervision as clinical oversight.
10. Review the referral directory and crisis information regularly; local availability and emergency procedures change.

Do not promise confidentiality that conflicts with law or safety policy, but do not disclose merely because a conversation is uncomfortable or emotionally intense.
