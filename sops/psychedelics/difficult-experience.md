# Difficult-experience response

1. Establish location, timing, immediate physical safety, current impairment, and whether emergency services or an on-site responsible person is needed.
2. Check for acute medical symptoms, danger to self/others, severe confusion/agitation, inability to stay safe, and loss of reality testing.
3. Escalate urgent or uncertain risk to local emergency/crisis/poison or qualified clinical support; do not attempt remote coaching management.
4. If the experience is over, the person is stable, and coaching scope is appropriate, assess sleep, food, routine, support, functioning, ongoing symptoms, and current professional care.
5. Reduce demands, avoid further use and high-consequence decisions, establish support/check-ins, and document deterioration triggers.
6. Explore meaning only after stabilization and without validating literal, recovered-memory, grandiose, persecutory, or diagnostic conclusions.

Never promise confidentiality beyond legal/ethical limits when immediate safety is at stake.
