# Herbal product and claim review

1. Define the intended use, exact population, outcome, time horizon, and whether the statement is historical, culinary, wellness, prevention, treatment, or cure.
2. Identify the product precisely: scientific name, plant part, preparation/extract, concentration, dose, route, duration, manufacturer, batch, jurisdiction, and all ingredients.
3. Check current authoritative monographs, regulator safety communications, clinical guidance, interaction resources, and the strongest applicable human evidence. Archived books cannot pass this step alone.
4. Verify that the studied product and dose match the proposed product; do not extrapolate across species, parts, extracts, routes, or mixtures.
5. Review adverse effects, allergy, dose toxicity, contamination/adulteration, pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions, contraindications, and vulnerable populations.
6. Check medications, other supplements, planned surgery, pregnancy/breastfeeding, age, and relevant liver, kidney, cardiovascular, neurological, bleeding, cancer, transplant, and immune conditions through an appropriate clinician/pharmacist.
7. Separate symptom change, biomarker change, and clinical outcome. Mechanism, tradition, preclinical findings, or testimonials do not establish efficacy.
8. Reject medication-replacement, cure, detox, universal adaptogen, immune-boosting, hormone-balancing, and “natural therefore safe” claims unless a narrowly defined statement passes the full evidence gate.
9. Add poisoning/emergency and adverse-event instructions where material; never improvise acute toxicology advice.
10. Record sources, search/review date, conflicts, uncertainty, decision, reviewer competence, and expiry date. Re-review when formulations or guidance change.
