# Direct messages

Use the [message decision and progression framework](../../frameworks/linkedin/message-decision-and-progression.md) for consequential outreach and the [message QA brief](../../templates/linkedin-message-qa.md) for new message families or experiments.

1. Identify the legitimate context: referral, prior interaction, explicit signal, event, content, or relevant business change. Separate what was publicly observed from what is merely inferred; do not treat a view, like, follow, vote or job change as buying intent.
2. Decide whether a connection request, comment, message, or no action is most appropriate.
3. Write the minimum viable message in Vlad’s voice: verified context, a relevance hypothesis, bounded value/proof if needed, one small optional next step, and an easy exit.
4. Avoid disguised templates, premature links, false familiarity, and diagnosis.
5. Treat a reply as permission to clarify, not as qualification. Advance only when the reply provides evidence of fit, purpose and willingness.
6. Move to a call, email or other channel only by mutual agreement. State why it may be useful, what it is for, and make declining easy.
7. Use a bounded follow-up cadence across all channels; stop after refusal, non-fit, channel preference, or exhausted sequence. Do not rotate through email, phone and LinkedIn to evade silence. A refusal ends the sales attempt—do not convert it into a referral request, gift exchange or alternate pitch unless the recipient independently initiates that path.
8. Log the observable signal/source, inference, relationship context, channel consent/preferences, stage, last touch, suppression state, and dated next action in one coordinated record.

Apply the [marketing permission lifecycle](../marketing/permission-lifecycle.md) when a conversation moves into recurring follow-up, email, a community or another system. Permission for one context is not blanket permission elsewhere.

Do not combine gifts, manufactured similarity, borrowed authority, popularity, scarcity, micro-commitments or “people like us” language to create obligation. The offer and easy refusal must remain clear without those cues.

Audio/video messages are optional and should be used only where existing relationship and context make them welcome. Provide concise text or captions/transcript and a low-bandwidth alternative. Personalization must be accurate, relevant, and non-invasive; appearance, accent and production quality are not competence signals.
