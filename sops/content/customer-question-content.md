# Customer-question content

1. Collect real questions from discovery, search, sales, support, onboarding, objections, cancellations, and non-buyers; preserve wording and provenance.
2. Prioritize by frequency, consequence, decision relevance, uncertainty, and potential harm—not keyword volume alone.
3. Classify the question: price, suitability, process, effort, risk, limitation, alternative, comparison, problem, evidence, or expected outcome.
4. Answer the question directly before adding narrative or a call to action.
5. Support material claims with the appropriate evidence; label assumptions, estimates, experience, and opinion.
6. Disclose scope, limitations, variability, non-fit cases, conflicts, and referral needs. Compare alternatives using explicit, fair criteria.
7. Apply privacy, consent, testimonial, brand-separation, medical, psychedelic, tax, and legal gates before publication.
8. Adapt the presentation to the channel without changing the factual meaning or hiding material detail.
9. Assign an owner and review date for price, product, platform, clinical, scientific, regulatory, and market-sensitive information.
10. Measure qualified use: comprehension, suitable inquiries, progression, retention, complaints, regret, and trust. Revise or retire content when it misleads or becomes stale.
