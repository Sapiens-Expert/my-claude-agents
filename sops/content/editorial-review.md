# Editorial review

## Intake

Record owner, audience, context/channel, user task, business role, desired response, evidence burden, source/version, deadline, constraints, and review authority. Clarify whether the request is developmental editing, line editing, copyediting, proofreading, or a combination.

## Pass 1 — truth and risk

- Verify names, dates, numbers, quotations, links, claims, evidence level, and source meaning.
- Check privacy, consent, copyright, accessibility, brand separation, clinical/legal/tax scope, and potential harm.
- Mark unknowns and unsupported assertions before improving rhetoric.

## Pass 2 — purpose and structure

- Test the controlling idea, scope, reader value, order, omissions, redundancy, argument/story logic, and CTA.
- Build or repair the outline in the reader's question/task order.
- Confirm that headline, opening, headings, summary, and conclusion promise the same thing the body delivers.
- When memorability is a goal, use the [evidence-preserving message framework](../../frameworks/content/evidence-preserving-message.md) only after Pass 1. Check the layered core, evidence, boundary and action rather than forcing surprise, emotion or story.

## Pass 3 — line and content design

- Improve cohesion, emphasis, transitions, paragraphing, concreteness, tone, scanability, navigation, labels, examples, and visual explanation.
- Preserve meaningful nuance and the author's recognizable voice.

## Pass 4 — copy and proof

- Apply the relevant style sheet for spelling, punctuation, capitalization, terminology, numbers, citations, and formatting.
- Run link/display checks and proof the final rendered form, not only the source text.
- Check that edits did not introduce factual, grammatical, layout, or accessibility errors.

## Resolution

Classify comments as required correction, evidence/safety issue, structural recommendation, optional style suggestion, or question. Record unresolved disagreements and who decides. Approve, return, hold, or retire the piece with reason.

Automated editing and generative AI suggestions remain untrusted inputs: verify facts, sources, quotations and inferences; protect confidential/personal data and rights; prevent fabricated experience or expertise; and retain named human accountability. Tool fluency never substitutes for human or specialist review.

Ordinary content should respect the two-pass-then-ship operating rule at the authoring stage; the four review lenses can be combined into those passes. Add cycles only for unresolved evidence, safety, legal, privacy, or high-consequence quality issues.
