# Discovery call

1. Confirm time, purpose, context, and permission to ask direct questions.
2. Ask what changed and what would make the conversation useful.
3. Establish current state with examples and measures.
4. Explore the problem, causes, affected stakeholders, and previous attempts.
5. Clarify consequences without amplifying fear.
6. Define desired state, value, timing, and what “good” would look like.
7. Map decision participants, criteria, process, constraints, and resources.
8. Summarize the gap and ask the buyer to correct it.
9. State fit, non-fit, and uncertainty honestly.
10. Agree one explicit next step with owner and date, or close the opportunity.

Do not turn discovery into interrogation, free consulting, diagnosis outside professional scope, or a disguised pitch. Notes must separate what the buyer said from seller inference.
