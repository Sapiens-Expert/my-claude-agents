# Scaling Up — Verne Harnish and the Gazelles team

## Bibliographic record

- *Scaling Up: How a Few Companies Make It...and Why the Rest Don't*; Verne Harnish and the team at Gazelles; Gazelles, Inc.; first edition, 2014.
- ISBN 978-0-9860195-5-5.
- One 387-page English PDF; SHA-256 `38c566ad25d49b0e8aeaa301bfb26cafb5f6bf63a1d9e30f4757efe61e8725c5`; about 92,483 extracted words; no OCR required.
- Complete coverage: introduction and tools; all 14 chapters across overview, People, Strategy, Execution and Cash; next steps, resources and index.
- Authority: an influential expert-practitioner growth toolkit developed through Gazelles coaching, executive cases and borrowed management frameworks. Strong for structuring management conversations and diagnostic artifacts. Most performance, hiring and financial claims are cases, testimonials or advisor rules of thumb rather than controlled comparative evidence; the author ecosystem sells coaching, software and related services.

## Bottom line

Growth multiplies coordination load, role ambiguity, system friction and cash demand. Harnish's useful contribution is an integrated operating lens across **People, Strategy, Execution and Cash**, supported by one-page artifacts and management rhythms. VladOS should use the tools as diagnostic scaffolds—not a doctrine of perpetual scale—and replace rank-and-yank talent language, metric visibility, one-number priorities and aggressive cash levers with lawful, humane, evidence-aware controls.

## Foundational model: four-decision health check

| Lens | Governing question | Failure signals | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **People** | Do roles, capabilities, authority, relationships and capacity support the work? | founder bottleneck, ambiguous ownership, overload, turnover, exclusion, weak succession | role/process maps, workload, skills, engagement, retention, safety/equity data |
| **Strategy** | Have we diagnosed a meaningful choice about where and how to create value? | generic promises, undifferentiated offering, conflicting markets, activity without advantage | customer/market evidence, economics, alternatives, capabilities, risks and strategy kernel |
| **Execution** | Can priorities turn into coordinated, learning-oriented delivery? | too many priorities, stale plans, dependency failure, meeting overload, gamed metrics | outcomes, guardrails, dependency state, cycle time, quality, customer/employee evidence |
| **Cash** | Can the organization meet obligations and fund its chosen pace under realistic scenarios? | growing revenue with deteriorating cash, margin erosion, arrears, inventory/WIP accumulation, debt stress | cash forecast, conversion cycle, working capital, scenario/sensitivity and covenant/tax review |

The four lenses interact. A cash action that delays suppliers is also a People/Strategy decision; a growth target without capacity becomes an execution and wellbeing risk. Diagnose the whole system before optimizing one box.

## People system

### Governed FACe — function accountability map

For each necessary function, record:

- purpose and decision rights;
- accountable integrator and deputies/succession;
- contributors, consulted/affected groups and independent checks;
- one or two outcome/quality/guardrail indicators;
- capacity, capability and conflict gaps;
- financial-statement or operating results influenced, without pretending sole causality.

One accountable person clarifies integration; it does not erase collective work or permit blame for structurally uncontrollable results.

### Governed PACe — process accountability map

Select the few cross-functional processes that create material value or risk. Map trigger, stages, owner, participants, customer, output, outcome, cycle time, quality, failure/recovery, controls, data and improvement backlog. Include frontline participants in redesign. Process metrics should reveal flow and failure, not intensify pace beyond safe capacity.

### Personal-plan boundary

The source's One-Page Personal Plan links relationships, achievements, rituals and wealth across horizons. Use only voluntarily in coaching. Personal goals, family, health and finances remain private; employment must not require disclosure or alignment of a person's life with corporate objectives.

### Talent and coaching correction

Replace “A Players” and top-10-percent categorization with **role evidence and development**:

1. Define outcomes, competencies, values-as-behaviors, constraints and onboarding support.
2. Use structured, job-related questions and work samples with consistent scoring.
3. Provide accommodations, data minimization and accessible candidate experiences.
4. Check references lawfully and with notice; never pressure disclosure of protected/personal history.
5. Audit selection rates, scoring consistency and opportunity differences.
6. After hire, clarify expectations, coach strengths and gaps, train managers and provide fair improvement paths.
7. Evaluate the role, system and manager before labeling the person.

High performers are contextual, not a fixed human class. Forced ranking, chronic replacement and “right people” rhetoric can destroy learning and psychological safety.

## Strategy system

### Core audit: values, purpose and competencies

- **Values:** a small set of behavioral decision principles supported by positive examples, prohibited conduct, escalation and evidence that leaders accept the same constraints.
- **Purpose:** the durable stakeholder contribution the organization exists to make; distinguish it from growth, revenue, a slogan or a leader's personal identity.
- **Competencies:** capabilities that create verified value and are difficult/relevant enough to invest in; identify dependencies, decay and build/buy/partner decisions.

Discovering “authentic” values is insufficient. Harmful inherited norms must be changed, and employees/affected stakeholders need a voice in interpreting them.

### Governed Seven Strata

| Source stratum | Governed question |
| --- | --- |
| Words you own | Which category/problem association is supported by current audience evidence—not claimed ownership? |
| Sandbox and brand promises | Which customers, needs, offerings and geographies are in/out; which few promises are measurable and controllable? |
| Promise guarantee | What proportionate remedy proves accountability without false certainty or excluding difficult customers? |
| One-phrase strategy | Which operational choice drives economics, and what trade-off or stakeholder cost does it create? |
| Differentiating activities | Which coherent activities reinforce the choice and are observable rather than generic? |
| X-factor | What underlying constraint/advantage hypothesis changes the economics, and how will it be tested? |
| Profit per X and BHAG | Which economic denominator supports responsible decisions; which long-horizon ambition includes guardrails and review? |

Use competitor analysis, substitutes and non-consumption. A “10x–100x advantage” or industry domination is a hypothesis, not a fact. Brand promises must reflect delivered experience and must not become misleading guarantees.

### Governed one-page strategic plan

Compress a validated strategy, but link each field to supporting evidence:

- core values/purpose and stakeholder outcomes;
- strategic diagnosis and central challenge;
- target customer/market and exclusions;
- positioning, promises and coherent differentiating activities;
- long-horizon ambition with safety/sustainability conditions;
- three-to-five-year capabilities and scenarios;
- annual outcomes and resource choices;
- quarterly outcomes, owners, dependencies and guardrails;
- current KPIs/critical uncertainty;
- assumptions, decision log and source links.

The page is a navigation layer, not the complete strategy. Rumelt's diagnosis–guiding policy–coherent action test governs it.

## Execution system

### Priority contract

Use one dominant quarterly change only when it truly reduces a shared constraint. Preserve a small portfolio for mandatory operations, safety, maintenance and learning. For each priority state outcome, finish line, owner, capacity, dependencies, guardrails, review cadence and what will not be done. Themes/celebrations must be culturally inclusive, voluntary and never substitute for resources or fair pay.

### Data loop

- Combine financial/operational measures with weekly qualitative signals from frontline workers and customers.
- Ask Start/Stop/Keep as an invitation, not a surveillance ritual; close the feedback loop visibly.
- Select leading indicators only after documenting their causal hypothesis and validity.
- Pair speed/volume with quality, safety, equity, workload and customer-outcome metrics.
- Provide access by role, protect individual data and let people challenge/correct records.
- Treat an all-green dashboard with skepticism: it may signal low targets, aggregation or suppressed bad news.

### Who–What–When decision log

At the close of a decision meeting, record the specific deliverable/decision, accountable owner, contributors, due/review date, dependencies, definition of done and escalation trigger. Do not break substantial work into artificial weekly promises that encourage superficial completion.

### Meeting-rhythm router

| Cadence | Legitimate purpose | Stop/shorten when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily huddle | Fast operational coordination and blockers in interdependent/volatile work | work is asynchronous/independent, status is available elsewhere, or it fragments deep work |
| Weekly | Metrics, customer/employee signals, priorities, decisions and WWW commitments | it becomes reporting theatre or lacks decisions |
| Monthly | Cross-functional learning, capability/process issues and financial review | topics can be handled at another cadence or participants lack decision roles |
| Quarterly | Strategy assumptions, outcomes, capacity and next-cycle choices | it merely refreshes themes without confronting evidence |
| Annual | Purpose, longer scenarios, portfolio and capital/capability decisions | annual plan is treated as a fixed forecast |

Every recurring meeting needs an owner, decision rights, required inputs, timebox, accessible record and periodic deletion review.

## Governed Rockefeller Habits checklist

1. Leadership team can debate safely, decide clearly and repair conflict.
2. A bounded quarterly priority is explicit alongside non-negotiable operations and non-goals.
3. Information travels through an appropriate communication rhythm with low distortion.
4. Functions and cross-functional processes have accountable integration and checks.
5. Frontline input is consented, protected, acted upon and closed-loop.
6. Customer/affected-stakeholder evidence is as timely as financial evidence.
7. Values/purpose govern leader behavior and difficult trade-offs.
8. People understand the strategic choices relevant to their work, not merely memorize slogans.
9. Teams have meaningful controllable feedback without reducing a “good day” to one number.
10. Plans/performance are sufficiently visible for coordination while privacy and sensitive strategy are protected.

Implement incrementally and measure whether each habit improves decisions/outcomes. The source's 24–36-month sequence is a practitioner suggestion, not a validated standard.

## Cash system

### Cash visibility and conversion map

Maintain a rolling, scenario-based cash forecast covering receipts, payroll, tax, debt, suppliers, committed investment and liquidity headroom. Map the cash conversion cycle:

`inventory/WIP days + receivable days − payable days`

Segment by business model; service/subscription/project firms may need different drivers. Cash forecasts, accounting policies, taxes, covenants and solvency decisions require qualified finance/accounting advice.

### Power of One sensitivity model

Model the marginal effect of:

1. price;
2. sales volume;
3. direct cost/cost of goods;
4. operating expenses;
5. receivable days;
6. inventory/WIP days;
7. payable days.

For each scenario record assumptions, interactions, implementation cost, time lag, confidence range and stakeholder consequence. Do not add seven isolated one-percent effects as if they were independent or equally feasible.

### Ethical cash-lever gate

- **Price:** test demand/fairness, contracts, accessibility and value delivery; prohibit deceptive fees.
- **Volume:** include capacity, service quality, returns/churn and working-capital demand.
- **Direct/operating cost:** protect wages, safety, quality, resilience and supplier viability; waste reduction is not indiscriminate cutting.
- **Receivables:** simplify invoicing and dispute resolution before pressuring financially vulnerable customers.
- **Inventory/WIP:** avoid stockouts, unsafe buffers or transferring volatility to workers/suppliers.
- **Payables:** honor contracts and supplier dependence; delaying payment is not free financing.

### Financial heuristics boundary

The book proposes ratios such as labor-efficiency and profit targets, two months of operating cash, and conservative debt practices. Treat these as questions for scenario analysis—not universal thresholds. Capital intensity, seasonality, regulation, geography, stage, ownership and risk tolerance change the answer. Never reduce staffing or hours from a ratio alone; analyze demand, process design, service commitments, worker consent, legal duties and downstream risk.

## Integrated scaling decision protocol

Before pursuing a growth initiative:

1. **Define value:** what stakeholder outcome improves and why scale is the right mechanism.
2. **Diagnose constraint:** demand, capability, leadership, process, technology, cash, regulation or something else.
3. **Choose scenario:** organic growth, partnership, replication, automation, focus, consolidation or no growth.
4. **People check:** roles, leadership, capacity, skill, workforce voice, safety and succession.
5. **Strategy check:** target, promise, differentiation, economics, alternatives and externalities.
6. **Execution check:** priority portfolio, dependency/process map, metrics/guardrails and cadence.
7. **Cash check:** conversion cycle, downside forecast, funding terms, tax/covenants and stakeholder effects.
8. **Stage gates:** run a bounded reversible pilot before irreversible infrastructure or debt.
9. **Review:** compare predicted and actual demand, quality, cash, workload, equity and harms.
10. **Decide:** scale, redesign, pause, shrink or stop. Scaling down can be strategic success.

## Evidence, ethics and applicability limitations

- The book foregrounds successful growth companies and supportive executives, creating survivorship, selection and confirmation bias. Failure cases and counterfactuals are sparse.
- Claims that habits “dramatically” improve profitability, cash or management time are not supported by comparative effect estimates in the text.
- Gazelles/Scaling Up benefits from tool adoption, coaching certification, software, events and community services; the commercial interest is material.
- Several concepts are licensed/borrowed from Collins, Topgrading, Lencioni, Deming, Simple Numbers and others. Preserve attribution and avoid presenting the combined toolkit as original empirical validation.
- Growth can worsen environmental impact, precarity, monopoly power or customer harm. Stakeholder value and planetary/legal constraints govern scale.
- “A Player,” “dominate,” “war for talent,” peer pressure, visible scores and labor trimming can normalize exclusion and instrumental treatment. VladOS rejects those framings.
- Culture is not created by repeated founder messaging. Incentives, decision rights, leader behavior, justice and the ability to dissent matter more.
- Financial examples reflect 2014 practices and particular private companies. Current accounting, tax, employment, lending and insolvency rules require professional verification.
