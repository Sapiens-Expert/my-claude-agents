- Authority: a widely used practitioner design and experimentation framework connected to Strategyzer and *Business Model Generation*. It is a strong shared language and hypothesis-management tool, not independent proof that a proposition will succeed.

## Bottom line

- The Value Proposition Canvas links a **Customer Profile**—jobs, pains and gains—to a **Value Map**—products/services, pain relievers and gain creators.
- Fit is progressive: plausible problem-solution fit on paper, demonstrated product-market fit in behavior, and viable business-model fit in economics and delivery.
- The canvas is a hypothesis surface. Its value comes from customer research, multiple prototypes, explicit critical assumptions, measurable experiments and continuous revision—not from completing sticky notes.
- The book's strongest operating principle is to spend little while uncertainty is high, then invest more only as evidence becomes stronger.
- VladOS should adopt the framework with added stakeholder, ethics, privacy, accessibility, safety, evidence and externality controls.

## Core ontology

### Customer Profile

**Jobs** are what a defined customer is trying to accomplish, solve or satisfy in a particular context:

- functional tasks or problems;
- social outcomes such as how the person wants to be perceived;
- personal/emotional states;
- supporting jobs as buyer, cocreator or transferrer/disposer of value.

Record jobs in the person's language and rank their importance. Context, frequency, consequence and current workaround matter. A demographic is not a job, and a business objective is not automatically the customer's objective.

**Pains** are unwanted outcomes, characteristics, obstacles and risks before, during or after a job. Make them concrete and rank severity. Separate observed difficulty, expressed concern, predicted risk and organizational harm; do not inflate anxiety to make an offer appear necessary.

**Gains** are functional utility, savings, positive emotions, social benefits or outcomes the customer values. Distinguish required, expected, desired and unexpected gains; make success thresholds concrete and rank relevance. “Unexpected” must mean discovered benefit, not an excuse to ignore what people say.

### Value Map

- **Products and services:** the offer components available to the segment.
- **Pain relievers:** how components are expected to reduce specific important pains.
- **Gain creators:** how components are expected to produce specific important gains.

Rank each item. A check mark between map and profile means a design hypothesis, not verified causation. An element without a meaningful profile connection may be waste; an important pain or gain need not all be addressed by one offer.

### Three stages of fit

1. **Problem-solution fit:** evidence that the segment cares about specified jobs, pains and gains, plus a proposed map that plausibly addresses them. The proposed solution is not yet validated.
2. **Product-market fit:** behavioral evidence that the delivered proposition creates value and gains traction for the defined segment and context. Treat this as an accumulating judgment, not a universal milestone.
3. **Business-model fit:** evidence that the proposition can be created, delivered and sustained through workable resources, partners, channels, acquisition/retention, revenues and costs.

Add two VladOS gates: **responsible-delivery fit** (safety, legality, privacy, accessibility, capability and referral) and **stakeholder/ecosystem fit** (affected noncustomers, partners, workers, community and externalities).

## Governed Value Proposition Design loop

### 1. Frame the decision

Define the proposition, decision owner, intended use, time horizon and evidence required. Name each relevant actor separately: user, buyer, budget holder, approver, administrator, beneficiary, referrer and affected nonuser. Create separate profiles when their jobs, risks or power differ.

### 2. Build an evidence-backed profile

Use existing research, interviews, observation, direct experience, cocreation and experiments. For every job, pain or gain retain:

- exact observation or source and date;
- actor, context and sample;
- interpretation and alternatives;
- importance/severity estimate and uncertainty;
- current behavior, workaround or competing alternative;
- privacy/consent status and sensitive-data class.

The book's six roles—data detective, journalist, anthropologist, impersonator, cocreator and scientist—should be mixed because each has different blind spots. “Be the customer” is useful empathy work but never substitutes for representative research.

### 3. Generate multiple value maps

Create rough alternatives before refinement. Use sketches, ad-libs, canvases, scenarios and only then learning prototypes/MVPs. Vary segment, job, delivery, pricing, channel and business-model assumptions rather than creating cosmetic versions of one preferred idea.

The book's prototyping discipline is: visual and tangible, beginner's mind, alternatives before attachment, comfort with ambiguity, low fidelity first, expose ideas early, learn faster, stay customer-centered and discard freely. Time boxes reduce sunk-cost attachment but should not suppress complex safety or stakeholder analysis.

### 4. Select hypotheses, not winners

Assess candidates against the book's ten characteristics:

1. embedded in a sound business model;
2. focused on the most important jobs, pains and gains;
3. aimed at underserved needs;
4. concentrated on a few elements done well;
5. attentive to functional, emotional and social jobs;
6. aligned with how customers define success;
7. relevant to many or valuable enough to a viable niche;
8. meaningfully differentiated;
9. substantially better on at least one valued dimension;
10. difficult to copy.

Add safety, fairness, accessibility, capability, environmental/social impact, strategic coherence and evidence readiness. A workshop score chooses what to test; it does not select truth.

### 5. Test in dependency order

Build a hypothesis inventory across:

- **circle/customer:** segment, jobs, pains, gains and their ranking;
- **square/proposition:** desired products/services, actual relief and actual gain;
- **rectangle/business model:** partners, activities, resources, channels, relationships, acquisition/retention, revenue and cost;
- **responsibility:** harm, exclusion, privacy, security, regulatory and professional-scope assumptions.

Identify “idea killers”—high-criticality assumptions whose failure undermines the model. Test cheap and fast while uncertainty is high, then use costlier and more realistic methods as evidence accumulates.

### 6. Use Test and Learning Cards

A governed **Test Card** records owner/date, hypothesis, criticality, experiment, population/context, comparison where feasible, metric, denominator, threshold, time, cost, expected reliability, ethical/privacy review and stopping rule.

A governed **Learning Card** records hypothesis, method, observations/data, missingness, reliability, result against the preregistered threshold, alternative explanations, insight, decision, owner and next action. Validating one test does not validate the whole proposition.

Possible decisions are invalidate/pivot, remain uncertain/test again, provisionally support/increase realism, or stop because risk/cost exceeds learning value. Preserve negative results to prevent organizational forgetting.

### 7. Launch, monitor and evolve

After launch, monitor three linked layers:

- business-model performance: sustainable revenue, cost, capacity, channel and retention;
- proposition performance: adoption, task success, relief/gain indicators, reliability and accessibility;
- customer/stakeholder outcomes: satisfaction plus behavior, harms, complaints, equity and unintended effects.

Use the canvases to align product, service, marketing, sales, partners, employees and governance, but provide role-specific evidence rather than one universal message. Revisit the canvas after material customer, market, technology, regulatory or capability changes.

## Experiment evidence ladder

| Evidence | What it can support | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interview/survey statement | language, perceived job, concern, preference or intention | actual behavior, willingness to pay or retention |
| Search/social/CRM pattern | salience or recurring expressed demand in the observed channel | representative population need or causality |
| Ad/link/landing-page action | message/channel response under test conditions | product value, payment, sustained use or outcomes |
| Prototype/task observation | usability, comprehension and interaction problems | market demand or scalable delivery |
| LOI/crowdfunding/preorder/mock sale | stronger commitment at a specified price/context | completed use, satisfaction, renewal or unit economics |
| Paid pilot/MVP use | adoption and value under bounded delivery conditions | broad product-market fit or scalable economics |
| Repeat use/renewal/referral/outcome | durable value for the observed cohort | incrementality without a counterfactual or transfer to other segments |
| Scaled cohort economics and safety | stronger business/responsible-delivery fit | permanent advantage; conditions can change |

Customers' actions are often stronger evidence than statements, but observable action is not automatically unbiased. Recruitment, framing, incentive, friction, surveillance, novelty and researcher presence all affect behavior.

## Safety, ethics and autonomy corrections

- Never enroll people “unknowingly” in material behavioral experiments without lawful basis, proportionality and appropriate consent/debriefing. The book's wording is not an acceptable default.
- Collect the minimum customer data needed; define retention, access and deletion. Search, CRM, social and website traces may reveal sensitive health, psychological, political or financial attributes.
- Do not infer hidden emotional jobs or vulnerabilities for persuasion. Confirm sensitive interpretations and allow refusal.
- A CTA should measure interest, not manufacture commitment through deception, scarcity or irreversible cost. Label mock, concierge and Wizard-of-Oz experiences honestly when nondisclosure would be material.
- Preorders and crowdfunding require clear refund, delivery-risk and data-use terms. Letters of intent must not be described as revenue.
- Include people who abandon, cannot access, decline tracking or are harmed—not only converters and satisfied users.
- In healthspan, coaching and psychedelic contexts, “pain reliever” is a canvas term, not a medical-treatment claim. Separate educational/coaching outcomes from diagnosis, prevention, treatment or cure; require clinical evidence and qualified review where applicable.
- Experiments cannot delay necessary care, induce unsafe behavior or test contraindicated exposure. Provide referral, crisis and adverse-event routes.
- Compare against doing nothing, current workaround and credible alternatives; represent competitors symmetrically and disclose commercial interest.

## Evidence and epistemic assessment

- The canvas integrates established jobs-to-be-done, design, customer-development, lean-startup and business-model ideas into a highly usable visual grammar. Ease of use is its main strength and also its main risk: a completed canvas can create false confidence.
- Most company examples illustrate concepts after the fact. They do not isolate the method's causal contribution, report unsuccessful adopters or establish base rates.
- Customer categories are modeler-created abstractions. Within-segment heterogeneity, changing contexts, power differences and noncustomers can overturn a clean profile.
- The three fit stages are not measured with universal thresholds. Teams must define proposition-, segment-, time- and model-specific criteria.
- The claim that market evidence “trumps opinion” is directionally valuable, but evidence is designed and interpreted by people. Data quality, ethics, strategic judgment and domain expertise still matter.
- The “revenue greater than cost” shorthand omits cash timing, capital, risk, public/nonprofit value, cross-subsidy, ecosystem health and externalized cost.
- The experiment library reflects a 2014 digital environment. Named advertising, tracking and analytics tactics need current platform, privacy and regulatory review before use.
- Strategyzer services and resources are commercially connected to the framework. This does not invalidate the method but warrants conflict awareness.

## VladOS applications

- **Executive coaching:** profile the executive, sponsor and organization separately; test format, adherence and perceived usefulness without claiming biological or business outcomes from engagement alone.
- **O!Sapiens:** connect each module to a verified user job and measurable learning/behavior outcome; treat health recommendations, sensitive personalization and AI inference as responsibility-gated assumptions.
- **Entheogen Expert:** map preparation/integration jobs and barriers without positioning psychedelic use as required. Include abstention, clinical care and emergency/referral alternatives.
- **LinkedIn/content:** use posts, comments and DMs as research signals and message experiments, not proof of need or entitlement to target individuals. Link variants to a canonical evidence-backed proposition.
- **Corporate workshops:** create role-specific canvases for buyer, participant, manager, HR/procurement and affected teams; test transfer and workplace outcomes beyond attendance/satisfaction.
- **FANATIC/brand strategy:** connect positioning and narrative choices to verified customer success criteria, differentiation and delivery capability; brand meaning cannot repair a weak or unsafe offer.

## Corpus synthesis

- *Business Model Generation* supplies the wider nine-block business model; this book zooms into the customer-segment/value-proposition relationship and its tests.
- *The Mom Test* strengthens interview design by reducing compliments, hypotheticals and solution pitching; *Continuous Discovery Habits* adds continuous opportunity mapping and team cadence.
- *Testing Business Ideas* should extend the experiment library and evidence design; *Lean Startup* supplies build-measure-learn and innovation accounting.
- *They Ask, You Answer* converts recurring buyer questions into education; Value Proposition Design determines which jobs and decision pains those answers should serve.
- *Blue Ocean Strategy*, category design and positioning sources widen alternative spaces; the Value Proposition Canvas forces those strategic claims back toward customer evidence and business-model feasibility.
- Behavioral-science and ethical-marketing sources are needed to govern emotional jobs, CTAs and experimentation so customer understanding does not become vulnerability exploitation.
