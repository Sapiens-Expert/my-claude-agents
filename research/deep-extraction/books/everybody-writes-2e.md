# Everybody Writes, second edition — Ann Handley

## Bibliographic record

- Full title: *Everybody Writes: Your New and Improved Go-To Guide to Creating Ridiculously Good Content*.
- Author/publisher/date: Ann Handley; John Wiley & Sons; second edition, 2023.
- ISBNs: 978-1-119-85416-6 (hardback), 978-1-119-85432-6 (Adobe PDF), 978-1-119-85431-9 (EPUB).
- Source: one identical 533-page English PDF stored in four source folders; SHA-256 `2ecb1c2c283f26bc5a210ea1a21c60431dbc5aae9debb90785b1e97f835965bb`.
- Completeness: Start Here; all 91 numbered chapters across seven parts; tools acknowledgments; epilogue; index. Embedded text yielded about 91,527 words; no OCR was required.
- Authority: an established marketer/editor's practitioner handbook supported by examples, interviews, cited commentary and selected studies. It is not a systematic research review, legal opinion, platform specification or universal style authority.

## Bottom line

- Writing is treated as a learnable operating capability rather than an innate gift. All consequential organizational writing—including email, internal documentation, social posts, UI copy, scripts and sales messages—deserves deliberate audience, editorial and publishing standards.
- The book's central contribution is the 17-step **Writing GPS: Go–Push–Shine**. It joins purpose, reader value, evidence, structure, drafting, distance, rewriting, voice, headline, automated and human editing, read-aloud/scanning review, publication, CTA and post-publication learning.
- Its strongest philosophy is publishing as a privilege: audience attention creates duties of usefulness, truth, empathy, originality, attribution, permission, correction and generosity.
- Brand voice is a durable identity expressed through repeated language choices; tone changes with situation and audience state. Voice should make an organization recognizable without making serious contexts playful or unsafe.
- The marketing-format advice is useful as a starting checklist, not a set of formulas. Page length, headlines, channel practice, LinkedIn conventions, hashtags, SEO metadata, email behavior, copyright and AI tools require context and current verification.
- VladOS should adopt a governed writing pipeline that adds risk classification, evidence-to-claim matching, accessibility, privacy/consent, sensitive-story safeguards, AI provenance and accountable approval to Handley's workflow.

## Governed Writing GPS

### Go — purpose, audience and evidence

| Step | Source instruction | VladOS implementation |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Goal | Define the audience decision/job, desired useful outcome, organizational objective, owner and success/guardrail measures |
| 2 | “So what?” | Reframe value for one contextual reader; identify questions, burden, constraints, alternatives and why now |
| 3 | Add data/examples | Build a claim–evidence map using primary/authoritative sources, first-hand experience, counterevidence, uncertainty and conflicts |
| 4 | Organize | Select structure for the reader's task and risk; create the one-sentence takeaway and logical outline |

### Push — draft, distance and perspective

| Step | Source instruction | VladOS implementation |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 5 | Ugly First Draft | Draft freely without treating speed or fluency as publication readiness; mark unsupported claims and missing inputs |
| 6 | Walk away | Add an appropriate cooling interval; urgent/high-risk content still needs independent review |
| 7 | Draft 2 | Remove wrong/redundant words, repair logic and align every section with the takeaway |
| 8 | Draft 3 for one reader | Test comprehension, relevance, emotional effect, ability, language, culture and accessibility without stereotyping |
| 9 | Draft 4 with voice | Add recognizable style, useful analogy, warmth and proportionate humor; preserve evidence and context |
| 10 | Headline/title | Make the promise accurate, clear, specific and independently understandable; no curiosity debt or risk inflation |

### Shine — verification, editing, publication and learning

| Step | Source instruction | VladOS implementation |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 11 | Robot edit | Use tools as fallible suggestions; protect confidential data and record material AI assistance where required |
| 12 | Human edit | Assign substantive, line/copy and specialist review proportionate to stakes; author remains accountable |
| 13 | Read aloud | Test cadence, ambiguity, voice, pronunciation, cognitive load and awkward phrasing |
| 14 | Eyeball for scanners | Check hierarchy, progressive disclosure, contrast, whitespace, link meaning, alt text, captions and device behavior |
| 15 | Publish + “what now?” | Give a proportionate CTA, easy no, safe referral/alternative and clear next step; fulfill the content promise first |
| 16 | Let go | Freeze/version the released artifact while retaining correction, update and withdrawal capability |
| 17 | Joy + regret | Run post-publication review: outcomes, error/correction log, feedback, harm, learning and reuse/maintenance decision |

### Added gates

Before Step 5, classify factual/causal/comparative/action claims and health, legal, financial, safety, reputational, identity and third-party risks. Before Step 15, require evidence, consent/rights, privacy/security, accessibility, brand, specialist and legal/clinical review only where applicable. The goal is not approval by committee; it is accountable routing.

## Part I — complete writing rules synthesis

### Practice and process (1–13)

- Treat everyday asynchronous communication as writing and a representation of personal/organizational character.
- Build skill through frequent practice, reading and a sustainable ritual. Handley's small daily goals, four-square diary (Did/Saw/Heard/Draw), prompts, morning pages and copywork are optional exercises, not productivity mandates.
- Reject school-derived verbosity and formula when it harms clarity. Retain structures when they serve the actual genre or evaluation context.
- Publishing is privilege, not content “churn”: useful, inspired and empathetic to the reader; credible, honest and real; differentiated by voice.
- Separate drafting from judging. The Ugly First Draft is a protected thinking space, followed by deletion/restructure, reader-perspective and voice/humor passes.
- Customer empathy should be evidence-led and bounded. Replace “pathological empathy” with **rigorous audience perspective-taking**: direct research, support/sales insight, observation, accessibility and diverse review. Do not claim to inhabit another person's experience or exploit pain.
- “Think before ink”: objective → point of view → repeated So what/Because → one-sentence message.

### Organization and expression (14–26)

Handley offers 15 structures: quiz, skeptic, explainer, case study, contrarian, how-to, quick how-to, how-not-to, first person, comparison, Q&A, data, expert round-up, provocative/insider and literary/borrowed form. Choose by decision job, evidence and audience—not novelty.

- Write initially to a trusted one-person proxy to loosen voice, then verify against the real audience.
- Delete the writer's “running start”; front-load important actors, actions and value.
- Keep modifiers beside what they modify; give first and last sentences special attention.
- Show through observable detail, mechanism, example, behavior and outcome rather than unsupported adjectives.
- Humor comes from truth, surprise, specificity, escalation, callbacks and rewrite. Do not punch down, trivialize harm or use inside jokes that exclude.
- Use fresh analogies/metaphors and sensory detail when they clarify. Label metaphor and avoid misleading causal transfer.
- Make lists parallel and visibly structured.
- Approach writing as teaching: evidence, context, explanation, example and genuine interest.
- Keep it simple, not simplistic. Plain language must preserve material complexity, uncertainty and technical meaning.
- Design and words are one system; whitespace, hierarchy and visuals support scanning, but historical “percentage read” and image-frequency findings are only hypotheses.

### Collaboration and delivery (27–32)

- A writing partner can support ideation, first-read feedback and accountability, provided confidentiality and roles are clear.
- Avoid “hot dog writing” by agreeing purpose/audience/voice, approving a detailed outline, assigning reviewers to expertise, defining review questions/rounds/deadlines and giving one editorial owner synthesis authority.
- Human editors remain essential. Separate substantive/developmental, line, copy/fact and proof functions; one person may perform several passes, but each function should be explicit.
- Word-count goals, stop-while-interested and deadlines can help momentum; they are optional scaffolds, not quality evidence.

## Part II — grammar and usage rules

The 13 chapters favor real, familiar words; reject invented corporate Frankenwords and “Weblish”; default to present tense and active voice; strengthen verbs; reduce unnecessary adverbs and clichés; correct common punctuation, agreement, modifier and word-choice errors; distinguish frequently confused terms; break formal rules intentionally; limit moralizing; and notice eggcorns/mondegreens.

VladOS language standard:

- Choose the form that is accurate, clear, inclusive and natural for the audience.
- Active voice is especially important for ownership and accountability; passive voice is valid when actor is unknown/irrelevant or focus belongs on the affected object.
- Present tense can add immediacy but must not falsify chronology or evidence status.
- Adverbs, fragments, contractions, sentence openings and purposeful rule-breaking are valid when meaning improves.
- Do not strip necessary qualifiers from scientific, medical, legal, financial or research claims merely to sound decisive.
- Maintain a versioned house style and terminology list; dictionaries and style guides resolve convention, not substantive truth.

## Voice system

### Definition

- **Voice:** the relatively stable personality, values and point of view audible across language choices.
- **Tone:** the contextual expression of that voice based on audience state, channel, moment, risk and relationship.

Handley's four voice dimensions—funny/serious, formal/casual, respectful/irreverent and enthusiastic/matter-of-fact—are useful sliders, not exhaustive categories.

### Voice-development workflow

1. Audit actual language across high-impact and neglected touchpoints: website, product/UI, errors, email, invoices, support, forms, policies, sales, social and internal writing.
2. Research how chosen audiences describe their situations and how the organization actually behaves.
3. Select four distinctive, defensible attributes; avoid table stakes and buzzwords.
4. Define each as observable language behavior: “we are / we are not / therefore we write like this.”
5. Document pronouns, terminology/jargon, mechanics, inclusivity/accessibility, examples and counterexamples.
6. Create tone adaptations for routine, celebratory, confused, frustrated, sensitive, crisis and high-stakes contexts.
7. Test unlabeled samples for recognition, comprehension, trust, fit and unintended exclusion.
8. Train contributors, embed examples/tools and review quarterly or after material change.

Voice must follow conduct. Quirky copy cannot compensate for weak service, misleading terms or unsafe practice. Humor yields to clarity and care in emergencies, complaints, addiction, illness, loss and regulated decisions.

## Story system

### Six elements

1. True: actual people, facts and emotions; sources, disclosures and corrections.
2. Human: specific people and lived consequences, even in B2B.
3. Original: a defensible perspective or expression unique to the brand.
4. Customer as hero: product/company enables value rather than monopolizing attention.
5. Emotional: a proportionate feeling grounded in truth.
6. Strategically aligned: brand positioning + distinctive value + marketing goal.

The source's useful maxim is “tell a true story well.” VladOS adds consent, dignity, representation, outcome substantiation, privacy, compensation/conflict disclosure and withdrawal/remedy—especially for clients, patients, employees and vulnerable people.

### Story artifacts

- **Unique brand story:** founding/evolution, problem, insight, mission, worldview, overlooked practices and credible future contribution; reject interchangeable “Messaging Karaoke.”
- **Product/customer story:** the Rudolph template—product, capability, doubt/barrier, triggering moment, need, ultimate beneficiary and customer hero. Do not manufacture a crisis or reduce complex outcomes to the product.
- **Hermit Crab Content:** place a true story into a borrowed form such as recipe, profile, contest, comic, instruction manual or cultural format. Respect copyright, cultural context and platform rules; borrowed form must improve meaning.
- **Case/campaign:** a human hero can generate affinity and insight, but lead conversion does not validate representativeness or social impact. Report selection, method, denominator and non-commercial outcomes.

## Publishing and evidence standard

### Editorial responsibilities

- Share news only when it is useful or consequential to the external audience; keep routine corporate updates in appropriate internal/archive channels.
- Be **biased and balanced**: state a point of view, present material facts, strongest counterargument, limitations and fair competitor/alternative comparison.
- A brand may operate media-like publishing, but it is not independent merely because the design feels editorial. Sponsorship, ownership, editorial mandate, conflicts and correction policy must be visible.
- Gate content only when the exchange is proportionate, transparent and useful. Prefer give-before-ask, optional formats, progressive profiling and controlled experiments; never make essential safety/access information conditional on lead capture.
- Use trends, memes, newsjacking and public causes only when there is a relevant contribution, adequate knowledge, stakeholder legitimacy, timely verification and an action beyond opportunistic attention.
- Interviews: prepare but converse; ask specific/open questions; listen, probe examples, allow silence, confirm identity/pronouns; record only with consent and agree on attribution/review terms.

### Fact and source protocol

1. Check names, titles, organizations, dates, URLs, quotations, numbers, math and visual labels.
2. Seek the closest primary source and current authoritative guidance.
3. Define on-record, background and off-record before substantive disclosure.
4. Identify incentives, sponsors, employer, competitors and other conflicts.
5. Match evidence type/quality to claim; “recognized expert” and major media are not automatically authoritative.
6. Cite as research is captured; preserve source, date, locator, excerpt/context and rights.
7. Prefer freshness appropriate to the claim—not the book's fixed two-/four-year rule. Foundational evidence can remain relevant; fast-changing claims may expire in days.
8. Publish a visible correction note with what changed, when and why.

Wikipedia is a discovery route, not automatically unusable; verify through cited primary/authoritative sources. Data is not credible merely because it comes from government, research reports, NGOs or Google tools; inspect methods, population, uncertainty, incentives and applicability.

### Ethical curation and rights

Curating means selecting, contextualizing and adding genuine analysis—not copying or mechanical aggregation. Attribute the primary creator prominently, quote proportionately, link directly, add an original contribution, preserve context and obtain permission/licence when required.

The copyright chapters give useful U.S.-oriented awareness but are not legal advice. Fair use is fact-specific and jurisdiction varies. Attribution alone does not grant permission; links do not cure copying; platform-provided music/media rights may be platform- and use-specific. Record rights-holder, licence, territory, duration, media, commercial/derivative/AI permissions and evidence of consent. Escalate uncertainty to qualified counsel.

The source's blanket advice to avoid `nofollow` when citing/curating is not an ethical rule and can conflict with current search policy for sponsored, affiliate or user-generated links. Link semantics require current official verification.

## Editorial roles and approval design

| Role | Primary question | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Author/owner | What are we trying to say and why? | Accountable draft and decisions |
| Developmental/substantive editor | Does the argument/story work for the audience? | Structure, gaps, logic, scope |
| Line editor | Does each paragraph/sentence express the intended voice and meaning? | Flow, precision, language |
| Copy/fact editor | Is it correct, consistent and sourced? | Mechanics, terminology, claim/source checks |
| Proof/accessibility QA | Is the released artifact intact and usable? | Final errors, links, layout, alt/captions, device checks |
| Specialist reviewer | Is high-stakes content safe/current in domain? | Approval, caveat, revision or rejection |

Reviewers comment within their scope; editorial owner adjudicates conflicts and records material decisions. “Please approve” is appropriate only when review criteria and accountability are explicit—not as a way to silence valid safety, factual or legal objections.

## AI and tools governance

The 2023 tool list is a snapshot. Named products, capabilities, prices and policies are volatile. Select tools by job, privacy/security, accessibility, integration, exportability, evidence of performance and total cost—not popularity.

Handley correctly treats automated editing/writing as assistance rather than a human-editor replacement and says creators must retain judgment. The book predates the full operational impact of modern generative AI and underweights:

- hallucinated facts/citations and plausible but false language;
- confidential, personal, client and copyrighted data exposure;
- training/use rights and output ownership/licensing ambiguity;
- homogenized voice and fabricated first-person experience;
- embedded bias, accessibility defects and multilingual quality variation;
- disclosure/provenance expectations and automation scale;
- prompt/model/version reproducibility and vendor change.

Approved workflow: classify data/risk → use approved tool/model → provide minimal authorized context → generate suggestions/draft → verify every material claim/source/quote → human edit and domain review → record material assistance/provenance → final accountable approval. AI may support ideation, structure, variants, transcription and mechanical review; it cannot supply lived experience, authority, consent or truth.

## Evidence and limitations

- The advice is primarily craft knowledge: practitioner experience, examples, interviews, selected research and cited expert commentary.
- Strongest for establishing repeatable writing/editorial behaviors and a reader-centered quality culture.
- Individual conversion lifts, email ROI, image/share relationships and campaign results are examples or secondary findings; they do not establish universal causal effects.
- “Pathological empathy,” customer-as-hero and emotion can become manipulative or reductive without direct research, representation and autonomy safeguards.
- Brand case studies are selected successes and may omit base rates, counterexamples, costs and longer-term effects.
- Legal discussion is U.S.-centered, simplified and explicitly non-advice; laws, licences and platform agreements change.
- Some platform observations are already dated (Twitter naming/features, social algorithms, LinkedIn newsletters, hashtag behavior, SEO tags/tools).
- Writing conventions are contextual. Clarity and reader value are stronger defaults than any individual rule.
- Evidence grade: high practitioner value for process, editing, voice and publishing mindset; moderate for format heuristics; low for universal effects, fixed metrics and current platform/legal/tool instructions.
