# Remote, Not Distant — Gustavo Razzetti

## Bibliographic record

- *Remote, Not Distant: Design a Company Culture That Will Help You Thrive in a Hybrid Workplace*; Gustavo Razzetti; Liberationist Press; first edition, 2022.
- ISBN 978-0-9990973-8-0 (hardback), 978-0-9990973-9-7 (paperback), 978-0-9990973-6-6 (ebook; source front matter contains a typographical omission).
- One 288-page English PDF; SHA-256 `8667b85f68935668d1624eb152b03986923f5d30b42279bd8b29f9c385807b10`; about 53,832 extracted words; no OCR required.
- Complete coverage: introduction, foundation, all five roadmap steps, exercises/canvases, leading-hybrid-team synthesis, afterword, acknowledgments, notes and index material.
- Authority: a research-informed practitioner guide based on the author's consulting/facilitation experience, interviews with hundreds of practitioners/experts, organizational examples and a mixed bibliography of academic, consultancy, vendor, survey, media and popular sources. Useful for design prompts and operating choices; it does not establish comparative causal effects for the roadmap or determine the best work model for a given organization.

## Bottom line

Hybrid work is not merely a location policy; it requires redesigning culture and work. The source organizes that redesign into five moves: intentionally reset culture, co-create purpose and priorities, strengthen belonging and psychological safety, choose among collaboration modes with an asynchronous default, and release agility through flexible norms and distributed decisions.

VladOS retains this systems approach while rejecting any universal “future of work.” Work design must fit tasks, employees, customers, law, safety, accessibility, security, labor relations and geography. Remote-first is not intrinsically superior; offices are neither necessary nor inherently harmful. The target is **location-equitable, outcome-capable and sustainable work**, including dignified conditions for people whose roles must remain onsite.

## Culture system

The source's Culture Design Canvas contains three layers:

- **Core:** purpose, values, priorities, rewarded and prohibited behavior.
- **Emotional culture:** psychological safety, feedback and rituals.
- **Functional culture:** decisions, meetings, norms and rules.

Use a fourth layer: **employment and technical infrastructure**—contracts, pay, working time, health and safety, accessibility, equipment, expenses, security, data protection, employee representation, cross-border compliance and physical/digital workplace services. Culture cannot compensate for a broken foundation.

### Governed culture brief

| Layer | Required decisions |
| --- | --- |
| Legitimate purpose | stakeholder value, strategy, work reality and non-negotiable duties |
| Employee conditions | pay, time, safety, access, equipment, privacy and support |
| Behavioral system | observable principles, decision rights, feedback and consequences |
| Connection | belonging options, collaboration, rituals and conflict routes |
| Work architecture | location, time, async/sync modes, meetings, documentation and tools |
| Evidence | delivery, quality, equity, wellbeing, risk and employee voice |
| Governance | owners, consultation, appeals, review cadence and change control |

Culture is a continuous hypothesis: `If we design these conditions and behaviors for this population and work, we expect these outcomes without exceeding these guardrails.` Test with disaggregated evidence and preserve dissent.

## Five-step roadmap, governed

### 1. Reset culture

The source proposes five mindset shifts:

1. culture by chance → culture by design;
2. input/visibility → impact;
3. work–life imbalance/separation → integration;
4. synchronous → asynchronous collaboration;
5. one-size-fits-all → flexibility.

VladOS calibrates each:

- **Design, not control:** co-create what can be designed and monitor emergent practice. Leaders cannot dictate feelings or belonging.
- **Outcomes, not digital presenteeism:** define results, quality and constraints; still record time where law, pay, fatigue or capacity requires it. Outcomes outside a person's control need contextual evaluation.
- **Work–life agency, not boundary collapse:** employees choose integration or separation. Establish right-to-disconnect norms, quiet hours, leave and coverage.
- **Async by default, sync by reason:** choose the least burdensome mode that preserves understanding, participation, speed and human connection.
- **Equitable flexibility, not arbitrary exceptions:** publish eligibility criteria, fund access, audit outcomes and offer alternatives for onsite or constrained roles.

### 2. Reimagine a shared future

The book uses team purpose and values to guide priorities. A governed team-purpose contract records:

- stakeholders and needs served;
- distinctive contribution and evidence;
- outcomes and quality/ethical boundaries;
- top priorities and explicit non-priorities;
- observable behaviors encouraged and prohibited;
- tensions/trade-offs and decision principles;
- employee input, dissent and review date.

Participation should shape the purpose, not merely manufacture buy-in. Employees may comply professionally without identifying personally with an organizational mission. Values must be behaviorally specific, applied to executives and compatible with pluralism, collective rights and legitimate challenge.

### 3. Reignite belonging

The source's Psychological Safety Ladder has three proposed levels:

1. **Welcome:** recognition, help, interpersonal connection and ability to raise wellbeing needs.
2. **Courageous conversations:** valued contribution, questions, difficult issues and disagreement.
3. **Innovation:** challenge to the status quo, learning from mistakes and bounded experimentation.

Treat this as a discussion map, not a validated scale or guaranteed causal ladder. Add **Level 0: basic dignity and protection**—non-discrimination, accessibility, lawful treatment, physical/digital safety, fair process and non-retaliation.

Measure safety through multiple channels: confidential survey, interviews/focus groups with safeguards, speak-up and retaliation patterns, meeting participation, incident handling, subgroup experience, idea/error reporting and closure. High idea volume is not proof of safety, and silence is not consent.

Belonging is voluntary connection plus fair membership. Do not require cameras, home tours, personal histories, vulnerability exercises, health disclosure, social calls or “bring your whole self.” Provide multiple participation modes, accessible timing, captions/transcripts where appropriate, inclusive facilitation and a private route for sensitive matters.

### 4. Rethink collaboration

The source uses two axes—individual versus shared time and deep versus casual work—to distinguish six modes:

| Mode | Purpose | Default design | Key boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Focus work | analysis, creation, research, planning | protected individual blocks; async inputs | not possible for every frontline/service role; avoid concentration privilege |
| Deep collaboration | complex co-creation or decision | bounded, prepared synchronous session; in-person only when value exceeds burden | accessibility, travel, time-zone and fatigue controls |
| Regular collaboration | coordination, updates and responses | documented async workflow plus limited collaboration windows | prevent channel sprawl and response anxiety |
| Learning | acquire/practice/transfer capability | blend self-paced and social practice | paid time, access, feedback and transfer evidence |
| Casual collaboration | optional relationship building and weak-tie exchange | inclusive opt-in formats, varied times | never promotion-critical; avoid gossip and forced fun |
| Unplugged | rest, reflection and recovery | protected disconnection and workload reduction | recovery cannot repair structurally excessive demand |

#### Async–sync router

Use asynchronous work when information can be documented, response need is not immediate, participants need reflection/time-zone flexibility, or a durable record adds value. Use synchronous work when the issue is urgent, highly ambiguous, emotionally sensitive, dependent on rapid interaction, a practice/co-creation event, or materially cheaper to resolve together.

Before scheduling, ask:

1. What outcome requires interaction?
2. Can a decision memo, update, recording or comments achieve it?
3. Who must contribute, decide, know or merely access later?
4. What context must be sent in advance?
5. Which accessibility, language, time-zone, caregiving and technical needs apply?
6. What artifact and owner close the loop?

Async requires documentation labor. Allocate and credit it; do not make distributed workers produce perfect records while colocated colleagues decide informally. Set channel purposes, response expectations, urgency routes, retention/search conventions and “no response needed” markers. Documentation must follow confidentiality, minimization and security rules.

### 5. Release agility

The source describes five models:

1. remote-friendly/office-first;
2. fixed hybrid/buckets;
3. partly remote/collaboration days;
4. flexible hybrid/flexible schedule;
5. remote-first/virtual-first.

Select using a **work-model decision record** rather than ideology:

| Dimension | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Work | Which tasks require equipment, presence, privacy, synchronous coordination or local service? |
| People | What preferences, disabilities, caregiving, housing, commute and safety realities exist? |
| Equity | Who receives flexibility, visibility, pay, opportunity and burden? What alternative value reaches onsite roles? |
| Stakeholders | What customer/community outcomes and coverage are required? |
| Risk | Employment, working-time, tax, immigration, PE, insurance, H&S, security, privacy and IP implications? |
| Infrastructure | Equipment, home-office support, space, accessibility, connectivity and technical support? |
| Economics/environment | Total costs, travel, real estate, emissions and displacement effects? |
| Reversibility | Pilot population, duration, guardrails, rollback and transition support? |

Consult employees and representatives before selection. Avoid individual manager discretion without criteria. Publish why the model exists, which roles/options are eligible, how exceptions work, expense/equipment/pay treatment, working hours, location limits, data practices, safety responsibilities, review and appeal.

## Location-equity standard

Hybrid often privileges people in the room. Apply:

- digital source of truth for agendas, evidence, decisions and actions;
- remote-accessible participation for every mixed-location meeting; consider everyone joining individually when room dynamics cannot be equalized;
- no consequential “meeting after the meeting”; capture new facts and reopen appropriately;
- rotate inconvenient time zones and track burden;
- evaluate outcomes with calibrated evidence, not presence, responsiveness or executive familiarity;
- equal access to sponsorship, information, development, high-visibility work and promotion;
- accessible equipment, captions, facilitation and async alternatives;
- travel/in-person attendance funded and planned fairly;
- location and schedule choices not disclosed beyond necessity;
- subgroup audits of ratings, pay, promotion, retention, workload and belonging.

Onsite employees deserve schedule agency, predictable shifts, safe staffing, paid learning, quality facilities and equivalent investment. Remote flexibility for some must not transfer undesirable coverage or invisible coordination to others without recognition and compensation.

## Norms and communication protocol

A hybrid team agreement should specify:

- working windows and time-zone display;
- protected focus and disconnection periods;
- collaboration hours, coverage and handoffs;
- channel map: urgent, action required, discussion, FYI, social and confidential;
- expected—not continuously monitored—response ranges;
- emergency definition, escalation tree and aftercare;
- meeting threshold, preparation, accessibility, recording/notes and decision rules;
- documentation owner, location, version, retention and security classification;
- camera/recording consent and background/privacy norms;
- leave, sick time, workload escalation and substitutions;
- conflict, feedback, accommodation and protected-reporting routes.

“Assume good intent” can reduce needless escalation but must not suppress evidence of harm. Use: **start with curiosity, evaluate impact, and preserve accountability**. Good intent does not negate discriminatory, unsafe or repeated conduct.

## Decision system

The source catalogs autocratic, delegated, democratic, consensus, consultative, consent and intentional-delay/avoidance methods. Route decisions by consequence:

| Context | Suitable pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate contained emergency | authorized rapid decision, logged and reviewed afterward |
| Expert/local operational issue | delegation/DRI within clear constraints |
| Broad preference, low consequence | democratic choice with participation safeguards |
| Material leader-owned decision needing expertise | consultative decision with published rationale |
| Reversible team policy | consent/objection processing or bounded pilot |
| Rights, safety, regulated or employment matter | formal authority plus qualified review, consultation and appeal; not majority vote |
| Insufficient evidence and low delay cost | explicit deferral with owner, information need and decision date |

For material choices, create a decision page: question; owner and method; context/evidence available; consulted and affected groups; options/criteria; decision/rationale; dissent; actions; risks/controls; date; review trigger; current status. Transparency is access to legitimate context, not indiscriminate exposure of personal or confidential data.

## Meetings and rituals

### Meeting gate

A meeting needs an interaction-dependent purpose, decision/work product, facilitator/owner, essential participants, accessible prework, timebox and closure artifact. Convert status sharing to async. Cancel recurring meetings that no longer create value. Preserve synchronous space for ambiguity, learning, conflict, relationships and complex joint work where it materially helps.

### Ritual design

The book describes rituals as repeated symbolic experiences with a trigger, beginning/middle/end, behavior change, frequency and shared meaning. Govern them with:

- legitimate purpose and target behavior;
- voluntary or role-required status made explicit;
- accessibility, cultural/religious fit and privacy;
- inclusion of remote, onsite, time-zone and language groups;
- no compelled emotion, confession, touch, alcohol or home exposure;
- workload/time cost and facilitator;
- evidence of value and retirement date.

Rituals can support transitions, recognition, learning, connection and closure. They cannot create belonging if pay, power, workload or discrimination remains unaddressed.

## Remote leadership

The source calls for trust, human leadership, culture stewardship, questions, consistency, transparency, respect and flexibility. Convert this into reciprocal commitments:

### Leaders provide

- clear outcomes, priorities, constraints and decisions;
- autonomy within role and risk limits;
- equipment, information, capacity, access and support;
- visibility and opportunity independent of location;
- privacy, non-retaliation and fair process;
- workload protection and modeled disconnection;
- honest uncertainty and closed feedback loops.

### Team members provide

- agreed outcomes and quality;
- timely handoffs and risk escalation;
- documentation proportionate to collaboration needs;
- coverage within contracted expectations;
- security/privacy compliance;
- constructive participation and respect;
- early communication when commitments need renegotiation.

Trust is not abandonment. Managers still coach, notice exclusion, address conflict, manage capacity and verify high-risk controls. Monitoring must be necessary, proportionate, disclosed, lawful and focused on system/security needs—not keystrokes, webcam presence or performative availability.

## Wellbeing and safety

- Assess workload, meeting load, interruption, overtime, isolation and time-zone burden rather than treating burnout as an individual resilience gap.
- Define disconnect rights and manager escalation outside ordinary hours.
- Provide ergonomic/safety guidance, equipment and legally required assessment/support.
- Preserve confidential routes for health, disability, domestic safety and accommodation.
- Do not assume home is safe, quiet, private or adequately connected; maintain alternatives.
- Design social connection without requiring it and offer professional support/referral where appropriate.
- Track leave uptake and chronic work extension; “unlimited” leave needs minimum-use and coverage safeguards.
- Use in-person contact when it serves an identified need, not as a loyalty test.

## Implementation cycle

The source's Define–Design–Demonstrate–Demand model becomes:

1. **Diagnose:** task/location analysis, employee/representative research, baseline outcomes, risks and subgroup experience.
2. **Define:** work model, purpose, eligibility, constraints, rights, success measures and guardrails.
3. **Co-design:** collaboration modes, norms, tools, meetings, decisions, connection and support.
4. **Enable:** equipment, training, documentation, security, accessibility, management capacity and transition help.
5. **Model:** leaders follow location, response, meeting, leave and documentation norms.
6. **Contract:** organization and team make reciprocal commitments; publish exceptions and appeals.
7. **Pilot:** bounded duration/population with service, equity, wellbeing and risk guardrails.
8. **Evaluate:** delivery, quality, employee experience, subgroup equity, costs, incidents and unintended work transfer.
9. **Adapt:** start/stop/continue, repair harms, version the agreement and set next review.

## Evidence and currency review

The book contains many references but mixes peer-reviewed research with corporate surveys, consulting reports, blogs, journalistic coverage, quotes and selected company cases. It was completed in March 2022 during exceptional pandemic conditions. Claims about employee preferences, productivity, real estate, technology-company policies, public health, labor markets and “the future” are temporally unstable.

Use current authoritative sources before policy decisions, especially for:

- employment, working-time, health-and-safety and right-to-disconnect law;
- cross-border tax, permanent establishment, payroll, social security and immigration;
- privacy, monitoring, cybersecurity and data residency;
- current organizational policies and vendor/platform capabilities;
- population preferences and productivity/retention estimates.

Source-supported as design hypotheses: intentional work-model choices; explicit norms and response expectations; documenting distributed decisions; matching collaboration mode to task; protecting focus/recovery; and auditing proximity bias.

Not established by this source: that remote/hybrid causes better culture or performance; that flexible hybrid/remote-first is generally optimal; that async work is more inclusive for every population; that the five-step roadmap improves outcomes; that three psychological-safety levels form a validated progression; or that featured companies succeeded because of the cited practice.

## Safe claim language

| Avoid | Use instead |
| --- | --- |
| The hybrid workplace is here to stay | Hybrid work was widespread and strategically salient in 2022; current applicability depends on sector, labor market and policy |
| You do not need an office to build culture | Strong culture can exist without a shared office, but work, people and infrastructure determine viable arrangements |
| Default to async | Prefer async when it reduces coordination burden without sacrificing speed, understanding, access or connection |
| Focus on impact, not input | Prioritize outcomes and quality while retaining lawful time, workload and process controls |
| Flexibility creates accountability | Well-designed flexibility can support agency; accountability also requires clarity, capacity, evidence and fair management |
| Remote work levels the playing field | Distributed formats can expand access but also create proximity, technology, home-environment and time-zone inequities |
| Culture drives business performance | Culture and outcomes are associated in some studies; direction and causality are context-dependent |
