# The Learning and Development Handbook

## Bibliographic record

- Michelle Parry-Slater, *The Learning and Development Handbook: A Learning Practitioner's Guide*.
- Kogan Page, 2021. Hardback ISBN 978-1-78966-334-1; paperback ISBN 978-1-78966-332-7; ebook ISBN 978-1-78966-333-4.
- Coverage: foreword, preface, all three parts and 14 chapters, afterword, cases, chapter reading lists and index.
- Authority: wide-ranging professional handbook based on practitioner experience, curated models, case contributions and selected research. It is neither a systematic evidence synthesis nor a formal professional standard.
- Source caveat: the supplied 570-page PDF was generated with Calibre in 2021. Its text layer is usable and the internal publisher record confirms the first edition.

## Bottom line

Modern workplace L&D is not a course-production function. It is a performance and capability partner that understands organizational strategy, consults before prescribing, builds stakeholder trust, connects people and knowledge, makes learning available in the flow of work, and measures useful change. Formal education remains one tool within a wider system of information, communication, resources, social learning, practice and environmental support.

The book's distinctive contribution is an operating architecture built from three practitioner frameworks:

- **ICE:** route a need among Information, Communication and Education.
- **EPC:** establish Environment, Permission and Culture for learning.
- **3Rs:** organize Required, Resourced and Referred learning, with Reflection as a useful extension.

These are sense-making tools, not evidence-validated laws. Their value comes from forcing broader choices and exposing the operating conditions that make an intervention usable.

## Modern L&D operating model

The L&D function has six connected jobs:

1. **Sense:** understand strategy, work, people, context and emerging capability needs.
2. **Diagnose:** determine the performance gap and whether learning is part of the response.
3. **Route:** select information, communication, education, environmental change, resources, referral or combinations.
4. **Enable:** connect people, tools, practice and knowledge at appropriate moments.
5. **Steward:** maintain quality, access, governance, communities, platforms and supplier relationships.
6. **Evaluate and adapt:** assess capability, transfer, organizational contribution and unintended effects.

The model shifts L&D from owning every learning interaction to creating conditions in which people, managers, experts and systems can support responsible learning. Decentralization does not remove accountability for accuracy, access or harm.

## Strategy-to-capability trace

Start with the organizational strategy and interrogate it rather than merely repeating its slogans:

- What outcomes and value are being pursued?
- Which work, decisions and capabilities make those outcomes more likely?
- Which populations need to begin, stop, improve or sustain what behaviors?
- What evidence shows a current or future gap?
- Which causes belong to tools, process, staffing, management, incentives, communication or skill?
- What can L&D influence, and what requires another owner?
- Which leading and lagging measures could reveal progress or harm?

Maintain a trace from strategic priority → capability → observable performance → diagnosed conditions → intervention portfolio → evidence. Strategy changes and local realities require periodic review. Avoid positioning every executive priority as a training need.

## Stakeholder relationship system

Map stakeholders by interest, influence, impact, expertise and implementation responsibility. Include executives, sponsors, managers, target performers, employee representatives, IT/data/security, communications, subject experts, accessibility support and external partners where relevant.

Use recurring, purposeful conversations rather than contacting people only for approval. Clarify:

- problem ownership and decision rights;
- what success and failure mean to each group;
- contributions, dependencies and capacity;
- evidence and assumptions;
- confidentiality and how learner feedback will be used;
- conflicts among business, learner and public interests;
- escalation and review points.

The book's informal “strategic latte” and “cake theory” emphasize relationship-building through ordinary human contact. Retain the principle, not the literal social ritual: participation must not depend on alcohol, food, unpaid social time, extroversion or cultural conformity.

## Consultative L&D protocol

Act as a critical friend: supportive of the sponsor's purpose while willing to test the proposed solution. Ask:

1. What happened that caused this request now?
2. What is observed, by whom, and with what evidence?
3. What should people do differently in the actual workflow?
4. Why is that not happening today?
5. What environmental, communication, knowledge, skill or motivation factors contribute?
6. What tools, resources, relationships and informal practices already help?
7. What would change if no formal training were created?
8. What is the smallest safe experiment or support that could test the diagnosis?
9. How will we know whether capability and performance changed?

Do not let “consultative” become unlicensed organizational diagnosis, therapy or executive authority. Document recommendations, owners and uncertainty.

## ICE routing framework

| Route | Primary job | Examples | Governance question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Information | Make accurate material findable at the point of need | policy, procedure, checklist, directory, reference | Does it need recall, or reliable access? Who maintains it? |
| Communication | Create shared awareness, meaning, timing or coordination | change notice, expectation, campaign, manager conversation | Is the message truthful, actionable and received through a credible channel? |
| Education | Develop knowledge, judgment or skill through experience and practice | guided learning, rehearsal, feedback, coaching | Does performance require practice, and how will transfer be supported? |

Many needs span routes. A new procedure may require a clear announcement, a searchable aid and practice for difficult judgment calls. Assign ownership across L&D, communications, operations and subject authorities. Avoid calling a message “learning” merely to improve completion tracking.

## EPC conditions framework

### Environment

Audit physical and digital space, time, access, tools, workload, information architecture, manager behavior and psychological safety. A learning platform is not an environment strategy if people cannot find, use or apply what it hosts.

### Permission

People need explicit and enacted permission to spend time learning, ask questions, experiment safely, share incomplete thinking and seek help. Manager behavior and workload must support the invitation. Permission is not consent to surveillance or compulsory disclosure.

### Culture

Examine the norms and consequences that define “how things are done”: whether expertise is hoarded, mistakes are punished, reflection is dismissed, development is equitable, and leaders model learning. Culture is not a learner deficit. Change requires aligned systems and leadership behavior, not inspirational messaging alone.

Use EPC before launch and during evaluation. If any condition is materially absent, assign remediation or reduce the claims made for the intervention.

## 3Rs portfolio framework

### Required

Reserve mandatory learning for legal, safety, regulatory, role-essential or explicitly governed organizational requirements. Define who must complete what, why, to which standard and when. Strip content to the minimum and distinguish attendance from competence.

### Resourced

Provide maintained resources people can pull when relevant: job aids, guides, examples, search, videos, FAQs, tools and practice. Make ownership, provenance, review dates, accessibility and retirement visible. A large content library without findability or curation is not support.

### Referred

Connect people to credible experts, peers, communities, mentors, external learning and specialist services. Explain why a referral is appropriate and protect choice, confidentiality and professional boundaries. Do not imply endorsement without due diligence.

### Reflection

Reflection can connect experience to improvement across all three routes. Add prompts, evidence and a next action; otherwise reflection can become pleasant but operationally inert.

## Learning-portfolio decision record

For each proposed asset or experience, record:

- target performance and audience;
- ICE route(s) and 3Rs position;
- evidence of need;
- point and frequency of use;
- EPC preconditions;
- owner, source authority and maintenance date;
- access, privacy, language and disability requirements;
- evaluation level and stop/revise rule.

This prevents duplicated content, mandatory-course inflation and orphaned resources. One intervention can occupy several cells, but each role must remain clear.

## Social learning and community governance

People learn by observing, asking, discussing, demonstrating and solving problems together. Enable expert directories, peer sessions, mentoring, communities of practice, discussion spaces and curated contributions when they fit the work.

Apply safeguards:

- articulate community purpose and membership boundaries;
- distinguish verified guidance from opinion;
- obtain consent before recording or repurposing contributions;
- credit contributors and avoid uncompensated extraction of tacit knowledge;
- support asynchronous, accessible and low-exposure participation;
- moderate harassment, misinformation and confidential-data leakage;
- define escalation to formal subject, clinical, legal or safety authority;
- measure contribution and problem solving, not posting volume.

Informal learning is not automatically accurate or inclusive. L&D's role can include curation, connection and quality infrastructure without dominating the community.

## Curation and knowledge-management standard

Curation is selection plus context, not link accumulation. A curated item needs audience, purpose, source authority, summary, limitations, date, recommended use and relationship to other material. Deduplicate versions, preserve provenance and retire stale guidance.

Capture tacit knowledge through interviews, demonstrations, paired work and after-action reflection, but do not assume all expertise can be documented. Some judgment requires practice, supervision and community access. Knowledge management and learning design should share taxonomy, search and maintenance rules.

## Digital-learning business case

Choose technology only after the performance and user need are clear. Evaluate:

- use case and workflow fit;
- target population's devices, bandwidth, language and access;
- ability to practice, collaborate, retrieve and receive feedback;
- security, privacy, accessibility and data portability;
- integration, administration, support and vendor viability;
- content ownership, analytics limits and exit costs;
- realistic adoption and maintenance capacity.

“Digital” does not equal modern, scalable or self-directed. Start with available tools when suitable, prototype the experience and verify behavior. Platform recommendations from 2021 require current review.

## Coherent blend architecture

A blend is a purposeful ecosystem, not the same information duplicated across classroom, webinar and e-learning. Design from the “whole 100” of how people learn at work: formal learning, guided practice, work experience, people, reflection, resources and systems.

Route each component by its job:

- asynchronous material for flexible orientation or reference;
- live time for practice, discussion, feedback and collective decisions;
- job aids for point-of-performance accuracy;
- peer/manager activity for modeling and transfer;
- spaced retrieval and reflection for durability;
- community and expert access for evolving judgment.

Give the overall offer a recognizable, truthful identity so people can find and understand it. Branding must not conceal requirements, effort, evidence limits or commercial intent.

## Data, evaluation and value chain

Begin evaluation during diagnosis. Define a value chain:

```text
need and conditions
  → participation/access
    → experience and capability
      → application/transfer
        → performance indicators
          → organizational outcomes
```

For each link, specify data, timing, owner, comparison and alternative explanations. Use quantitative and qualitative evidence; stories can explain mechanisms and lived effects but should not replace denominators or counterevidence. Do not claim ROI or causal impact without appropriate cost and attribution analysis.

Ask better evaluation questions: what can people do, what support helped, what blocked application, what changed after delay, who did not benefit, and what unintended effects occurred? Completion and satisfaction are operational signals, not proof of value.

## Evolution–revolution change router

Choose modernization pace from context:

| Condition | Evolution may fit | Revolution may fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risk/urgency | Low immediate harm; safe incremental testing | Existing model is materially failing or unsafe |
| Trust/readiness | Stakeholders need evidence and experience | Strong coalition and clear mandate exist |
| Infrastructure | Dependencies require staged change | Replacement can be supported end to end |
| Evidence | Direction is promising but uncertain | Problem and alternative are sufficiently validated |

In either mode, protect continuity, test assumptions, communicate honestly and define rollback or adaptation. “Move fast” is not permission to bypass safety, accessibility, labor consultation or governance.

## Reflective-practice loop

Use individual or group reflection tied to evidence:

1. What happened, and what evidence do we have?
2. What did we expect and assume?
3. What helped or obstructed performance?
4. Whose perspective is missing?
5. What did we learn about the system and ourselves?
6. What will we continue, stop, change or test?
7. Who owns the next action, by when?

Personal learning networks can broaden reflection but are not quality assurance. Protect confidential information and seek appropriately qualified review for high-stakes topics.

## Evidence, scope and ethics limitations

- Most prescriptions are practitioner synthesis and cases, not comparative evidence for the complete operating model.
- ICE, EPC and 3Rs are proprietary practitioner frameworks whose usefulness should be tested in context.
- “Modern” can become a rhetorical label that undervalues effective formal instruction or oversells digital/social formats.
- Adult-learning and neuroscience claims cited indirectly require source-level verification before strong public use.
- Social learning can reproduce hierarchy, misinformation, exclusion and unpaid knowledge labor.
- Self-directed learning assumes time, confidence, access and organizational permission that are distributed unequally.
- Business alignment does not override employee autonomy, safety, privacy, labor rights or public interest.
- Technology and platform examples date rapidly and require current due diligence.
- Organizational culture cannot be transformed by L&D assets alone.
