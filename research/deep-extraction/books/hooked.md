# Hooked — Nir Eyal with Ryan Hoover

## Bibliographic record

- *Hooked: How to Build Habit-Forming Products*; Nir Eyal with Ryan Hoover; first edition; 2014.
- ISBN 1494277530; ISBN-13 978-1494277536.
- One 188-page English PDF; SHA-256 `a4ed643a0fa0181d0c4334cce060ac02ae28bfec625280a2ae7a8af3a619ca94`; about 44,696 extracted words; no OCR required.
- Complete coverage: introduction, eight chapters, Bible App case study, appendix, notes, acknowledgments and author material.
- Authority: a commercial practitioner synthesis of behavior design and product-growth practice. It combines selected psychology studies, interviews, company examples and author interpretation. The four-stage model is a design heuristic, not a clinically validated theory or proof that repeated use benefits users. Company cases, adoption figures and product details reflect roughly 2010–2014 conditions.

## Bottom line

The book explains recurring product use as a loop:

```text
trigger → easy action → variable reward → user investment → next trigger
```

The model is useful for diagnosing why a behavior starts and repeats. Its original commercial objective—creating unprompted engagement and making switching difficult—is not adopted as a VladOS objective. Frequency, engagement and retention are behaviors, not evidence of value.

VladOS converts the Hook Model into an **agency-preserving supportive-use loop**. It may help people establish a routine they deliberately chose, but only where benefit is plausible, prompts are consensual, rewards do not cultivate compulsion, stored value remains portable, and stopping is easy. It must not engineer automaticity around high-impact decisions or exploit distress, loneliness, fear, social rejection, sunk cost or intermittent reinforcement.

## Four phenomena that must not be conflated

| Phenomenon | Meaning | Design stance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| user-beneficial routine | repeated behavior advances a goal the person chose | may support with safeguards |
| neutral convenience | repetition saves effort without material benefit or harm | optimize proportionately |
| commercially induced recurrence | product use repeats because the business engineered return | never treat recurrence alone as success |
| harmful compulsion/dependency | impaired control or persistence despite harm | detect, interrupt, support and never optimize |

Habit is not engagement; engagement is not retention; retention is not wellbeing; wellbeing is not goal attainment. Measure each separately.

## The agency-preserving supportive-use loop

### Gate 0 — Is automaticity appropriate?

Before using the model, record:

- the user-declared outcome and how repetition plausibly serves it;
- who benefits financially or institutionally from greater frequency;
- foreseeable harms, displacement and vulnerable groups;
- the minimum sufficient cadence and a natural completion point;
- whether the action requires fresh deliberation or informed consent;
- opt-out, pause, export, deletion, cancellation and redress routes; and
- an owner, review date and stop conditions.

Do not design automatic behavior for financial spending, gambling, drug use, sexual content, medical consent or self-treatment, civic/political persuasion, high-impact employment or legal choices, crisis interactions, or minors. Clinician-supported adherence may use reminders only under appropriate professional, consent and safety controls.

### 1 — Trigger with integrity

The source distinguishes external prompts from learned internal cues. External prompts include advertising, publicity, interpersonal sharing and channels the user permits; internal cues include routines, contexts, thoughts and emotions. Negative emotions such as boredom, loneliness, frustration, confusion and fear are presented as particularly powerful cues.

Governed trigger standard:

1. Tie the prompt to a goal the user explicitly selected.
2. State why it appears and what will happen if acted upon.
3. Obtain granular, revocable permission for channel, type, timing and frequency.
4. Default to quiet periods, batching and frequency caps.
5. Let users snooze, disable and inspect prompt logic without penalty.
6. Never infer emotional vulnerability in order to time persuasion.
7. Escalate credible crisis or clinical risk to appropriate support rather than engagement mechanics.

An install, email address, contact permission or previous click is not blanket consent to escalating prompts. Relationship triggers must never use forced invitations, contact scraping, guilt or reputational pressure. Correlation between distress and internet use does not establish that a product relieves the distress.

### 2 — Make the chosen action possible

The book uses B=MAT: behavior occurs when sufficient motivation and ability coincide with a trigger. It groups motivation around pleasure/pain, hope/fear and social acceptance/rejection, and simplicity around scarce resources such as time, money, physical effort, cognitive effort, social acceptability and routine familiarity.

Adopt:

- remove irrelevant steps, jargon and accessibility barriers;
- design around the user's currently scarce resource;
- test comprehension and task success, not clicks alone;
- make the next action concrete, small and reversible; and
- simplify only after confirming that the action serves the chosen outcome.

Preserve **beneficial friction** for consequential actions: cost and term disclosure, confirmation, cooling-off periods, spending/time controls, cancellation parity, undo, reporting and human review. Ease must not erase meaningful consent. Fear, pain and social rejection are not acceptable motivational levers. A phrase affirming freedom is not genuine choice when refusal carries hidden cost or pressure.

### 3 — Provide feedback that permits satiation

The source classifies variable rewards as:

- **tribe:** connection, recognition and social belonging;
- **hunt:** information or material-resource discovery; and
- **self:** competence, mastery and completion.

Variability can sustain curiosity where outcomes are naturally uncertain. It is not permission to leave people deliberately unsatisfied. VladOS replaces “fulfilled yet wanting more” with **useful feedback plus a stopping cue**.

Requirements:

- the feedback must relate to the declared benefit;
- variability must be inherent or meaningfully useful, transparent and bounded;
- completion, satiation and “you are done” states must remain visible;
- predictable feedback is the default for health, education and safety;
- social metrics must not make unpredictable approval the primary reward; and
- monetized chance, loot-box dynamics, infinite scroll, artificial scarcity and intermittent alerts require prohibition or specialist governance, especially for minors and vulnerable users.

Neuroscience language about dopamine, anticipation and reward is simplified in the source. Do not reuse it as a medical or causal claim without an updated primary-literature review.

### 4 — Invite investment without lock-in

The book distinguishes immediate action from investment made for later value. It identifies content, data, followers, reputation and learned skill as stored value and shows how a contribution can create the next notification. It also invokes the IKEA effect, commitment/consistency and cognitive dissonance to explain why effort increases attachment.

Ethical investment test:

| Question | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| user value | contribution improves a function or asset the user wants |
| proportionality | work and data requested are the minimum necessary |
| ownership | user can inspect, correct, export and delete contributions |
| portability | content, reputation and useful history are not intentionally trapped |
| privacy | purpose, access, retention and security are clear |
| reciprocity | value exchange is visible; unpaid labor is not disguised |
| next trigger | any future prompt is expected and separately controllable |
| exit | cancellation and departure do not punish prior investment |

Do not exploit sunk cost, identity commitment or consistency pressure. Do not require contact uploads, invitations, public posting or extensive profile data by default. Skill-specific lock-in should be reduced through conventional formats, documentation and transferable workflows. A service becoming “harder to leave” is a risk signal, not a product virtue.

## An accountable influence test

The source's Manipulation Matrix asks whether the maker would use the product and believes it materially improves users' lives. It labels makers facilitators, peddlers, entertainers or dealers. This can prompt reflection but cannot establish legitimacy: makers may differ from users, sincerely hold false beliefs, underestimate dependency, externalize harms or profit from the behavior they assess.

Replace it with this review:

1. **Purpose:** What user-defined benefit is intended, and is repeated use necessary?
2. **Evidence:** What supports the causal pathway and what would disconfirm it?
3. **Autonomy:** Can a person understand, refuse, pause and leave without penalty?
4. **Harm:** What are foreseeable individual, relational and societal costs?
5. **Vulnerability:** How do outcomes differ for minors, distressed users and people with impaired control?
6. **Incentives:** Does revenue increase when users exceed the beneficial dose?
7. **Transparency:** Are persuasive mechanics and personalization understandable?
8. **Data rights:** Are collection, inference, portability, deletion and retention governed?
9. **Monitoring:** Which leading indicators reveal compulsion, regret or displacement?
10. **Redress:** Who acts, what is stopped, and how can users obtain help or remedy?

Responsibility cannot be shifted to users merely because severe dependency affects a minority. Product telemetry can help detect excessive patterns, but sensitive inferences require privacy controls and must not become new targeting inputs.

## Benefit Testing: a governed replacement for Habit Testing

The source proposes three continual steps: identify habitual users, codify their shared “Habit Path,” and modify the product so more users follow it. Its five-percent threshold is an author rule of thumb, not a validated universal benchmark. A path associated with retention may be a marker of selection, prior motivation, network effects or coercive friction rather than its cause.

Use this sequence instead:

1. **Define:** specify the beneficial outcome, sufficient cadence, time horizon, affected groups and harm limits before examining retention.
2. **Identify:** use cohorts to find patterns of both benefit and possible overuse; include non-users, leavers and low-frequency successful users.
3. **Codify:** map candidate pathways, confounders, failure paths and distributional differences.
4. **Test:** run bounded, consent-appropriate experiments or quasi-experimental analyses where feasible; do not infer causation from a retained-user path.
5. **Modify:** reduce obstacles to benefit while preserving choice, stopping cues and exit.
6. **Monitor:** compare outcome, autonomy and harm measures over time and stop when thresholds fail.

### Minimum measurement set

| Dimension | Example measure |
| --- | --- |
| user outcome | declared goal attainment, task quality, durable capability |
| sufficient use | proportion achieving benefit without excess frequency |
| autonomy | opt-out success, cancellation time, notification control use |
| experience | comprehension, satisfaction, regret and perceived pressure |
| displacement | sleep, work, relationship or financial costs where relevant |
| compulsion signal | repeated failed limits, rapid re-entry, escalating sessions |
| equity | benefit and harm by relevant user group |
| exit/data rights | export/deletion success and unresolved complaints |

Activation, daily active use, streaks and retention are diagnostic metrics only. They cannot be primary benefit metrics.

## Case-study and opportunity boundaries

The YouVersion Bible App chapter maps scripture notifications, easy reading, varied passages/social elements, bookmarks, notes, sharing and reading plans to the four stages. It is a selected founder narrative with reported adoption data, not an evaluation of spiritual, behavioral or wellbeing outcomes. Religious belief and community identity must not be instrumentalized through shame, public commitment or pressure.

The book suggests finding opportunities in personal needs, emerging behaviors, enabling technologies and interface changes. These remain useful discovery lenses if paired with representative research. “Build for yourself” supplies one observation, not inclusive validation. Early adopters can reveal possibilities but also differ systematically from later users. New ease and faster cycles create risk as well as opportunity.

## Evidence and claim discipline

- Treat the Hook Model as a practitioner hypothesis map, not settled behavioral science.
- Trace psychological claims to the cited primary studies before quantitative or health use.
- Reassess replication, construct validity, effect size, population and ecological validity.
- Treat 2010–2014 company metrics and interface examples as historical illustrations.
- Do not generalize one product's frequency threshold, onboarding milestone or retention path.
- Separate association, mechanism hypothesis, experiment and demonstrated user outcome.
- Do not use selected success cases as evidence that the model caused growth.
- Require current legal, platform, privacy and child-safety review for implementation.

## Risk register

| Risk | Typical mechanism | Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| distress exploitation | cueing use at boredom, loneliness, fear or confusion | prohibit vulnerability targeting; support or neutral prompt |
| compulsive consumption | variable rewards, infinite feeds and rapid cycles | bounds, stopping cues, limits and independent harm review |
| social pressure | approval metrics, invitations and fear of rejection | private defaults, invitation consent and non-comparative feedback |
| lock-in | trapped data, reputation, followers or learned workflow | export, interoperability, deletion and cancellation parity |
| sunk-cost extraction | investment followed by upsell or escalation | transparent future cost and penalty-free exit |
| privacy harm | passive behavioral data used to personalize triggers | minimization, purpose limits, access controls and audit |
| metric substitution | retention treated as benefit | outcome, autonomy, regret, displacement and harm measures |
| vulnerable-user harm | mechanics affect minors or impaired-control groups | exclusion, age assurance or strengthened safeguards |
| ethical self-licensing | maker belief used as proof of good | independent evidence, affected-user input and governance |
| overclaiming | simplified neuroscience and selected company stories | claim register and primary-source verification |

## Relationships to other VladOS foundations

- **Nudge:** both concern choice architecture; transparent, easy-to-resist assistance is retained, while covert welfare assumptions and manipulation require governance.
- **Decisive:** use WRAP and disconfirming tests before deciding that habitual use is the right intervention; set harm tripwires before launch.
- **Mindset:** reward learning, strategy and feedback rather than identity, streak preservation or public comparison.
- **Influence/persuasion sources:** disclosed persuasion and genuine refusal replace scarcity, commitment and social proof used as covert compliance levers.
- **Product and content systems:** optimize for completion, capability and user-owned outcomes rather than attention duration or return frequency.
