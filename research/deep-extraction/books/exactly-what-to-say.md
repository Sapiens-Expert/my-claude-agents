- Author: Phil M. Jones.
- Edition/date: Box of Tricks, 2017; produced by Page Two.
- ISBNs: 978-0-692-88195-8 (paperback), 978-0-692-88196-5 (ebook).

- Completeness: opening, all 23 phrase chapters, final thought, acknowledgments and author/promotional matter; about 14,800 extracted words; no OCR required.
- Authority: commercial practitioner phrasebook supported mainly by assertion, anecdote and demonstrations. It contains no bibliography, study methods or empirical claim register.

## Bottom line

- Small wording changes can improve clarity, lower defensiveness and keep a conversation moving, but language does not override another person's agency.
- The book repeatedly frames interaction as control: secure agreement before disclosure, make refusal difficult, exploit identity and indebtedness, prescribe a close, or use silence as pressure.
- Its strongest VladOS value is as a **persuasion-risk catalogue**. Preserve the legitimate conversational purpose of selected phrases, then rebuild them with explicit consent, honest evidence, meaningful options and an easy right to pause or refuse.
- Do not promote “magic words,” subconscious control, guaranteed responses or scripts designed to get one's own way.

| “What's the best number…?” | Obtain contact details | Bypasses permission to contact | “May I follow up? Which channel, if any, do you prefer?” |

## Governed conversation protocol

1. **Permission and context:** identify purpose, role and material interest; ask whether the person wants the conversation.
2. **Diagnose:** ask open, non-leading questions; distinguish facts, interpretations, preferences and constraints.
3. **Explain:** present relevant options, evidence, costs, uncertainty, conflicts and credible alternatives—including no action.
4. **Check understanding:** invite questions and correction; do not treat a nod, address, minor answer or silence as consent.
5. **Invite a decision:** ask what the person prefers. Permit “no,” “not now” and time to consult others.
6. **Confirm scope:** restate the selected action, conditions, data use and next step; obtain the appropriate explicit consent.
7. **Document and follow up:** record commitments accurately; make follow-up frequency/channel revocable.

## Language-integrity gates

Reject or rewrite a script when it:

- presupposes consent, purchase, scheduling, contact permission or disclosure;
- makes disagreement evidence of being closed-minded, lazy, dishonest or unlike a valued group;
- fabricates a binary, norm, scarcity claim, guarantee or causal promise;
- uses fear, imagined loss or anxiety without proportional evidence and relevance;
- withholds the request until after securing a favor or exploits gratitude as debt;
- treats silence, embarrassment, eye contact or a low-stakes response as binding agreement;
- obscures limitations with vague approval such as “it works great” when a factual answer is due;
- persists after a clear no or makes declining socially or professionally costly;
- requests a third party's identity or contact without a lawful, consent-respecting introduction process.

## Evidence and epistemic assessment

- “Subconscious brain,” hardwiring and predictable yes/no outputs are rhetorical explanations, not demonstrated mechanisms in this source.
- Statements such as “almost always,” “unanimous,” “most people” and “highly likely” lack denominators, settings, outcome definitions and comparison groups.
- Examples show how a phrase might operate; they do not establish general effectiveness, durability, customer welfare or comparative performance.
- Author experience and client anecdotes can generate hypotheses but are exposed to selection, confirmation, survivorship and commercial-interest bias.
- Even if a phrase increases immediate compliance, that is not sufficient success. Assess decision quality, informed choice, trust, complaints, reversals, retention and downstream harm.
- Any behavioral-science promotion requires independent sources, contextual replication and explicit uncertainty. Do not inherit the book's scientific-sounding explanations as evidence.

## Safety, privacy and scope

- Do not deploy compliance scripts in health, psychedelics, coaching, legal/tax, financial, employment or other high-stakes decisions. Informed consent, competence, referral and cooling-off requirements prevail.
- Never infer consent from emotional state, politeness, gratitude, silence or prior small agreements.
- Referral workflows should ask the customer to offer an introduction; do not collect or contact a third party without appropriate permission and a valid basis.
- AI must not optimize wording toward individual vulnerabilities, sensitive traits or inferred psychological state. Do not run covert persuasion experiments.
- Record calls or analyze messages only with lawful notice, minimum necessary data, access limits and retention controls.
- Reassurance must be fact-based. When risk is unknown, say so and route to qualified support rather than closing the sale.

## VladOS applications

- **LinkedIn/social selling:** use concise, permissioned invitations and useful questions. Never shame non-response, presume a call, fabricate popularity or treat profile data as contact consent.
- **Sales:** maintain an approved language library tagged by purpose, risk, evidence, context and prohibited variants; coach outcomes beyond conversion.
- **Executive coaching:** questions may clarify goals and ambivalence, but the client owns the decision. Avoid identity pressure, forced positivity and “no” conversion.
- **FANATIC/branding:** message testing may compare clarity and comprehension, not covertly exploit fear or misrepresent evidence.
- **O!Sapiens/healthspan:** scenario language must include uncertainty and clinical boundaries; no promise that an intervention will deliver an imagined result.
- **Entheogen Expert:** never use compliance language to encourage psychedelic use, disclose sensitive information or purchase services. Declining/referral is a valid outcome.

## Corpus synthesis

- *Never Split the Difference* also emphasizes calibrated questions, labels and silence; both require a governed distinction between discovery and control.
- *SPIN Selling* offers a more systematic discovery model, but its implication questions likewise need proportionality and a no-pressure stopping rule.
- *Influence* and *Nudge* provide stronger conceptual foundations for reciprocity, social proof, defaults and choice architecture; neither makes manipulative use ethically acceptable.
- *Cracking the Sales Management Code* can place language quality under Call Management, but conversion targets must be balanced with fit, complaints and customer outcomes.
- LinkedIn sources can supply channel workflows; this audit supplies their consent and language-quality gate.
