# How to Get a Meeting with Anyone — Stu Heinecke

## Bibliographic record

- Stu Heinecke; BenBella Books, 2016; ISBN 978-1-941631-78-2.
- One 278-page English PDF; SHA-256 `f060fe19091e8a9171d85d11f44b21433953f73f380912d18fdd5760f16d065e`; about 80,474 extracted words; no OCR required.
- Complete coverage: introduction, 21 chapters, appendices/resources and notes.
- Authority: practitioner casebook of “contact marketing” campaigns. Response/ROI examples are selected successes, not representative causal estimates.

## Bottom line

The book treats high-value access as a campaign-design problem: select a small account set, research deeply, create a relevant and memorable contact device, collaborate with executive assistants, make a concise anti-pitch, and measure meeting-to-business economics. VladOS should retain account selectivity, relevance and campaign economics while rejecting extravagance, surveillance-like personalization, gifts that create obligation, assistant bypass and manufactured scarcity.

## Governed executive-access campaign

1. Define a defensible account hypothesis, value ceiling, exclusion rules and delivery capacity.
2. Select few accounts using verified fit—not prestige alone.
3. Research role, business priorities and public work; document provenance and uncertainty.
4. Choose the least intrusive viable contact concept. A useful insight or thoughtful artifact usually outranks an expensive gift.
5. Review gift/anti-bribery, procurement, privacy, accessibility, security and sustainability constraints.
6. Identify sender and commercial purpose; state the relevance hypothesis and optional ask.
7. Coordinate transparently with the assistant and follow their process.
8. Set a bounded cadence and stop on refusal, policy restriction or non-fit.
9. Prepare a meeting that delivers the promised value; do not make access the end goal.
10. Measure eligible accounts → delivery → response → permissioned meeting → qualified opportunity → customer outcome, including cost and complaints.

## Contact-device integrity gate

A device must be truthful, safe, proportionate, relevant to business context, easy to decline/return, and free from quid pro quo. Prohibit concealed trackers, sensitive personal references, fake publications/awards, live animals, hazardous or burdensome packages, gifts exceeding recipient policy, and any tactic meant to embarrass someone into responding. “Hold-back” may create a legitimate two-part explanation only when the first contact is not misleading and the recipient is not forced to engage to obtain material information.

## Evidence and skill mapping

- Case studies generate creative hypotheses but suffer survivorship, novelty and author/client selection bias; published response rates need denominators and failure cases.
- ROI must include research, production, fulfillment, staff, opportunity and reputation costs—not just revenue from wins.
- Reusable D4 skills: Top-account selector, contact-concept brief, executive-assistant protocol, gift/integrity review, campaign economics ledger and post-meeting value plan.
- Applications: FANATIC account-based entry, high-value corporate coaching/workshops and selective LinkedIn outreach. Never use health, psychedelic or private-life signals for creative targeting.

## D4 promotion contract

Promote the selective-account campaign, assistant protocol, integrity review and economics ledger after comparison with ABM/outbound sources. Keep all campaign response/ROI claims source-bound; reject obligation, policy evasion and invasive personalization.
