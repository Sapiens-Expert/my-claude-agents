# LinkedIn for Business — Corinne Keijzer

## Bibliographic record

- Author: Corinne Keijzer.
- Full title: *LinkedIn for Business: Mastering the Art of Social Branding*.
- Publisher/edition/year/language: Some Books Publisher; first edition, September 2020; English translation/editing credited in the book.
- ISBN: 9789083011790.
- Source: one 339-page PDF; SHA-256 `56c19d5a917c009135fb47147ca39900b9f883f6bb9f0da1e8301e1c5e90921d`.
- Completeness: preface, all 13 numbered chapters and subsections, afterword and author biography. Clean embedded text yielded 41,583 words; no OCR was required.
- Authority: a detailed specialist practitioner manual from a LinkedIn trainer and Digital Moves founder. The author reports LinkedIn ambassador/designer access and extensive training work, but many performance statistics are platform/vendor or secondary claims without enough sourcing to establish causality.

## Bottom line

- This is the most operationally detailed company-page manual reviewed so far. Its durable contribution is not menu instructions but an integrated **organizational LinkedIn control plane**: owned surfaces, administrators, content, employee participation, paid distribution, measurement, employer brand, jobs, response and page portfolio.
- The strongest chapter is employee advocacy. Keijzer rejects identical photos, prescribed summaries, prewritten posts, point systems and treating personal profiles as automated company channels. She favors voluntary, personality-led contribution and capability-building.
- The strongest paid-media contribution is the explicit chain from objective and audience to creative, budget, conversion instrumentation, reporting and learning. It needs modern privacy, consent, fairness, attribution and experimentation controls.
- The showcase checklist usefully challenges page proliferation: create a sub-page only when it has a distinct audience, sufficient recurring content, named ownership, response capacity and ongoing value.
- Most interface mechanics, dimensions, limits, ad types, prices, audience thresholds, algorithm beliefs and performance benchmarks describe LinkedIn in 2020 and must not be operationalized without current official verification.

## Organizational LinkedIn control plane

The book's chapters can be synthesized into ten governed layers:

1. **Purpose and stakeholder set:** define the decisions the page should support—validation, learning, demand, service, employment, recruiting or corporate communication.
2. **Surface architecture:** choose personal profiles, main company page, group, Life/career surface, showcase or related page according to its job and audience.
3. **Ownership and identity:** verify organization, name, URL, domain, accountable owner and recovery route.
4. **Access and continuity:** assign least-privilege page, content, recruiting, advertising, billing, analytics and technical roles; keep a tested backup and offboarding process.
5. **Message and evidence:** audience language, positioning, company facts, specialties, proof, disclosures and accessible CTA.
6. **Content operations:** research, source permissions, calendar, production, accessibility, publishing, community response and correction.
7. **Employee participation:** voluntary contributors, enablement, paid capacity, individual voice, disclosure and safe refusal.
8. **Distribution:** organic participation and paid campaigns governed by purpose, audience, creative, budget and frequency limits.
9. **Data and measurement:** consent, tags/forms, event taxonomy, quality checks, outcome/guardrail metrics and retention.
10. **Portfolio maintenance:** periodic accuracy, access, page necessity, job closure, complaints, experiments and deprecation reviews.

The page is therefore an operated organizational asset, not merely a branded profile.

| Surface | Proper job | Primary owner | Create when | Do not use as |

## Company-page integrity and continuity standard

1. Verify legal/operating identity, domain, brand relationship and who is authorized to claim or rename the page.
2. Use truthful company size, industry, locations, services, jobs and employee relationships; label estimates and change dates.
3. Describe stakeholder value in plain language and support outcomes/customer references with permission and evidence.
4. Use mobile-safe accessible visuals, meaningful alt text/captions, readable contrast and no decorative Unicode that breaks assistive technology.
5. Route the CTA to a maintained, secure, audience-relevant destination with tracking disclosure.
6. Separate page administration, content publishing, recruiting, advertising, billing, measurement and technical tag permissions.
7. Maintain at least two authorized organizational stewards, but never give a partner/friend full access merely as an informal backup.
8. Keep an access register, recovery process, periodic recertification and immediate leaver/contractor offboarding.
9. Create an evidence-backed process for disputed employee affiliations; contact the person privately and preserve correction/appeal paths.
10. Review page accuracy, accessibility, links, claims, ownership and platform configuration on a defined cadence.

The book's suggestion that leavers must “pay for everything” in a contract is replaced by a lawful IP/access-return clause drafted for the jurisdiction and employment relationship.

## Content operating loop

1. Define the audience, decision, intended outcome and acceptable guardrails.
2. Source topics from customer questions, product/service work, employees, research, operations, jobs and company developments.
3. Verify claims, confidentiality, permissions and affected stakeholders.
4. Select the clearest format; links, documents, images and videos serve different communication jobs but have no timeless reach ranking.
5. Make content accessible: alt text, captions/transcript, readable mobile design, descriptive links and language/locale fit.
6. Publish at a sustainable capacity-based cadence, not a universal daily/weekly quota.
7. Respond to substantive questions and complaints according to severity and service ownership.
8. Measure comprehension, audience fit, qualified action and stakeholder outcome alongside reach/engagement.
9. Record the experiment and confounders; adapt, stop or repeat.
10. Preserve/correct/deprecate content according to its continuing validity.

Useful content families from the book are company developments, product/service education, practical expertise, work/process views, research and curated industry material, customer-safe cases, events and jobs. Employees and customers appearing in content require informed permission, not assumed ambassador status.

## Employee advocacy charter

Keijzer's anti-script insight should become a high-priority D4 candidate, with stronger labor safeguards:

- Participation through a personal account is voluntary unless it is an explicit, lawful and proportionate job responsibility agreed in advance.
- Non-participation must not affect pay, performance evaluation, promotion, belonging or access to opportunities.
- When participation is work, provide paid time, training, accessibility, tools and manager support.
- Employees retain choice over photo, cover, biography, topics, wording, channel, connection network and whether to share company material.
- The organization may provide verified facts, optional prompts, assets, disclosure guidance, legal/safety boundaries and review help—not finished posts for identical distribution.
- Prohibit quotas, leaderboards, points, prizes, compulsory likes/shares, scheduled interaction waves and algorithm-gaming coordination.
- Protect confidential information, customer consent, regulated claims, security, harassment response and whistleblowing/lawful worker speech.
- Credit contributors and subject-matter experts; never convert their story, likeness or network into reusable corporate property without explicit terms.
- Offer non-public roles—research, photography, review, interviews, internal subject expertise—without treating them as a lesser form of participation.
- Measure value and employee experience, including burden, discomfort and opt-out, rather than reach alone.

This corrects contradictions in the book: “focus on people who want to” is compatible with autonomy; “they should make time,” mandated agenda pressure and delaying colleague engagement to manipulate distribution are not.

## Governed paid-campaign architecture

### 1. Campaign brief

- business/stakeholder problem and decision;
- objective and causal mechanism;
- eligible audience and explicit exclusions;
- proposition, evidence and landing experience;
- primary outcome, leading indicators and safety metrics;
- budget ceiling, duration, frequency and stop rules;
- privacy/lawful basis, sensitive-category review and owner approvals;
- baseline, comparison design, attribution window and expected lag.

The book's awareness/consideration/conversion categories are useful planning labels, but fixed allocations such as 60%/30%/10%, “ten content pieces” or “90% research complete” are unsupported heuristics, not budget rules.

### 2. Audience governance

- Target from legitimate job-to-be-done relevance, not convenience or inferred vulnerability.
- Audit source freshness and error: member-entered titles, skills, companies and locations are incomplete and can be wrong.
- Treat company lists, contact lists, lookalikes, website audiences and CRM integrations as personal-data processing requiring lawful basis, notice, minimization, security, retention and deletion controls.
- Never use inferred age, gender, health, politics, ethnicity, disability or other sensitive/proxy traits for employment, housing, credit or similarly consequential decisions.
- Review geographic and job-attribute targeting for exclusion/disparate impact.
- Audience expansion/network placements require separate quality and brand-safety checks, not default acceptance.

### 3. Creative and landing integrity

- Match promise, creative, form and landing page; label advertising clearly and substantiate every number, testimonial and urgency claim.
- Never simulate intimacy by inserting a person's name/photo or disguising sponsored content as a personal message when this could mislead or intrude.
- Message ads require a real consenting sender, responsible response coverage, contact limits, easy refusal and suppression.
- Video needs captions and preferably a transcript; all formats require mobile/readability checks.
- Lead forms collect only fields required for the stated next step. Consent for the asset/request is not automatic consent for newsletters, sales calls, enrichment or indefinite CRM retention.

### 4. Instrumentation and access

- Define events and values before launch; test form, tag, consent, source/medium, CRM routing and deletion behavior.
- Use a consent-management and privacy review for Insight Tag/cookies, retargeting and website demographics; the book's “absolute must” framing is rejected.
- Restrict access by role, recertify it and separate billing authority from creative/analysis where scale warrants.
- Log configuration, creative version, audience, spend, approvals and material changes.

### 5. Evaluation

Report exposure, attention, qualified action, conversion quality, downstream value, incremental lift, cost, frequency, audience composition, complaints/blocks, privacy incidents and accessibility defects. CTR, engagement rate, platform-reported conversions and ROAS are diagnostics; they do not independently establish incrementality or profit.

The book recommends A/B testing but sometimes favors platform auto-optimization. A valid experiment requires comparable exposure, one planned material difference, predeclared outcome/window, adequate sample, audience consistency and uncertainty. Optimization algorithms can bias allocation and invalidate a naive comparison.

## Employer-brand and job-integrity standard

1. Define the employee value proposition from workforce research, employment reality and candidate needs—not only recruitment marketing.
2. Represent real roles, locations, work conditions, compensation where possible, accessibility, process and organizational limitations accurately.
3. Include diverse, representative employee voices voluntarily; obtain scoped consent for quote, photo/video, editing, duration and reuse.
4. Do not script testimonials or imply that selected positive stories represent every employee experience.
5. Offer equivalent job information to people outside a target segment and audit targeting for exclusion/disparate impact.
6. Keep vacancies verified, current, secure and connected to an accountable recruiter/application route.
7. Track candidate quality and fairness beyond clicks/applications: progression, experience, representation, time, offer acceptance and retention.
8. Remove closed jobs and outdated claims promptly; maintain a complaint/correction route.

Claims that Life-page followers apply faster, reduce recruitment cost by 50% or reduce attrition by 28% require the original study, population, comparison and causal design before reuse.

## Page-proliferation gate

Create a showcase, related page or localized career page only when all are true:

1. a distinct stakeholder group has materially different information or action needs;
2. the distinction reflects real product/service/brand/legal/geographic architecture;
3. a recurring, non-duplicative content and service stream exists;
4. a named owner, backup, response route and maintenance budget exist;
5. the new page will not fragment authority, follower reach, analytics or search clarity without compensating value;
6. accessibility, localization, claims and data governance can be maintained;
7. success and retirement criteria are documented.

Otherwise use targeted posts, campaign landing pages, website navigation or the main page. Review every sub-page periodically and deactivate it safely when the gate no longer passes.

## Measurement model

| Layer | Questions and examples |
| --- | --- |
| Integrity | Is the page accurate, accessible, current and safely administered? |
| Audience | Are priority stakeholders reached without unfair exclusion or excessive frequency? |
| Content value | Do people understand, save/use, ask substantive questions or correct misconceptions? |
| Community/service | Are mentions, questions and complaints handled well and on time? |
| Talent | Are suitable, diverse candidates progressing fairly and experiencing the employer promise? |
| Commercial | Are qualified journeys, opportunities, revenue contribution and customer value improving? |
| Efficiency | What are total labor, media, tooling and opportunity costs per meaningful outcome? |
| Safety | Opt-outs, hides/blocks, spam reports, accessibility failures, privacy incidents, employee burden and claim corrections |
| Learning | Which causal hypothesis changed, with what uncertainty and next decision? |

Use platform definitions consistently but retain raw components. The book's 2% engagement guideline and self-average can supply a descriptive baseline, not a universal success threshold. Competitor follower/engagement comparisons lack audience, spend, objective and outcome equivalence.

## Evidence assessment

- Strengths: unusually comprehensive operational coverage; explicit 2020 timestamp and repeated warnings that LinkedIn changes; many interface figures; differentiation among roles/surfaces; practical continuity and showcase-capacity checks; strong critique of scripted employee advocacy.
- Weaknesses: no formal reference list or transparent study audit; numerous exact percentages are attributed generally to LinkedIn, other practitioners or vendors without population, date, sample or method; screenshots demonstrate features but not business impact.
- Causal claims about follower behavior, reach multipliers, content formats, recruitment savings, conversion, algorithm factors and ad creative largely come from platform research or practitioner observation and are vulnerable to selection, attribution and publication bias.
- The book acknowledges member-entered audience data are incomplete, a valuable limitation, but underestimates privacy, discrimination, labor autonomy and measurement/attribution risks.
- Commercial provenance is material: the book promotes the author's training, workshops, speaking and Digital Moves services; the afterword invites success stories for future promotion.
- Evidence grade: high operational/practitioner utility for reconstructing the 2020 feature system; moderate conceptual value for page governance and employee advocacy; low independent causal authority.

## Cross-book synthesis

- **Business Gold:** both treat company pages as Awareness/Authority/Advantage infrastructure. Keijzer is much stronger on paid campaign configuration, employee-voice autonomy, technical measurement and employer surfaces; *Business Gold* is stronger on strategic company-page program framing and modern governance. Combine their strengths, not their feature instructions.
- **Steven Mostyn:** Mostyn offers a brief company-page checklist inside a multi-role beginner manual. Keijzer supplies the missing operational depth, ownership roles, analytics, advertising, advocacy and sub-page architecture.
- **Sandra Long/Dodaro/Disney:** Keijzer correctly separates individual and organizational voice. Personal-profile works provide stronger individual positioning; this book shows how company infrastructure should support rather than absorb it.
- **Scott Ingram anthology:** Keijzer's rejection of prewritten posts, point systems and employee bubbles directly strengthens the anti-pod/anti-coercion guardrails developed from Ingram's engagement-focused contributors.
- **Brand Portfolio Strategy:** showcases and related pages are a platform-specific expression of portfolio architecture. The page-proliferation gate should inherit audience distinction, endorsement clarity, resource sufficiency and retirement rules.
- **B2B Social Media Book:** both connect content, paid distribution, landing conversion and measurement. Keijzer adds LinkedIn-specific implementation; the corrected system adds privacy, incrementality and lead-quality controls absent from both.
