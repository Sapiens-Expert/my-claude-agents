## Bottom line

Readers experience prose as clear when sentence subjects name familiar characters, verbs name important actions, openings connect to prior information, endings carry new/complex material, and paragraphs form coherent thematic arcs. Grace adds concision, balanced shape and controlled elegance; ethics asks writers to respect readers rather than conceal responsibility or inflate complexity.

## Clarity-and-grace diagnostic

1. Mark characters and important actions.
2. Where useful, put characters in subjects and actions in strong verbs.
3. Diagnose nominalizations, passive constructions and empty openings by function—not taboo.
4. Link sentence openings to familiar/topic information; move complex/new information toward stress positions.
5. Check paragraph topics, progression and global problem–response architecture.
6. Cut redundancy and metadiscourse without deleting evidence or caveats.
7. Vary sentence length/shape; read aloud.
8. Audit ethical agency: who acts, who benefits, who is harmed and what is obscured?
