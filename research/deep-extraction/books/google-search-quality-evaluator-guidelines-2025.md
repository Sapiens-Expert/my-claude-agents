# Google Search Quality Evaluator Guidelines — January 2025

## Bibliographic record

- Document: *General Guidelines* for the Search Quality Rating Program, commonly known as the Google Search Quality Evaluator Guidelines.
- Version/date/language: January 23, 2025; English; copyright 2025.
- Source: one 181-page PDF stored identically in two source folders; SHA-256 `b2426f7e5818dca0ac89549ea1805ed7e5eb20612a8f946c734c9de14ed09556`.
- Completeness: overview/introduction; Part 1 Page Quality; Part 2 Understanding Search User Needs; Part 3 Needs Met; special-result guidance; evaluation-platform appendix; change log. Embedded text yielded 83,600 words. Extraction contains invisible formatting marks but substantive text and tables are readable; no OCR was required.
- Authority: primary operational guidance for human search-quality raters. It is authoritative about the stated evaluation framework, not a complete disclosure of Google's ranking systems.
- Critical scope: the document explicitly says no single rating directly affects how a specific page or result ranks. Ratings measure system performance and supply examples for improvement. E-E-A-T is not presented as an observable ranking score or ranking guarantee.

## Bottom line

- The document is broader than E-E-A-T. **Page Quality** asks how well a page achieves its purpose and whether it is trustworthy; **Needs Met** asks how helpful a result is for a particular query, intent, locale, location, device and time.
- Trust is the center of E-E-A-T. Experience, expertise and authoritativeness support trust only in context; none can rescue inaccurate, dishonest, unsafe, unreliable or conflicted content.
- Quality is purpose-relative and risk-sensitive. Humor, lived experience, product transactions and clinical advice require different evidence. Clear YMYL topics receive the strongest scrutiny because inaccuracies may materially harm health, financial security, safety or society.
- Main-content quality considers effort, originality, talent/skill and, where relevant, accuracy. Format, length, site size, advertising or use of generative AI do not determine quality by themselves.
- Harmful purpose/content, deception, severe untrustworthiness, malicious behavior and spam/no-added-value content can force the lowest rating before ordinary balancing.
- A high-quality source can still fail a user need through wrong intent, language, locale, freshness, specificity or usability. “Fully Meets” is deliberately rare and usually requires an unambiguous request with one specific answer/result.

## Two-axis quality model

| Axis | Core question | Factors | Typical failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intrinsic quality/trust | Does the artifact safely and trustworthily achieve its purpose? | purpose, harm, MC, accountability, reputation, E-E-A-T, design/monetization | polished but deceptive, unsafe, unsupported or low-value content |
| Need fit | Does it help this person make this decision or complete this task now? | brief/query, intent, specificity, locale, location, language, device, freshness, usability | excellent content answering the wrong question or context |

|  | High need fit | Low need fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High quality/trust | Use; state residual uncertainty | Preserve as reference, do not serve for this task |
| Low quality/trust | Do not use merely because it looks relevant | Reject |

For safety-sensitive decisions, low trust is disqualifying even when apparent fit is high.

## Purpose-risk-trust sequence

1. Identify the true purpose, intended user action and beneficiary.
2. Classify potential health/safety, financial, societal, group and third-party harm.
3. Set the evidence threshold: experience, expertise, official authority, corroboration or a combination.
4. Apply vetoes for harm, deception, severe untrustworthiness, malicious behavior, impersonation, fake credentials, coercion or spam/no value.
5. Inspect the main contribution for effort, originality, talent/skill, accuracy, sufficiency and purpose fulfillment.
6. Identify the creator, responsible entity, contact/remedy, ownership and disclosures.
7. Research topic-specific independent reputation and conflicts.
8. Assess trust—accuracy, honesty, safety and reliability—supported by appropriate experience/expertise/authority.
9. Inspect ads, supplementary material, privacy/security, access barriers and whether design helps or obstructs.
10. Judge audience/intent/scope/locale/freshness/format fit separately.
11. Record uncertainty and disposition: use, caveat, improve, expert review, quarantine, reject or deprecate.

## Trust-centered E-E-A-T

### Trust

Trust asks whether the artifact is accurate, honest, safe and reliable for its purpose. It also includes secure transactions, service/remedy, peer review, correction, privacy, conflicts, accessibility and operational reliability.

### Experience

First-hand experience is especially useful for lived reality, coping, preference, product/service use, process observation, community context and implementation barriers. It does not automatically authorize diagnosis, causal explanation, treatment, legal/tax instruction, financial recommendation or universalization.

### Expertise

Expertise is the knowledge or skill required for the task. Evidence may include applicable education/licensure, validated competence, practice, research, accurate work product and peer recognition. Credentials must be verified and matched to scope.

### Authoritativeness

Authority is contextual go-to-source status. Sometimes it is unique and official; often it is distributed among institutions, researchers, practitioners, communities and witnesses. Followers, title and venue are insufficient proxies.

### Conflict rule

Self-description is a starting point, not validation. Manufacturer reviews, paid endorsements, affiliate recommendations, undisclosed sponsorship, borrowed domain reputation and internal testimonials require conflict-adjusted trust. Disclosure enables evaluation but does not erase conflict.

## Experience-versus-expertise router

| Need | Best contribution | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What did this feel like? | First-hand experience and diverse accounts | Do not imply representativeness or causality |
| How did you cope/use it? | Contextual first-hand experience | Separate personal practice from universal advice |
| What is medically safe/effective? | Qualified clinical/scientific expertise and current evidence | Experience contextualizes but does not prove safety/effectiveness |
| How do I file this tax/legal form? | Current official guidance or qualified jurisdictional expert | Anecdotes reveal usability, not compliance |
| What is the official rule/status? | Responsible authority/primary record | Commentary must link to and date the authority |
| Is this product usable? | Verified first-hand testing with method/disclosure | Manufacturer/affiliate claims need independence |
| What does this community need? | Affected people plus relevant research/practice expertise | Include disagreement and power effects |

On clear YMYL topics, safe first-person experience may be high quality for experience-sharing. Advice that materially changes treatment, finance, safety, rights or civic action requires appropriate expertise/authority.

## YMYL spectrum for VladOS

- **Clear high risk:** psychedelic contraindications/emergencies, medication interactions, arthritis treatment changes, mental-health crisis guidance, tax/legal filing, investment/retirement advice, safety procedures and voting eligibility/process.
- **Context-dependent:** sleep/exercise/nutrition, stress, executive performance, product/service comparisons and career advice. Risk rises with vulnerability, intensity, substitution for care, financial stakes and certainty.
- **Usually low risk:** personal reflection, aesthetics, harmless entertainment and non-consequential writing/style guidance.

Tag the claim or proposed action, not merely the folder. A coping story and a treatment-comparison article about the same diagnosis require different authority.

## Main-content quality rubric

| Dimension | Question | Failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Does the main contribution do its stated job? | title mismatch, filler, conversion-first pseudo-help |
| Effort | Is there meaningful work, oversight, testing or synthesis? | unattended mass generation, shallow paraphrase |
| Originality | Is there new evidence, analysis, expression, organization or utility? | copying, no added value, reputation borrowing |
| Talent/skill | Is execution competent for task and format? | unreadable writing, unsafe technique, broken function |
| Accuracy | Are facts and action/causal claims correct and current? | factual errors, citation laundering, false consensus |
| Consensus fit | For YMYL, is established consensus accurately reflected? | fringe view presented as settled |
| Sufficiency | Is there enough context and limitation to fulfill purpose? | thin content, missing method/population |
| Experience | Is claimed experience authentic and scoped? | fabricated testing, borrowed anecdote |
| Usability | Can intended users understand and act safely? | obstruction, inaccessible media, hidden caveats |

The guideline says generative AI can help or be misused. Do not convert “human effort” into a categorical AI ban. Judge contribution, oversight, originality, accuracy, transparency and added value. Scaled abuse can be human, automated or mixed.

## Independent reputation protocol

1. Identify actual creator, responsible organization and primary site/account.
2. Define the topic-specific reputation question.
3. Search outside creator-controlled properties.
4. Prefer professional/official evidence for YMYL and detailed user evidence for service experience.
5. Read evidence rather than relying on stars, traffic, followers or snippets.
6. Examine volume, detail, recency, corroboration and incentives.
7. Distinguish isolated complaints from credible repeated harm/fraud.
8. Treat no reputation as no evidence, especially for small/new creators—not automatically negative.
9. Record positive, negative and unresolved identity/provenance evidence.
10. Never let reputation override direct evidence of harm, deception or inaccuracy.

## Harm, deception and spam vetoes

Reject or quarantine artifacts involving harmful purpose/content; dangerously misleading YMYL claims; fake identity, credentials, location, testing, reviews or affiliation; impersonation or disguised advertising; coercive interfaces/misleading buttons; unjustified sensitive-data collection, phishing, malware or scams; hacked/spam content; expired-domain or site-reputation abuse; scaled/copied no-value content; gibberish, filler or title-content mismatch.

Monetization, paywalls, ads, user content, anonymity and AI are not automatic vetoes. Evaluate effect, disclosure, necessity and risk.

## Needs Met adaptation

Reconstruct wording/constraints, dominant/common/reasonable intents, information/action/source/place intent, language/locale/location/device, freshness requirements and whether multiple perspectives are inherently needed.

| Rating | VladOS interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Fully Meets | Rare: unambiguous request and one specific artifact/answer safely satisfies nearly everyone in context |
| Highly Meets | Very helpful for a dominant/common/reasonable intent with strong quality/usability |
| Moderately Meets | Helpful but less complete, representative, current, direct or usable |
| Slightly Meets | Some value for the main intent or mainly useful for an unlikely minor intent |
| Fails to Meet | Off-topic, wrong context, materially stale, inaccessible, unsafe or unhelpful |

High PQ cannot rescue irrelevance. A relevant-looking result must be rejected when untrustworthy, especially in YMYL. Freshness and language/locale are substantive fit. Open-ended strategy, medicine, research and identity questions should rarely be considered fully resolved by one source.

## Search/SEO misuse prevention

Do not claim that E-E-A-T is a single creator-visible score; biographies, credentials or citations mechanically improve ranking; individual rater decisions directly rank pages; traffic/backlinks/followers prove trust; long content, human-only creation or institutional hosting is inherently superior; guideline vocabulary is an optimization recipe; or visibility proves accuracy.

## Evidence assessment

- Primary source for the evaluator program and its PQ/Needs Met constructs, examples and instructions.
- Not a peer-reviewed study, law, clinical guideline or full ranking-system specification.
- Examples calibrate judgment; they are not representative samples or causal ranking evidence.
- Scales require contextual human judgment; this document does not provide inter-rater reliability or downstream validation.
- The framework reflects Google's operational choices and incentives. VladOS adaptations must be labeled as adaptations.
- Version/date are material because guidelines, policies, result types and operations change.
- Evidence grade: very high for describing the January 2025 rater framework; limited for ranking-algorithm or scientific-validity claims.

## Cross-framework synthesis

- **Evidence hierarchy:** E-E-A-T adds creator/context trust but does not replace study design, claim grading or primary-source verification.
- **Claim register:** YMYL risk plus experience/expertise routing can set substantiation, reviewer and wording requirements per claim.
- **LinkedIn authority:** credentials, followers and endorsements are signals; trust requires accurate content, relevant proof, independent reputation, disclosure and safe conduct.
- **Corinne Keijzer:** ownership, contact, continuity and complaint handling support organizational trust but do not guarantee authority.
- **Low-quality LinkedIn compilations:** voice discontinuity, irrelevant disclaimers, untraceable claims and low added value map directly to provenance/MC-quality failures.
- **Content strategy:** usefulness and distribution are different. Engagement cannot validate YMYL truth.
- **AI governance:** generative assistance is outcome-governed; fake authors, fabricated experience and scaled no-value material are deception/quality failures.
