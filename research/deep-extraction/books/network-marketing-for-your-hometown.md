# Network Marketing for Your Hometown

## Bibliographic record

- Author: Dale Moreau; foreword by Tom “Big Al” Schreiter.
- Full title: *Network Marketing for Your Hometown*.
- Edition/year/language: first edition, 2019; English. The copyright page contains blank ISBN fields.
- Source: one 150-page PDF generated from an ebook; SHA-256 `56ced4054bcf99a4fbe76adbc92ae283546bcb74221f60714c15babf6234884f`.
- Completeness/extraction quality: complete dedication, table of contents, foreword and all 12 chapters; good embedded text; no OCR required.
- Authority: a commercial script manual for recruiting and selling through one’s local relationships. It supplies no independent evidence for its psychology, conversions, product outcomes, earnings or business viability.

## Relationship to *LinkedIn for Network Marketing*

This is a separate, complete book rather than a renamed file or direct text duplicate. An eight-word shingle comparison found approximately **0.9%** overlap of this book’s unique shingles with the earlier LinkedIn book and **0.7%** in the reverse direction. The conceptual overlap is nevertheless high:

- both organize sales around “rapport,” tiny yes/no questions, pain/benefit contrasts, a short story, a two-minute story, pre-closing, objection handling and referral extraction;
- both use the same folk model of subconscious decisions and repeatedly promise large income, rapid health outcomes and lifestyle transformation;
- the LinkedIn book applies the system to profiles, search, invitations, notifications, DMs and scheduled calls;
- this book relocates it to a hometown “warm market”: friends, relatives, coworkers, strangers and professionals, with face-to-face contact and team duplication.

Accordingly, this extraction records additive channel, local-context and training concepts while reusing—not recreating—the earlier persuasion and claims safeguards.

## Bottom line

- Central thesis: local network marketers can build faster by using brief word-for-word formulas across communication channels and audiences, moving interested people into face-to-face short/two-minute presentations and teaching new distributors the same scripts on day one.
- Limited durable value: match communication depth to task, keep explanations concise, ask permission before more detail, answer material questions honestly, train new representatives before customer contact and make processes teachable.
- Primary risk: the book treats personal trust, family ties, coworker distress, pregnancy, retirement, health concerns and local familiarity as conversion assets. It systematically narrows choices, intensifies pain and makes unsupported health/income promises.
- VladOS disposition: do not adopt the recruiting, selling, objection, referral or “duplication” system. Preserve it as a high-resolution risk source for warm-market exploitation, workplace solicitation, vulnerable-audience claims and scripted autonomy reduction.

## Reconstructed operating system

1. Begin with the local “warm market” rather than online strangers.
2. Rank communication modes from text to face-to-face conversation over food.
3. Use text/phone/video primarily to identify interest and arrange a richer interaction.
4. Ask tiny yes/no questions about a feature, pain or desired benefit.
5. Present two choices: remain with the problem or choose the opportunity/product benefit.
6. Deliver a short story, ask “what do you think?” and use a two-minute story if the person wants detail.
7. Calculate an income target as a required number of recurring consumers.
8. Agree with objections, restate a consensus claim and pivot to a benefit.
9. Use audience-specific scripts for friends, relatives, coworkers, strangers and professionals.
10. Sell products or extract referrals from each interaction.
11. Teach new distributors the five core formulas immediately so the behavior replicates.

The system’s teachability is real; that makes its ethical and compliance defects more—not less—important.

## Six-channel model, corrected

| Book’s level | Useful affordance | Limitation/risk | Safe selection rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Text/messaging | Low interruption, searchable, asynchronous | Tone loss, privacy, easy automation/spam | Use when expected/consented and the message can stand alone honestly |
| Graphic/video asset | Repeatable explanation and demonstration | Selective editing, accessibility, unsupported claims | Provide captions/transcript, evidence and a non-tracked alternative where appropriate |
| Telephone | Synchronous clarification without travel | Interruptive, no visual cues, pressure | Schedule/permission first; allow written alternative |
| Video call | Visual/context cues and screen sharing | Fatigue, privacy/home exposure, recording risk | Use only when materially useful and mutually chosen |
| Face-to-face | Rich interaction and physical demonstration | Travel, safety, power, social pressure | Never engineer chance encounters; ensure neutral setting and exit freedom |
| Meal meeting | Time and informal relationship context | Reciprocity, cost, dietary/accessibility and prolonged pressure | Not a “highest” channel; use only for a genuine mutually desired relationship purpose |

Channel richness is not channel quality. The correct channel minimizes burden while supporting the decision and the participant’s preference.

## Additive warm-market risk map

| Relationship/context | Risk introduced by the book | VladOS boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Friends | Existing affection becomes presumed product/venture fit | No friendship debt, affinity assumption or repeated commercial ask |
| Relatives | Family loyalty, life-stage knowledge and private context become targeting inputs | Do not target relatives by age, pension, debt, health or education; explicit opt-in only |
| Coworkers | Shared stress, salary, pregnancy and leave concerns become recruitment triggers | Default prohibition during work; comply with employer policy; never exploit confidential/power information |
| Direct reports/vendors/clients | Economic dependency can make refusal unsafe | No solicitation across material power imbalance without an independently safe process |
| Service workers | Politeness and inability to leave are mistaken for interest | Never prospect people while they must serve or accommodate you |
| Strangers in public | Compliments and observations become concealed opening scripts | State intent, accept immediate no and avoid appearance/health-based comments |
| Retired/financially strained people | Income anxiety is paired with extraordinary promises | Heightened vulnerability and financial-promotion review; representative outcomes mandatory |
| People with health concerns | Symptoms/appearance become product leads | No diagnosis, sensitive inference or health selling outside governed clinical/product context |
| Local community | Repeated visibility makes avoidance difficult | Protect social safety and reputation; no saturation, referral pressure or community surveillance |

## Communication and consent replacement

1. Determine whether a commercial conversation is appropriate in this relationship and setting.
2. State the topic and financial interest plainly.
3. Ask whether the person wants a brief explanation; provide a safe, effortless no.
4. Give a balanced summary: what it is, for whom, evidence, material conditions, cost, effort, risks and alternatives.
5. Answer questions directly; do not pivot every concern into a reason to buy.
6. Offer a written source or independent disclosure so the person can assess without social pressure.
7. Allow time and channel choice; never frame delay as an objection.
8. End after no or silence. Do not request referrals as a consolation outcome.

Familiarity raises the duty of care because refusal can threaten the relationship.

## Short-story framework, corrected

Replace the book’s `problem → false binary → extraordinary benefit → close` with:

1. **Context:** what prompted the conversation and why it may be relevant.
2. **Offer/category:** what the product, service or opportunity actually is.
3. **Mechanism:** how value is expected to be created.
4. **Evidence:** representative support and what remains uncertain.
5. **Material conditions:** cost, work, eligibility, dependencies and recurring commitments.
6. **Risks/non-fit:** who should not proceed and important alternatives.
7. **Choice:** invite questions or end; no presumed next step.

Two minutes may orient someone, but complex health, financial, legal or business decisions cannot be responsibly closed in two minutes.

## Objection-to-decision-information map

| “Objection” in book | What it may actually mean | Responsible response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No time | Capacity constraint, low priority or polite no | Accept; provide optional concise information only if requested |
| No money / too expensive | Affordability, value or risk concern | Do not finance pressure; disclose total cost and alternatives |
| Not interested | Complete decision | End the commercial conversation |
| Need to think | Legitimate deliberation | Provide material disclosures and no-pressure timeframe |
| Don’t know anyone | Business-model fragility or role mismatch | Do not mine their network; revisit viability and fit |
| Not a salesperson | Preference, skill or ethical discomfort | Do not reframe away; clarify actual work and allow non-fit |
| How much have you made? | Request for evidence and conflicts | Give verified net outcome and representative disclosures |
| Compensation plan? | Material economics question | Explain fully; never answer with “more users means more money” |
| Is it a pyramid? | Legality/value/recruitment-dependence concern | Provide structure, retail-demand evidence and independent diligence |
| Product/health concern | Safety/effectiveness uncertainty | Provide substantiation, contraindications and qualified referral |

## Claims register

The book includes or implies:

- retirement in five years at full pay, monthly raises, doubled pensions, unlimited/full-time income from part-time work, debt elimination, paid homes/cars/vacations and tax advantages;
- simplified income arithmetic based on recurring buyers without participant distributions, churn, costs, rank/volume conditions or recruitment effects;
- rapid weight/fat/inch loss, wrinkle/acne removal, restored memory, immunity, cellular healing, energy/sleep, joint/heart monitoring and reduced medical need;
- legal, insurance, utility, credit-card, communications and product-performance savings without terms or jurisdiction.

Every such claim is blocked. Reuse the health/income claim and MLM due-diligence gates from [LinkedIn for Network Marketing](linkedin-for-network-marketing.md), expanded with local-context and vulnerable-audience review.

## Local and workplace compliance gate

Before any legitimate local commercial outreach, check:

- employer non-solicitation, conflict-of-interest, working-time and data policies;
- power relationships, professional duties and fiduciary/confidential obligations;
- consumer-protection, direct-selling, earnings-claim, health-claim and financial-promotion rules;
- location/event/community rules and whether the person can freely disengage;
- language/accessibility, recordkeeping, privacy and consent;
- product/company authorization for representatives’ claims and materials;
- complaint, suppression, correction and remediation process.

No script can compensate for missing authorization or an inappropriate relationship.

## Team-training model, corrected

The book correctly recognizes that new representatives should not be sent untrained into customer conversations. Its proposed day-one replication teaches persuasion before competence. A safe readiness sequence is:

1. company/product/legal structure and conflicts;
2. representative role, authority and prohibited activities;
3. evidence, claims, disclosures and documentation;
4. product safety, contraindications, referrals and incident escalation where relevant;
5. audience vulnerability, privacy, consent and relationship/power boundaries;
6. neutral discovery and fit/non-fit assessment;
7. total cost/economics, alternatives and cancellation/refund handling;
8. supervised practice using varied outcomes, including no-sale endings;
9. competency check before unsupervised contact;
10. monitoring for complaints, misleading claims, pressure and customer outcomes.

Teach judgment and stop rules, not just memorized conversion language.

## Evidence assessment

The foreword calls the tactics tested, but the book provides no protocol, sample, baseline, records or failures. Dialogues resolve in purchases or sign-ups and appear instructional rather than auditable. Universal psychological claims are unsupported. The work has no references, earnings disclosure, product studies, legal analysis or participant/customer outcome data.

The low exact-text overlap with *LinkedIn for Network Marketing* confirms separate wording, not independent evidence. Both books come from the same author, reuse the same conceptual machinery and examples, and cannot corroborate one another.

## Limitations and do-not-infer

- Do not infer local, face-to-face or meal-based contact is more trustworthy or effective than another channel.
- Do not infer friends, relatives, coworkers or people in the same town want commercial contact.
- Do not treat agreement with a generic statement as trust, rapport, need, consent or fit.
- Do not infer a short explanation contains enough information for a business, health, legal or financial decision.
- Do not infer any health, earnings, pension, tax, product or lifestyle outcome.
- Do not use family/workplace knowledge, health appearance, pregnancy, retirement or financial strain for targeting.
- Do not ask for referrals after refusal as a default practice.
- Do not treat two books by the same practitioner as independent support for the same model.
