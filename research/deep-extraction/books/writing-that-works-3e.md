## Bottom line

- Clarity is necessary but not sufficient. Business writing “works” when the intended reader can understand the issue, evaluate the evidence and take an appropriate next step.
- Decide the action or decision first, then identify the few facts the reader needs. Lead with the point instead of recreating the writer's path to it.
- Visible organization—purposeful headings, short coherent units, informative chart titles, summaries and explicit ownership—reduces reader effort and exposes weak thinking.
- Plain, specific and active language usually improves accountability. Brevity must not remove uncertainty, material conditions, dissent, risk or necessary context.
- Editing is a separate thinking stage: test purpose, order, logic, facts and tone; allow distance and solicit useful review.

## Action-first communication protocol

### 1. Define

Before drafting, write:

- the reader or decision body;
- the decision, action or understanding sought;
- why it matters now;
- the three most important things required to act responsibly;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- alternatives, risks, constraints and dissent;
- owner, deadline and follow-up mechanism.

If no action is required, state the informational purpose and what should be retained.

### 2. Structure

Use this default executive sequence:

1. **Subject/decision:** a descriptive title that states the issue or conclusion.
2. **Bottom line:** recommendation, request or status and why it matters.
3. **Evidence:** essential facts, source/date and interpretation.
4. **Options:** alternatives and tradeoffs, including doing nothing.
5. **Risks/unknowns:** material limitations, dissent and dependencies.
6. **Action:** owner, decision required, deadline and next checkpoint.
7. **Appendix:** detail needed for audit but not first-pass comprehension.

Narrative or relationship-sensitive communications may reorder the sequence, but must not conceal the real request.

### 3. Draft

- Address the actual reader and their likely knowledge, questions and authority.
- State actors and responsibility explicitly when material.
- Use concrete amounts, dates, states and outcomes rather than vague modifiers.
- Distinguish observed fact, sourced claim, estimate, interpretation, recommendation and commitment.
- Use technical terms only when necessary; define them for mixed audiences without distorting meaning.

### 4. Design for navigation

- Use informative headings that state the point, not labels such as “Background” when a conclusion is possible.
- Keep paragraphs conceptually unified; use lists for true parallel items and tables for repeated comparisons.
- Title charts with the conclusion they support and include units, denominator, period, source and uncertainty.
- Provide semantic structure, accessible reading order, meaningful link text, contrast and alternatives to visual-only information.

### 5. Edit and approve

Run separate passes for purpose, structure, logic, evidence, language, tone, accessibility/privacy and mechanics. Let time elapse when risk and deadline permit. Ask reviewers targeted questions and preserve accountable final ownership rather than accepting every suggestion.

## Format decision matrix

| Need | Suitable form | Essential contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quick coordination | Short message/chat/email | Clear subject, context, action/owner/date; use a durable record when needed |
| Decision or recommendation | Decision memo/proposal | Bottom line, evidence, options, tradeoffs, risks and decision required |
| Status/reporting | Structured report/dashboard | Scope, period, definitions, variance, causes, implications and actions |
| Live persuasion/alignment | Presentation plus accessible leave-behind | Audience problem, point-of-view, evidence, interaction, decision and record |
| Policy/process | Controlled document/SOP | Scope, roles, steps, exceptions, version, approval and change history |
| Sensitive conflict | Richer conversation followed by written record | Appropriate privacy, empathy, factual recap, commitments and escalation |

Channel convenience does not determine confidentiality, authority or record-retention requirements.

## Editing checklist

1. What must the reader understand or do, and is it unmistakable?
2. Does the order match the reader's decision process rather than the writer's discovery process?
3. Are claims, quotations, figures, names and dates correct and sourced?
4. Are opinion, inference, estimate and fact clearly distinguished?
5. Are alternatives, risks, dependencies, affected stakeholders and missing information represented?
6. Can wording be shorter without losing scope, conditions, respect or evidentiary accuracy?
7. Is tone candid, proportionate and appropriate to power differences?
8. Is the content accessible, privacy-safe and suitable for the channel?
9. Are action, owner, deadline and response method explicit?
10. Is the correct version approved and distributable to every recipient listed?

## Evidence and limitations

- Most claims rely on executive anecdotes, selected before/after examples, quotations and author judgment. These demonstrate craft possibilities, not average effect sizes.
- Short words, sentences and paragraphs often reduce burden but do not guarantee comprehension. Cohesion, prior knowledge, syntax, information density, layout and testing matter.
- Active voice can reveal responsibility; passive voice remains useful when the receiver/process is focal or the actor is unknown. The ethical rule is not to obscure relevant agency.
- “Come to the point” is strong for routine decisions but must not erase relational context, procedural fairness or explanation in sensitive/high-stakes matters.
- The book's chapter on “political correctness” mixes a valid warning against euphemism and unclear language with dated examples and a sometimes dismissive framing of inclusion. VladOS uses accurate self-identification, current inclusive terminology and impact review rather than treating respect as stylistic excess.
- Recommendations to repeat solicitations, intensify urgency or prevent readers from disengaging require consent, frequency, vulnerability and pressure controls.
- Email, résumé scanning, online recruiting, software, typography and presentation advice dates largely from 2000 and must be revalidated.

## High-stakes and organizational safeguards

- Do not optimize executive brevity by burying bad news, dissent, uncertainty or safety implications in appendices.
- Separate persuasion owner from independent evidence/risk review when stakes justify it.
- Health, psychedelic, psychological, financial, tax and legal communications require qualified review, scope/uncertainty and referral language appropriate to the claim.
- Messages about people must follow need-to-know access, data minimization, respectful language and documented authority.
- Fundraising, sales and internal change communications must make decline, questions and reconsideration possible without retaliation or shame.
- AI-generated business writing requires source verification, confidential-data controls, material-assistance provenance and human accountable approval.

## Corpus synthesis

- Strunk's composition defaults support sentence economy; Roman/Raphaelson extend them into decisions, formats, organizational action and self-editing.
- Redish and Podmajersky provide stronger digital task, accessibility and measurement models; this book adds executive memos, plans, reports, proposals and speeches.
- Bly and Sugarman focus on commercial response; Roman/Raphaelson generalize to internal decisions and professional communication. VladOS makes informed action—not pressure—the shared objective.
- D4 should merge the action-first protocol with the claim register, decision template, evidence standards and platform-specific writing rules.
