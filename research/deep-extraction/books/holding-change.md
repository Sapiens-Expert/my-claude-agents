# Holding Change

## Bibliographic record

- adrienne maree brown, *Holding Change: The Way of Emergent Strategy Facilitation and Mediation*.
- Emergent Strategy Series No. 4, AK Press, 2021.
- Coverage: author's note, foreword, opening, assessments, Black Feminist Wisdom contributions, all six paired facilitation/mediation domains, and the closing note on visibility.
- Authority: reflective practitioner guidance grounded in more than twenty years of movement facilitation and mediation, enriched by essays from Black feminist facilitators. It is situated knowledge, not a validated clinical protocol or comparative effectiveness study.
- Source quality: the supplied 187-page PDF has a complete, usable text layer and consistent internal bibliographic data.

## Bottom line

To “hold change” is to support people and the dynamics among them while change unfolds. Facilitation helps people with shared intentions do difficult collective work; mediation helps people hear one another and address conflict generatively and accountably. The holder's work is less about controlling a predetermined destination than building conditions for truth, dignity, relationship, agency, boundaries and forward motion.

The book's distinctive contribution is an emergent-strategy operating stance. Small interaction patterns scale; change is constant; the route is iterative; power and labor should be decentralized; resilience requires root-level accountability; and healthy processes expand rather than prematurely contract possibility. VladOS should use these as diagnostic lenses, not universal laws. The work is explicitly political, relational, embodied and grounded in Black feminist movement practice.

## Holding-change operating model

### Definition and role router

Before accepting work, classify the request:

1. **Facilitation:** a group needs support to dream, understand, plan, decide or organize.
2. **Mediation:** parties need help hearing one another and navigating tension or misunderstanding.
3. **Formal accountability or transformative-justice process:** alleged serious or ongoing harm requires a deeper, resourced process with multiple support roles. Do not relabel this as ordinary mediation.
4. **Clinical, legal, safeguarding or emergency need:** route to qualified help; a facilitator is not a substitute.
5. **No legitimate mandate:** decline when consent, capacity, role clarity or adequate safety cannot be established.

Record the purpose, sponsor, participants, decision rights, power asymmetries, risks, desired outcomes, time, accessibility needs, confidentiality and referral path. The role can include content or movement knowledge, but any departure from process neutrality must be explicit and agreed.

### Six emergent-strategy lenses

| Lens | Diagnostic question | Operational move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fractal | What small interaction reproduces the larger pattern? | Intervene at a repeatable micro-behavior and watch whether the system changes |
| Intentional adaptation | What changed, and what response still serves the vision? | Revise format, pace or sequence transparently; preserve purpose and consent |
| Nonlinear / iterative | What are we learning across cycles? | Alternate action, reflection and adjustment; retain an evidence trail |
| Interdependent / decentralized | How are relationship, labor, access and power actually distributed? | Move work and voice toward the edges while keeping ownership visible |
| Transformative justice / resilience | What harm, oppression or root condition must be addressed for recovery? | Center affected needs, accountability and repair; escalate beyond mediation when required |
| Creating more possibilities | Where has scarcity or urgency narrowed imagination too early? | Generate multiple life-giving pathways before selecting one |

These lenses interact. “Decentralized” does not mean leaderless or unaccountable; “adaptive” does not mean endlessly changing agreements; “resilient” must not become endurance of harm; and “possibility” does not suspend decisions indefinitely.

## Practice principles converted into controls

- **Begin by listening:** inquire before designing; attend to words, silence, bodies and context, but verify interpretations rather than claiming to read minds.
- **Name what is:** distinguish observation, feeling, inference and allegation. Shared reality cannot be manufactured by facilitator confidence.
- **What we practice is what we become:** evaluate the meeting's interaction patterns as well as its output.
- **Small is consequential:** look for modest, repeatable shifts without claiming that every structural problem can be solved interpersonally.
- **Change is constant:** maintain a live agenda and make adaptations visible.
- **Trust is graduated:** ask whether trust is adequate for the next action. Trust is not owed; boundaries may be the valid result.
- **Critical connection before scale:** strengthen working relationships needed for the task instead of treating participation volume as success.
- **Invitation over manipulation:** explain the purpose of exercises, permit meaningful choice and do not use charisma to force assent.
- **Release perfection and judgment:** support learning without abandoning standards, consequences or discernment.
- **Celebrate without bypassing:** notice progress and pleasure while keeping unresolved harm and obligations visible.
- **Presence over over-preparation:** preserve room to respond, but do enough preparation to manage access, risk, decision rights and logistics.
- **Time is a resource:** renegotiate scope or timing with the group; do not unilaterally “bend time” past participant limits.

## Assessment architecture

### Group diagnostic

The book crosses **relational health** with **functional effectiveness**, producing four provisional patterns: both high; relationship high/effectiveness low; effectiveness high/relationship low; and both low. Additional prompts inspect follow-through, conflict, presence, breaks, power and boundaries.

VladOS use:

1. Collect examples rather than relying on a single observer's impression.
2. Score relationship and effectiveness separately.
3. name the provisional pattern without labeling the group's identity.
4. Identify the smallest behavior, structure or power condition maintaining it.
5. Choose a facilitation lens and observable next experiment.
6. Reassess after action.

The supplied score bands are author-created routing tools, not psychometrically validated measures. Never use them for diagnosis, employment evaluation or claims of objective group health.

### Facilitator and partnership reflection

Self-inquiry covers one's relationship to change, shared power, resilience, solutions, identity fit, capacity and feedback. Co-facilitator inquiry adds communication signals, overload, conflict, client history, administration, money and how partners will intervene with each other in-session. Convert answers into a written co-facilitation agreement: roles, cues, vetoes, handoffs, conflict repair, capacity changes, payment and debrief.

### Client intake

The book's why/who/how/when/what prompts become a compact intake:

- Why this gathering, why now, and why does it matter?
- Who is accountable, affected, skilled, willing and empowered to decide?
- How should values appear in the process and its impact?
- When must work begin, end or be revisited?
- What is possible together, what serves the constituency, and what is at the root?

## Facilitation system

### Prepare the container

- Map purpose, existing assets, histories, power, trauma exposure, access requirements and actual capacity.
- Determine whether the facilitator is culturally, politically and practically suited to the group; identity similarity is neither automatic competence nor neutrality.
- Build community agreements with participants, not merely rules imposed on them. Clarify what is negotiable and what legal, safety or organizational constraints are fixed.
- Design for multiple modes of participation: speaking, writing, movement, silence, remote access, breaks and opting out.
- Establish care roles where warranted, but do not offload professional safeguarding or mental-health responsibilities onto informal volunteers.

### Hold the room

Track four layers simultaneously:

1. **Purpose:** is the gathering moving toward what it exists to do?
2. **Relationship:** can people hear, trust and work with one another sufficiently?
3. **Power and access:** who is centered, interrupted, burdened, absent or constrained?
4. **Emergence:** what new information requires a transparent change of route?

Interventions should be invitations or clear use of contracted authority: reflect a pattern, ask a question, pause, breathe, shift scale, form pairs, name a power dynamic, clarify a decision, take a break, change the agenda, establish a boundary or end the process. Somatic signals can prompt inquiry; they are not proof about another person's intent.

### Convert process into action

Close by recording:

- what was named and learned;
- decisions and how they were made;
- agreements, owners and dates;
- boundaries and confidentiality;
- open tensions and what container will hold them;
- care or referral actions;
- who communicates what to affected people;
- when the group will review both outcomes and relationship quality.

## Mediation system

### Five-phase sequence

1. **Surrender:** parties acknowledge that support is needed and clarify why.
2. **Landscape:** conduct separate intake so each party can describe the conflict, impact, needs, power and hoped-for outcomes.
3. **Listening:** create bounded conditions for each person to speak and demonstrate what they heard; reflection is not agreement.
4. **Negotiation:** surface clear requests, offers, accountability, apologies, boundaries and incompatible needs. No participant can be required to continue a relationship.
5. **Closure:** document next steps, communication protocols, boundaries, review dates and community-facing information.

Use short, capacity-sensitive sessions and slow down around genuine requests or accountability. Avoid infinite retelling, coerced eye contact, touch, forgiveness, reconciliation or public performance. Restored relationship is only one valid outcome; distance or permanent separation may be safer and more honest.

### Kitchen Table Mediation: bounded use

The model favors relational, non-carceral, private settings and asks what larger community, value or lineage the parties are accountable to. It can help with misunderstandings, broken communication, relationship transitions and lower-risk community conflict when all parties consent.

Do **not** use it as a lightweight response to violence, coercive control, stalking, abuse, serious harassment, major power imbalance, active retaliation risk, incapacity, legal jeopardy or ongoing danger. The author explicitly distinguishes formal transformative-justice processes as more intensive and multi-role. Screening, separate support, safeguarding, documentation and qualified referral take priority over ideological commitment to mediation.

## Power, identity and Black feminist lineage

The book does not offer culturally neutral techniques. Its analysis comes from Black feminist, abolitionist, climate-justice and movement traditions and repeatedly challenges authoritarian efficiency, false objectivity and invisible feminized labor. Use the concepts with attribution and preserve their political context; do not detach the language into generic corporate inspiration.

Relevant operational lessons include:

- breath can support regulation and attention, but participation must be optional and adapted for disability or trauma;
- consensus seeks meaningful alignment, not endless unanimity or suppression of dissent;
- boundaries regulate proximity and protect agency; they are revisable by the boundary-holder, not negotiated away by pressure;
- principled struggle turns disagreement toward clarity and greater unity, while retaining consequences and the possibility of separation;
- grief and loss affect collective capacity and require acknowledgment, accommodation and referral—not amateur therapy;
- “brave” or “safer” space is an ongoing practice, never a guarantee of safety;
- facilitation makes culture through who decides, whose time counts, whose knowledge is recognized and how care labor is distributed;
- credit, visibility and storytelling require consent, especially when the facilitator benefits publicly from others' vulnerable work.

## Evidence and ethics limitations

- The book primarily offers experience, philosophy, narrative and practitioner heuristics; it does not demonstrate comparative effectiveness.
- Intuition, energy, spirituality and body-reading are meaningful to the author's practice but must not be presented as objective measurement.
- Nature and complexity metaphors orient attention; they do not validate a specific intervention.
- The group assessments lack reported validation, reliability and normative samples.
- “Trust the people” requires the book's own boundary qualification; it must never override evidence of risk.
- Critiques of dominant facilitation can reveal real power patterns, but attributing a process choice to a single oppressive culture requires contextual evidence.
- Facilitation cannot substitute for structural reform, material redistribution, governance or enforcement.
- Mediation is inappropriate where participation is unsafe or consent is compromised.
- Cultural and movement-specific practices require attribution, humility and adaptation with—not for—the people concerned.
