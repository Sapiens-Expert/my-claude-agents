# Making Websites Win — Karl Blanks and Ben Jesson

## Bibliographic record

- Full title: *Making Websites Win: Apply the Customer-Centric Methodology That Has Doubled the Sales of Many Leading Websites*.
- Authors/date: Karl Blanks and Ben Jesson; first edition, 2018.
- ISBNs: 978-1-5445-0053-9 (hardcover), 978-1-5445-0051-5 (paperback), 978-1-5445-0052-2 (ebook).
- Source: one 371-page English PDF; SHA-256 `180feddb6d5397fdf9ac422ec3e2e29c21e11624327f451e52a2026b6131fd96`.
- Completeness: preliminaries and all four sections, including the full diagnostic toolkit, 14 problem/solution families and final goHenry case study. Embedded text yielded about 55,138 words; no OCR was required.
- Authority: a detailed commercial practitioner manual from Conversion Rate Experts, supported by agency cases, client testimonials, research tools and controlled-test examples. The book itself warns that client results are unique and non-typical. It is not an independent systematic review, complete experimentation textbook, privacy standard or proof that reported growth was caused solely by the described interventions.

## Bottom line

- Conversion optimization should begin with **diagnosis**, not imitation or isolated tweaking. The core DiPS loop is diagnose → problem → solution, followed by measurement and iteration.
- The book's most valuable contribution is triangulated voice-of-customer research: behavioral analytics reveal where; observation and user testing reveal how; surveys, support, sales and interviews reveal why and the language people use.
- Solution design should address specific observed barriers—clarity, usability, unmet need, weak value, offer, trust, risk, complexity, competition, attention, urgency, external funnel failures or lifetime value—not deploy generic persuasion tricks.
- A/B tests measure whether a particular implemented variation changed a defined outcome in its context; they do not automatically generate insight or scientific truth. Testing low-value hypotheses can optimize a broken or unethical experience.
- Conversion is one business outcome, not the purpose of every website or interaction. VladOS must optimize informed benefit, task success, safety and long-term trust, with conversion subordinate to autonomy and evidence in consequential domains.

## Core operating systems

### 1. Governed DiPS loop

1. **Define:** specify user job, target population/context, legitimate organizational outcome, baseline, unit of analysis, decision horizon and non-negotiable safety/autonomy guardrails.
2. **Diagnose:** triangulate behavioral, attitudinal, observational and operational evidence; document data quality and consent.
3. **Problem:** express an evidence-backed barrier as who + context + intended task + observed failure + consequence; distinguish symptom, cause and organizational constraint.
4. **Solution:** generate alternatives that remove the cause, including product, policy, service or process change—not only page copy/design.
5. **Pretest:** review truth, accessibility, privacy, security, safety, localization and implementation fidelity; run qualitative comprehension/task tests.
6. **Experiment:** when feasible, test a preregistered hypothesis with valid randomization, exposure, sample/duration and guardrails.
7. **Decide:** consider effect size, uncertainty, total economics, segments, downstream behavior and harm; ship, revise, reject or investigate.
8. **Learn:** preserve methods, variants, results—including null/adverse findings—and update the opportunity map.

### 2. Diagnostic spotlight matrix

| Evidence source | Best question | Common risk/control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analytics/funnel/form data | Where and for whom does behavior change? | Instrumentation, denominators, bots, selection and attribution |
| Click/scroll/session replay | How does interaction unfold? | Sensitive-data capture; mask/minimize and obtain lawful basis |
| Search, support, sales, chat, feedback | What questions, vocabulary and obstacles recur? | Staff/systematic bias; sample across roles and severity |
| On-page/exit/customer surveys | Why do respondents say they acted or stopped? | Self-selection, recall, leading questions and nonresponse |
| Interviews/usability/cobrowsing | What meaning, expectation or breakdown appears during tasks? | Reactivity, moderator bias, privacy and small samples |
| Method marketing/competitors | What is the end-to-end experience and category norm? | Researcher is not representative; respect terms/ethics |
| A/B experiment | Did this exact variant affect predefined outcomes? | Peeking, underpowering, contamination, multiple tests and local metrics |

No single spotlight reveals the whole problem. Link every inference to its evidence and actively seek disconfirming observations.

### 3. Opportunity/barrier register

For each opportunity, record:

- Audience/task/stage and estimated reach.
- Barrier family: comprehension, usability, need, value, offer, trust, risk, readiness, complexity, competition, attention/timing, handoff or lifecycle value.
- Direct evidence, frequency/severity and confidence.
- Root-cause hypothesis and non-content dependencies.
- Candidate response/counterobjection with proof required.
- User benefit, business value, safety/autonomy risk and reversibility.
- Research/test method, primary measure, guardrails and owner.

### 4. Objection–response evidence table

Replace the book's sales-oriented O/CO table with:

| User question/objection | Evidence/source | Truthful response or product change | Placement/stage | Residual uncertainty | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Do not “overcome” a valid objection by reframing it away. Price, risk, cancellation, data use, contraindications and suitability may require changing the offer or enabling comparison/exit.

### 5. Experiment decision standard

- State hypothesis and causal mechanism before exposure.
- Define eligibility, randomization unit, sample-size/power rationale, duration and stopping rule.
- Choose one primary outcome plus task, accessibility, complaint, cancellation/refund, safety and downstream guardrails.
- Validate assignment, exposure and instrumentation; monitor sample-ratio mismatch and implementation failures.
- Report absolute rates, effect size, interval/uncertainty, segment analysis chosen in advance and practical significance.
- Correct/limit multiple comparisons; never lower significance opportunistically or ship because a variant “was winning” before the stopping rule.
- Preserve novelty, seasonality, interference and external-validity caveats; replicate material wins when risk/scale justify it.

The book's suggestion that 95% significance may simply be relaxed for low traffic is not adopted. Low traffic calls for larger hypothesized effects, longer accumulation, stronger qualitative/observational evidence, sequential/Bayesian methods designed in advance, or an explicit decision under uncertainty—not post-hoc confidence reduction.

## Solution principles with ethical boundaries

- **Writing:** use customer vocabulary, concrete claims, active/simple structure and sufficient detail; test comprehension, not only readability formulas.
- **Usability:** remove task friction, prototype cheaply and observe realistic use; do not confuse ease with informed consent.
- **Value/offer:** make the actual product more useful and terms more favorable; avoid cosmetic messaging around weak value.
- **Proof/trust:** show verifiable, representative, current evidence with source/context; do not borrow logos, celebrity or authority beyond the relationship proved.
- **Guarantees:** state scope, exclusions, process and time clearly; do not use a guarantee to conceal risk or make claiming deliberately difficult.
- **Funnel/readiness:** offer relevant education, trial, comparison, contact or deferral; avoid surveillance-heavy nurture and artificial obstacle sequences.
- **Urgency:** only communicate externally verifiable deadlines, stock or capacity; no resetting timers, invented scarcity or loss framing that bypasses judgment.
- **Lifecycle:** measure activation, successful use, support burden, refund/cancellation, retention quality and referral consent—not merely immediate revenue.

## Evidence and epistemic assessment

- The book explicitly states that cases are unique and not typical, an important limitation often overshadowed by strong marketing language elsewhere.
- Client testimonials and selected wins are evidence of satisfaction and possible effects, not an unbiased estimate of average treatment impact. Losing/null tests and full denominators are not reported systematically.
- Multi-intervention case studies cannot isolate which component caused total business growth; concurrent acquisition, product, market and organizational changes matter.
- A/B tests can estimate local causal effects when implemented correctly, but “keep only winners, so conversion can only go up” ignores false positives, regression, novelty, drift, interference, implementation differences and downstream harm.
- Economic “power law” illustrations are scenario models, not universal empirical laws. Profit response depends on margins, capacity, acquisition markets, retention, service costs and competitive reaction.
- The broad diagnostic toolkit is the most transferable part; exact tools, platforms, benchmarks and conversion claims are volatile.

## Current-use corrections and governance

### Keep as durable

- Diagnose before solution; triangulate methods; research customer language/obstacles; prototype and user-test; connect responses to real objections; test large evidence-led changes; optimize end-to-end and lifetime outcomes; institutionalize learning.

### Revalidate before use

- All 2018 tools, platforms, ad/social/mobile patterns and vendor recommendations.
- Reported uplifts, benchmarks and financial multiplier claims.
- Pricing, guarantees, trust signals, follow-up and urgency tactics by market/jurisdiction.
- Experiment methods against current platform, privacy and statistical requirements.

### VladOS additions

- **Purpose hierarchy:** health, psychedelic integration, psychology, tax/legal and public-interest content optimize safety, comprehension, appropriate referral and informed choice before conversion.
- **Privacy:** session replay, chat, surveys and screen sharing require minimization, masking, access/retention limits, transparent notice and lawful consent/basis.
- **Vulnerable audiences:** prohibit behavioral targeting or persuasive experimentation that exploits distress, impairment, addiction, financial vulnerability or sensitive health inference.
- **Accessibility:** every diagnostic and treatment includes disabled users, semantic/assistive modes and accessibility guardrails.
- **AI/personalization:** audit training/inference data, sensitive segments, generated claims and disparate impact; provide explanation/appeal where consequential.
- **Governance:** independent review for high-risk experiments, a test registry, rollback criteria, incident escalation and no suppression of null/adverse findings.

## Corpus synthesis

- Krug and Redish provide stronger neutral usability/content foundations; Blanks/Jesson add a broad commercial diagnostic toolkit and business-economics lens.
- Podmajersky's measurement triangle is the better top-level model: A/B tests, research and heuristics complement one another rather than A/B being the “final word.”
- *Cashvertising* and *Influence* supply persuasion mechanisms; this note constrains all of them through evidence, autonomy, privacy and long-term outcome gates.
- D4 should treat DiPS as the experimentation/growth branch of the unified content operating system, not make conversion the default objective for all VladOS work.
