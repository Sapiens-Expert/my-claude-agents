# Made to Stick

## Bibliographic record

- Authors: Chip Heath and Dan Heath.
- Edition/year/language: expanded edition; originally published 2007, expanded copyright 2008; English.
- Publisher/identifier: Random House; ebook ISBN 978-1-58836-596-5.

- File completeness/extraction quality: 284 pages; introduction, all six core chapters, epilogue, three expanded advice sections, reference guide, notes and acknowledgments present; complete text extraction; no OCR.
- Authority and intended audience: popular communication framework combining cognitive/educational research with cases, folklore, classroom exercises and practitioner examples. Useful for message design, but not a systematic review of memory, persuasion or behavior change.

## Bottom line

- Central thesis: useful ideas become understandable, memorable and actionable when they are Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional and expressed through Stories—the SUCCESs checklist.
- Strongest contribution: it converts vague communication goals into observable design questions and diagnoses the Curse of Knowledge, burying the lead, abstraction, decision paralysis and data-heavy messaging.
- Main limitation: “stickiness” is an outcome of transmission, not a marker of truth, importance or benefit. The same mechanics help false, polarizing and harmful ideas spread.
- Safe VladOS use: establish claim accuracy, evidence, scope, consent and safety first; then use SUCCESs to make the valid message easier to attend to, understand, remember, evaluate and act on.

## Argument map

Ideas compete in environments where attention and memory are limited. Some succeed because their structure makes them easy to encode, reconstruct, retell and use. The authors reverse-engineer recurring traits into six principles: find the essential idea; violate expectations productively; express it through concrete people, objects and actions; give it appropriate credentials; connect it to something the audience cares about; and use stories to simulate or inspire action.

The primary obstacle is the Curse of Knowledge. Once communicators know a subject, they struggle to imagine the audience’s missing context and overestimate how much their shorthand conveys. Expertise helps generate the answer but interferes with telling others. SUCCESs serves as a tangible external checklist that forces translation from expert abstraction into audience-usable form.

Communication effectiveness is staged: gain attention, support understanding and recall, establish warranted belief, create care and enable action. Simple supports all stages; Unexpected chiefly gains attention; Concrete improves understanding and memory; Credible supports belief; Emotional supports caring; Stories help rehearsal and action. These are functional associations, not isolated or guaranteed causal channels.

## SUCCESs operating framework

| Principle | Design question | High-value techniques | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simple | What is the single core idea that should govern decisions? | Commander’s Intent, core + compact, proverb, high-concept or generative analogy | Slogan without substance; oversimplification |
| Unexpected | Which valid expectation should be broken, and what useful gap follows? | Surprise with postdictability, mystery, curiosity gap, uncommon sense | Clickbait, random novelty, unresolved anxiety |
| Concrete | What can the audience see, do, count or recognize in context? | Specific actor/action, sensory example, prototype, case, shared concrete turf | Anecdote treated as prevalence or proof |
| Credible | Why should this claim be believed and checked? | Relevant authority, experienced witness, telling detail, human-scale statistic, testable credential | Prestige cue, false precision, cherry-picked detail |
| Emotional | Why should this matter to this audience? | Identifiable person, empathy, identity, aspiration, meaningful self-interest | Fear, shame, tribal pressure, sentimental substitution for evidence |
| Stories | What situation lets people mentally rehearse or see possibility? | Challenge, connection, creativity or springboard story | Hero myth, survivorship bias, invented causality |

## Two-gate VladOS standard

### Gate 1: validity and safety

Before optimizing stickiness, confirm:

1. The message is accurate, current and supported at the strength claimed.
2. Scope, uncertainty, exceptions, alternatives and material risks remain visible.
3. The source, commercial interest and relevant authority boundaries are clear.
4. The intended action is proportionate, voluntary, reversible where possible and appropriate for the audience.
5. Health, psychedelic, psychological, legal/tax and financial claims pass their domain-specific review and referral gates.

### Gate 2: communication quality

Then ask:

1. Is the core decision-relevant idea unmistakable?
2. Does surprise reveal understanding rather than merely capture attention?
3. Can a novice picture the mechanism, example or next action?
4. Can recipients inspect the evidence or test the relevant claim safely?
5. Does the emotional frame respect dignity and autonomy?
6. Does the story clarify representative reality and enable appropriate action?

A highly sticky message that fails Gate 1 is a communication risk, not an asset.

## Curse of Knowledge diagnostic

| Symptom | Likely cause | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience nods but cannot retell or act | Expert shorthand and abstraction | Ask a novice to paraphrase; add concrete actors/actions and a worked example |
| Everything seems equally important | Burying the lead / unwillingness to prioritize | State the governing intent and one required decision |
| Teams interpret the same strategy differently | No shared concrete referent | Use a case, prototype, observable behavior and nonexample |
| Data are accurate but forgotten | Statistics lack scale and meaning | Preserve source/denominator, then translate to human scale or comparison |
| Attention drops after an initial hook | Surprise without a meaningful gap or payoff | Open a relevant question and resolve it with the core insight |
| People care but take the wrong action | Emotion/story detached from operating guidance | Add a concrete, safe next step and explicit boundaries |

## Layered message architecture

Simplicity should not delete evidence. Use layers:

1. **Core:** one accurate sentence stating the decision-relevant meaning.
2. **Concrete explanation:** example, mechanism or scenario that a novice can reconstruct.
3. **Evidence:** source, population, denominator, comparison, uncertainty and date.
4. **Boundary:** who/when it does not apply, risks, alternatives and referral conditions.
5. **Action:** the smallest appropriate next step, with consent and reversibility.

This preserves a sticky entry point while keeping the deeper knowledge base auditable.

## Ethical and safety guardrails

- Never use unexpectedness as clickbait or withhold a material fact merely to create a curiosity gap.
- A concrete anecdote, testimonial or identifiable victim must not substitute for representative evidence or expose private information.
- Credentials must be relevant and verifiable; “antiauthorities” and lived experience cannot imply clinical or scientific authority.
- Human-scale statistics must retain the original denominator, time frame, comparison and uncertainty in the evidence layer.
- Do not use fear, disgust, shame, status anxiety, identity threat or belonging pressure to drive health, psychedelic or coaching decisions.
- Stories must be true or clearly labeled as composite/hypothetical; disclose selection and avoid implying typicality from a success case.
- Simplification cannot remove contraindications, red flags, consent terms, commercial interest, limits or referral advice.
- Track misinterpretation and harmful retellings; a message can mutate as it spreads.

## VladOS applications and skills

- **LinkedIn:** one core claim per post; earn attention with a relevant expectation gap; use a concrete example; link evidence; state limits; end with a proportionate next step.
- **O!Sapiens / Sapiens OS:** create a layered explanation for each domain—core principle, concrete behavior, evidence, non-fit and action—without turning health guidance into slogans.
- **Executive coaching:** translate abstract goals into observable behavior and decision rules; use client-generated stories while protecting privacy and avoiding guaranteed outcomes.
- **Entheogen Expert:** use stories only with consent/anonymization and explicit atypicality; keep risks, contraindications and clinical boundaries more salient than transformation narratives.
- **FANATIC:** add SUCCESs QA after strategic and claim validation; build a library of truthful customer-language examples, proof and nonexamples.

## Limitations and do-not-infer

- The book’s cases are selected for explanatory power and memorability; they do not estimate average effect sizes or guarantee outcomes.
- Many examples come from U.S. business, public campaigns and education before contemporary social-platform dynamics.
- The framework does not address distribution, frequency, attribution, accessibility, media incentives or platform algorithms in sufficient depth.
- A message need not maximize every SUCCESs dimension; forced surprise or emotion can reduce clarity and trust.
- “Curse of Knowledge” is a useful bias label, not an explanation for every communication failure or proof that experts communicate poorly.
- Do not infer that statistics are inferior to stories; use narrative for comprehension and evidence for warranted belief.
- Do not infer that emotion causes durable action, that memorability equals learning, or that retelling preserves accuracy.
- Do not use stickiness metrics alone; assess comprehension, correct recall, source attribution, behavior, outcomes and harm.
