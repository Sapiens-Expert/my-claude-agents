## Bottom line

Content design begins not with “How should we write this?” but “What content best meets the evidenced user need?” The answer may be words, a tool, diagram, data, transaction or no new content. Its central operating loop—research, user/job stories, structure, plain-language drafting, pairing, critique and iteration—is a canonical candidate for VladOS.

## Governed content-design workflow

1. Define audience, task, risk and organizational outcome separately.
2. Gather multiple evidence types; note sample, bias, missing groups and privacy basis.
3. Write a need statement: `[user] needs [task/information] so they can [outcome]`, with evidence and acceptance test.
4. Decide the right content format—or whether service/product/process change is needed instead.
5. Structure by user priority, not organization chart; use descriptive headings and front-load answers.
6. Draft in audience vocabulary and accessible plain language; preserve evidence, caveats and safety steps.
7. Pair/critique against need, not taste.
8. Test comprehension/task completion with representative users.
9. Publish with owner, provenance, review/expiry and feedback signals.
10. Iterate, consolidate or remove when evidence changes.
