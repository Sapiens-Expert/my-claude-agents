# Letting Go of the Words — Janice (Ginny) Redish

## Bibliographic record

- Full title: *Letting Go of the Words: Writing Web Content That Works*.
- Author/publisher/date: Janice (Ginny) Redish; Morgan Kaufmann, an imprint of Elsevier; second edition, 2012.
- ISBN: 978-0-12-385930-3 (paperback).
- Source: one 515-page English PDF; SHA-256 `46ccd25699d9c8cda2d36b87f95edf0d666bad1701662a42920e791e1e7d8787`.
- Completeness: introduction, 15 chapters, five interludes, bibliography and indexes. Embedded text yielded about 70,295 words; no OCR was required.
- Authority: a mature practitioner manual grounded in usability practice, annotated examples, selected studies and established UX references. It is not a systematic review, current technical accessibility standard, SEO specification or legal opinion.

## Bottom line

- Digital content is a **conversation initiated by the visitor**. The organization succeeds by helping a specific person complete a task, answer a question or make a decision while meeting a legitimate business goal.
- Purpose, audience evidence, personas and scenarios precede wording. Every item should support a validated visitor conversation or have a specific governance reason to exist.
- People switch between hunting, scanning and reading. Good systems provide clear pathways, front-load key messages, layer detail and use headings, lists, tables, links and illustrations as functional information architecture.
- Content and interface design are inseparable. Real content enters design early; accessibility, responsive behavior and usability testing belong in the system rather than after publication.
- The 2012 screenshots and prescriptions are historical examples. Retain the task-centered principles but revalidate device, search, navigation, link, visual, accessibility and social-platform implementation.

## Core operating systems

### 1. Purpose–person–conversation gate

1. **Purpose:** What legitimate organizational outcome should this support?
2. **Person:** Which evidence-based audience or audience state is it for?
3. **Conversation:** What question, task or decision did that person initiate?
4. **Success:** What observable outcome means the conversation worked for both sides?
5. **Route:** Where do they arrive, what do they need next, and what happens after action?

Personas are data-derived memory aids, not permission to project assumptions. Content without a supported scenario is deleted, combined, archived or retained for a documented regulatory, evidentiary or operational reason.

### 2. Seven-step content-strategy lifecycle

1. Inventory current content: location, title, description, links, dates and owner.
2. Decide messages, media, style and tone from purposes, people and conversations.
3. Start and use an organic style guide.
4. Create workable designs around real content.
5. Audit and act: delete, move, combine, separate or edit; prioritize work.
6. Test the strategy with representative people and real tasks.
7. Plan future ownership, review, maintenance and retirement.

The steps can overlap. VladOS should add `audience`, `task`, `risk`, `evidence`, `consent`, `accessibility`, `jurisdiction`, `review_due`, `supersedes` and `retention` to the inventory.

### 3. Hunt–scan–read model

| Mode | User question | Content response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hunt | “Where is what I need?” | Clear labels, audience vocabulary, findability, obvious routes, minimal distraction |
| Scan | “Is this right and where is my answer?” | Descriptive headline, key-message lead, headings, short sections, lists/tables, visible actions |
| Read/use | “Can I understand, trust and act?” | Plain language, evidence/context, layered detail, instructions, examples, responsible next steps |

Do not force reading during hunting. Do not optimize only for scanning when comprehension, consent, safety or legal sufficiency requires careful reading.

### 4. Bite–snack–meal layering

- **Bite:** key message sufficient to orient the person.
- **Snack:** concise explanation, decision support or next action most people need.
- **Meal:** full evidence, qualifications, method, examples, policy or specialist detail.

Layering is not concealment. Costs, risks, contraindications, material conditions, sponsorship and privacy consequences appear before commitment.

### 5. Content review card

- Can the intended person recognize their task in the title and first screen?
- Does the lead give the key message rather than organizational throat-clearing?
- Can headings alone communicate structure and useful gist?
- Are lists, tables and visuals doing information work?
- Does each link name its destination/action, and does the landing content honor it?
- Is the language direct, plain, globally intelligible and respectful?
- Are authorship, date, evidence, ownership and review status visible where trust requires them?
- Can keyboard, screen-reader, low-vision, cognitive and mobile users complete the task?
- Was it tested with representative people and changed from findings?

## Evidence and epistemic assessment

- Strongest support comes from convergent usability principles, cited scanning/reading and plain-language research, accessibility practice and repeated applied observation.
- Examples demonstrate application but do not prove that wording/layout caused an outcome.
- Small formative studies diagnose task problems efficiently; they do not estimate population prevalence or prove universal preference.
- VladOS strengthens source checks with an evidence hierarchy, claim register and expert review for high-stakes domains.
- “About eight words,” list lengths and other numerical heuristics are testing prompts, not universal thresholds.

## Current-use corrections and governance

### Keep as durable

- Visitor-initiated, task-centered content; evidence-based personas/scenarios; real content in design; plain language and progressive detail; semantic headings, descriptive links, usable lists/tables, purposeful visuals; lifecycle ownership and iterative testing.

### Revalidate before use

- Search ranking/keyword tactics; separate “mobile versions”; fixed typography, click-count, headline-length and link-placement prescriptions; visited-link, home-search, same-page navigation and embedded-link conventions; 2012 Twitter/Facebook/RSS/press-release patterns; legal and accessibility statements.

### VladOS additions

- **Accessibility:** semantic structure, keyboard/focus, reflow/zoom, contrast, alternatives, captions/transcripts, error recovery and assistive-technology testing.
- **Safety/evidence:** classify claims by consequence; attach sources, uncertainty, dates and qualified review for health, psychedelics, psychology, tax and legal content.
- **Consent/privacy:** minimize data, state purpose/use, protect participant stories and obtain permission for identifiable material.
- **Inclusion:** validate language and imagery with represented communities; avoid tokenism and persona stereotypes.
- **AI provenance:** accountable human ownership, source traceability, factual verification, material disclosure and checks for invented evidence, homogenized voice and unsafe personalization.
- **Measurement:** pair task completion, comprehension, error/recovery and trust with business outcomes; clicks/conversion alone do not prove a successful conversation.
