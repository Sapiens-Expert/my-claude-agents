# Strategic Writing for UX — Torrey Podmajersky

## Bibliographic record

- Full title: *Strategic Writing for UX: Drive Engagement, Conversion, and Retention with Every Word*.
- Author/publisher/date: Torrey Podmajersky; O'Reilly Media; first edition, June 2019 (first release May 31, 2019).
- ISBN: 978-1-492-04939-5.
- Source: one 320-page English PDF; SHA-256 `853d885525e6d838bb818cd9f94f1a266082125460e33168ab85db8619d41966`.
- Completeness: preface, all nine chapters, index and author note. Embedded text yielded about 46,301 words; no OCR was required.
- Authority: a specialist practitioner framework based on the author's product work, teaching and illustrative cases, with established UX research methods. It is not a systematic evidence review, current accessibility standard or universal product-growth model.

## Bottom line

- Strategic UX content aligns two legitimate sets of goals: those of people using an experience and those of the organization responsible for it. “Fixing the words” cannot compensate for broken architecture, policy, product behavior or incentives.
- The **voice chart** converts abstract product principles into operational decisions across concepts, vocabulary, verbosity, grammar, punctuation and capitalization. It is both a creative tool and an externally agreed review standard.
- Content-first conversational design exposes sequence, terminology, expectations, edge cases and recovery before the interface is fixed. It turns a role-played goal-oriented conversation into a journey and initial wireframe.
- Eleven reusable text patterns and a purposeful–concise–conversational–clear editing sequence make production scalable, but patterns are starting hypotheses rather than formulas.
- Content effectiveness should be triangulated through behavioral metrics/experiments, qualitative research and heuristic review. A business uplift without comprehension, accessibility, autonomy or downstream benefit is not sufficient evidence of good UX content.

## Core operating systems

### 1. Dual-goal strategy gate

For every flow or content intervention, specify:

1. Person, context, job and desired outcome.
2. Organization's legitimate outcome and mission connection.
3. Shared value and any tension or conflict between the goals.
4. Product/policy/technical opportunities and constraints.
5. Content's exact role: attract, investigate, convert/commit, set up/onboard, use/engage, support/fix, retain/prefer or refer/champion.
6. Non-content dependencies and accountable owners.
7. Success, guardrail and failure measures for both sides.

If organizational success depends on obscuring cost, risk, consent or exit, the goals are not aligned and wording must not conceal the conflict.

### 2. Voice chart

Columns are ratified product/experience principles. Rows are:

| Row | Decision governed |
| --- | --- |
| Concepts | Ideas the experience should reinforce when relevant |
| Vocabulary | Distinctive preferred/avoided words—not the full terminology list |
| Verbosity | Expected information density and variation by context |
| Grammar/syntax | Sentence structure, tense, person, active/passive and formality choices |
| Punctuation | Rhythm, emotional signaling and permitted marks |
| Capitalization | Case rules and intentional signals of hierarchy or action |

VladOS extensions: add evidence/examples, counterexamples, risk exceptions, accessibility/localization implications, component/state coverage, owner and review date. Ratification should create alignment, not freeze the chart against user evidence.

### 3. Conversational content-first workshop

1. Put the person's intention at the start of a visible path and list both goal sets.
2. Assign one participant to the person and another to the experience; include research, product, design, engineering and relevant domain roles.
3. Improvise the conversation without screens, jargon or premature components; repeat until both sides' legitimate goals are met.
4. Record topics, terminology, questions, promises, decisions, emotional shifts and exceptions.
5. Introduce alternate contexts, abilities, prior knowledge, failures and edge cases.
6. Distill the dialogue into a sequence/journey; remove speech artifacts that do not read well.
7. Map person utterances to inputs/options and experience utterances to titles, descriptions, status, help and feedback.
8. Prototype, test and widen into branches and recovery states.

The workshop generates hypotheses. Representative-user research, accessibility review and actual system behavior remain required.

### 4. Eleven-pattern library

| Pattern | Core job |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Establish place, content or single task |
| Button/link/command | Let the person express an action or destination |
| Description | Supply supporting context, expectation or consequence |
| Empty state | Explain absent content and enable a useful next action |
| Label | Name information or input compactly and consistently |
| Control | Explain choice, state and effect for toggles/options |
| Text input | Request understandable information with persistent guidance and rules |
| Transitional text | Orient during movement, processing or multi-step change |
| Confirmation | Verify completion and relevant next state |
| Notification | Bring timely, actionable information without abusive interruption |
| Error | Explain, recover and prevent recurrence without blame |

Each pattern record should include purpose, trigger/state, data/system truth, examples, accessible name/announcement, localization notes, risk, analytics and owner.

### 5. Four-pass edit

1. **Purposeful:** Does every string/content unit serve the person's current goal and a legitimate organizational purpose? Is this a content problem?
2. **Concise:** Remove repetition and nonessential detail; layer or relocate needed depth without hiding material information.
3. **Conversational:** Use the sequence, vocabulary and active constructions people recognize; conversational means turn-taking, not compulsory casualness.
4. **Clear:** Make reference, terminology, action, consequence, time and system state unambiguous; test across audience knowledge, locale and assistive modes.

Order matters: polishing clarity cannot rescue content with no valid purpose.

### 6. Measurement triangle

| Method | Answers | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct behavior/experiment | Did a controlled content variation change completion, onboarding, engagement, retention, referral or cost? | Attribution, novelty, sample/power, instrumentation and local-metric harm |
| Research | Why do people interpret/behave this way; what language, expectation or exclusion is present? | Small/nonrepresentative samples and moderator/analysis bias |
| Heuristic scorecard | Where does content violate shared purposeful, concise, conversational, clear, accessible or voice standards? | Expert judgment predicts issues but does not establish user impact |

Use all three when consequences justify it. Predefine the primary outcome, guardrails, segment checks, decision rule and downstream window; retain null and adverse results.

## Heuristic adaptation

The book's usability criteria are purposeful, concise, conversational, clear and accessible. Voice criteria evaluate concepts, vocabulary, verbosity, syntax/grammar, punctuation and capitalization. VladOS adds:

- Truth/evidence and accurate system state.
- Autonomy, consent and non-manipulation.
- Safety and qualified-review boundaries.
- Privacy/security and data minimization.
- Inclusive language and localization readiness.
- Recovery, reversibility and support.
- Provenance, ownership and maintenance.

A composite score must not hide a failure in safety, accessibility, truth or consent; these are release gates.

## Team and operational model

- Write in the design/state whenever possible, not in an isolated list of strings; preserve a stable content ID for implementation and tracking.
- Review requests include goal, audience, context, design, state, constraints, open questions, evidence and the kind of feedback needed.
- Decision rights are explicit: autonomous owner, informed leader or consensus for defined classes—not whoever comments last.
- Publishing connects design content to repositories/CMS/localization with versioning, QA and rollback; verify the shipped build.
- Content debt is tracked alongside product work with impact, urgency, affected states, dependencies and owner.
- A first UX writer's 30/60/90 plan should learn before standardizing, fix high-impact fires, build relationships, document foundations, demonstrate value and scale through enablement rather than gatekeeping.

## Evidence and epistemic assessment

- The frameworks are experience-derived and internally coherent, illustrated through fictional cases rather than comparative field studies.
- Pattern guidance and editing heuristics align with established UX/plain-language practice, but specific prescriptions such as very short buttons are contextual hypotheses, not universal laws.
- Direct metrics can show association or experimental effect in one implementation; they do not establish that content alone will transfer across products or populations.
- The heuristic scorecard improves consistency and prioritization but depends on evaluator calibration and requires user evidence for validation.
- The attract–convert–engage–retain/referral lifecycle reflects organizational growth goals and can overprivilege business activity; public-service, health and support experiences may optimize access, comprehension, safety or successful exit instead.

### Revalidate before use

- A/B test advice against modern experimentation, privacy and statistical standards.

### VladOS additions

- **Accessibility:** semantic roles/names, focus/order, status announcement, visible labels, reflow, cognitive access and disability-inclusive testing are release requirements.
- **High stakes:** health, psychedelic, psychology, tax, legal and financial content uses safety outcomes, uncertainty and professional review—not conversion/retention as primary optimization.
- **Autonomy:** cancellation, decline, privacy choice and successful departure can be positive lifecycle outcomes.
- **Privacy/security:** minimize event and content-review data; threat-model sensitive states and avoid experimentation that exposes or infers protected attributes.
- **AI:** version prompts/models and generated strings, protect inputs, evaluate hallucination/bias, preserve human decision ownership and offer explanation/appeal for consequential automation.
- **Statistics:** require hypothesis, power/sample rationale, randomization/unit checks, duration, multiple-testing control where relevant and practical significance—not uplift alone.
