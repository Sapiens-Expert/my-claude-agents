# Research standards

Define the question, decision, population, claims, inclusion criteria, and freshness requirement. Prefer primary sources, record methods and limits, preserve contradictory evidence, and cite enough context to audit the conclusion.

## Provenance-integrity gate

Before treating a book, report, guide or compilation as an attributable authority, check:

1. credited author, publisher, edition, date and identifiable original source;
2. stable voice, terminology, audience and conceptual structure;
3. citations or traceable provenance for precise claims, statistics and quotations;
4. internal consistency of dates, population figures, product names and recommendations;
5. disclosed commercial relationships, vendors, affiliates and services;
6. distinction between original reasoning, synthesis, examples and copied/quoted material;
7. evidence that the source is complete rather than an excerpt, summary or altered copy;
8. rights and redistribution constraints.

Also compare visible credits with file metadata, first-person experience claims, editorial quality and subject-relevant boilerplate. Metadata mismatch is a warning—not proof of false attribution—because legitimate conversion and publishing tools often rewrite it.

Abrupt voice changes, incompatible time periods, conflicting statistics, unexplained vendor promotion, malformed/repeated text, irrelevant disclaimers or absent attribution trigger **heterogeneous-compilation status**. This is a provenance-risk classification, not an accusation of plagiarism. Retain independently defensible concepts only after comparison with stronger sources; do not reuse distinctive wording, assign originality, or promote unsupported claims from the compilation alone.
