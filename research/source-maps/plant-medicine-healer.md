# Plant medicine healer source map

## Processing model

Twenty-one PDFs representing twenty unique files were mapped; the two James Duke handbook files are exact copies. Sources were separated into botanical safety/science, clinical phytotherapy and integrative medicine, historical traditions, and consumer home-remedy/adaptogen material. Traditional use, textbook inclusion, constituent chemistry, and practitioner recommendation do not by themselves establish clinical efficacy or safety.

## Open gates

- Stage B must extract exact botanical identity, part, preparation, dose, route, population, indication, outcome, adverse effects, interactions, and cited evidence—never generic plant claims.
- Recheck every live recommendation against current authoritative monographs, regulator communications, clinical guidance, interaction/toxicology resources, and jurisdictional rules.
- Do not extrapolate between species, parts, teas, tinctures, powders, essential oils, extracts, isolated compounds, mixtures, or brands.
- Treat adaptogen, detox, immune-boosting, hormone-balancing, tonic, energy, and healing language as claims requiring operational definition and evidence.
- Poisoning, severe reactions, medication changes, pregnancy, surgery, significant disease, and vulnerable populations require pharmacist/clinician, poison-control, or emergency pathways as appropriate.
