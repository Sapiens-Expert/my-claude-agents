# Seven influence principles produce reliable universal compliance effects

## Claim

Reciprocity, liking, social proof, authority, scarcity, commitment/consistency and unity reliably increase compliance across people, cultures, media and decision stakes.

## Assessment

**Rejected as a universal effect claim.** The evidence base mixes experiments, field research, reviews, observations and anecdotes of varying quality. Effects depend on implementation, context, population, culture, outcome and publication/replication quality. Compliance does not establish comprehension or welfare.

## Safe use

Treat each cue as a context-specific hypothesis and recipient-protection concern. Review current mechanism-specific evidence for consequential use; verify cue truth and relevance; test cumulative pressure, comprehension, voluntariness, outcomes and harms; and never claim guaranteed response or infer individual susceptibility.
