## Diagnosis and strategic bet

The psychedelic content market on Instagram/TikTok is saturated at both ends and empty in the middle: one end is evangelism (transformation stories, ceremony aesthetics, high reach/low trust/terrible client quality), the other is institutional (careful, accurate, unwatchable, no distribution). The middle — someone who already had the experience, felt something real, and weeks later is living the same life — is almost unserved.

**The bet: do not build an audience interested in psychedelics. Build an audience interested in why insight does not become change.** Psychedelic experience is the entry context, not the topic. Practical consequence: roughly 60% of content should be usable by someone who has never taken anything — which is also the single strongest platform-safety mechanism available, since content about the insight-action gap is not drug content to a moderation classifier.

## Platform division of labor

Not the same channel with the same content pasted twice. **TikTok is discovery and reps** — volume, mostly unqualified, hooks get tested cheaply, sends a small qualified fraction onward. **Instagram is qualification and conversion** — older skew, better DM culture, profile functions as a landing page, saves/shares carry more weight per view. Expect TikTok to produce ~20x the views and ~1/5 the qualified DMs — that's a working system, not a failure. Cross-platform rule: same script, different cover text and different first two seconds. TikTok opens on tension; Instagram opens on precision.

**Success at 90 days is not follower count.** Twelve to twenty qualified inbound conversations, four to eight discovery calls, two to four paid clients, and a comment section that reliably self-selects. If followers grow fast and DMs stay empty, the content is wrong regardless of the numbers.

## Positioning

**Internal version:** For stable adults who had a meaningful altered-state experience that has not changed their life, Vladislav Andreev is the scientifically literate translator who explains what may have happened and what turning it into behavior actually requires. Unlike evangelists, retreat brands, and generic coaches, he sells no experience, promises no outcome, and says plainly when the right next step is a clinician rather than a coach.

**Public one-liner (bio use):** "The experience is not the change. I help stable adults work out what to do with what they saw."

**The three sentences that carry the whole position — every video should be traceable to one of them:** Insight is not yet change. Meaning does not require certainty. Some of this is not a coaching problem.

## Content pillar system

Seven pillars, fixed formats, fixed ratios, one named recurring series each — named series make the account legible and saved videos findable.

| # | Pillar | Series name | Share | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Insight is not yet change | "Three weeks later" | 25% | Core recognition, primary lead driver |
| 2 | Before another experience | "Before you book the next one" | 15% | Highest-intent audience, direct to consult |
| 3 | Difficult experiences and referral literacy | "This is not a coaching problem" | 15% | Trust and disqualification |
| 4 | Evidence versus story | "What the study actually said" | 15% | Credibility, differentiation from evangelists |
| 5 | Meaning without certainty | "It meant something. It may not be true." | 10% | Philosophical depth, share driver |
| 6 | Preparation without fantasy | "Questions nobody asks first" | 10% | Pre-experience audience, save driver |
| 7 | Coaching, therapy, and the right next step | "Which room are you in" | 10% | Category education, qualifies inbound |

**Format library — seven repeatable structures, no improvisation:** (1) Recognition open — first line names the viewer's exact situation, no wind-up, then mechanism, then one action. (2) Mechanism explainer — one named process, 40 seconds, no diagnosis language. (3) Claim audit — state the popular claim fully and fairly, isolate the inference that doesn't follow, give the actual determinant. (4) The referral video — names a symptom cluster, states plainly this is outside coaching, gives the category of support to look for. No CTA, no link, no offer, ever. (5) Two people, same experience — same event, two outcomes, the variable named at the end; strongest share format. (6) The question set — three to five questions the viewer should be able to answer; strongest save format. (7) Answering a comment on camera — highest trust per second of any format; screenshot the comment, answer it as an adult.

**Cadence:** TikTok one per day at 14:00 Barcelona. Instagram: five Reels per week, plus three static/carousel posts that are not repurposed Reels. Build a 14-video edited buffer before starting daily.

## Growth triggers, ranked by leverage

Specificity of the stuck state beats generic language ("you keep telling the story of the experience instead of changing anything" travels; "integration matters" doesn't). Named enemies that are safe to attack — the insight-action gap, the retreat that sells the peak and abandons the return, the idea that a bigger dose fixes a behavior problem — attacking a bad idea travels, attacking a person doesn't and costs the professional network. Counter-consensus only works paired with a real study or mechanism, not as edgelord contrarianism. Numbered/named series produce return viewing and profile visits at a much higher rate than one-offs — say the series name out loud in the video. Cover-frame text carrying the hook verbatim (never repeating a mid-sentence transcript fragment) is worth more than any topic decision. Comment mining: every qualified comment is a script; answering one on camera rewards the commenter and signals that comments get read.

**What will not work and should not be attempted:** trending audio, dances, duets with psychedelic influencers, aesthetic-visual-only content, personal experience storytelling as a primary format (tempting, and it converts the wrong people).

## Trust triggers

Trust in this niche is built by what's refused, not what's claimed: saying "I do not know" on camera at least once a month; referring away in public ("if this is your situation, a coach is the wrong person, here's the category to look for"); naming the limits of your own role unprompted (not a therapist, not a clinician, not a facilitator — stated as fact, no apology, no over-explaining); citing sample sizes and limitations out loud ("24 participants, open label, no control group" is a trust bomb in a feed full of "science shows"); refusing dose/sourcing questions in public, once, calmly, on camera; no before-and-after transformation content — client material only as a flagged composite illustrating a mechanism; consistency of face, setup, and voice (production polish is irrelevant, predictability is not).

## Engagement triggers

Optimize for qualified comments, saves, and shares, in that order — likes are noise. Question CTAs that require lived specificity ("what did you understand that you still haven't acted on" filters instantly — nobody with no experience can answer it, everybody stuck can). The unfinished frame (end with the second half named but not delivered, deliver it in comments or part two) — sparingly, twice a month at most, degrades trust if habitual. Explicit, reason-bound save instructions ("save this and read it back the week after, not now") substantially outperform bare "save this" requests. Share prompts aimed at a specific third party ("send this to the person who came back from a retreat and hasn't stopped talking about it") are the strongest algorithmic signal available on both platforms. Weekly Instagram Stories polls/question stickers — audience intelligence plus DM warming, since a sticker reply lands in DMs already and removes the friction of writing first.

**Comment operating protocol:** reply within 60 minutes for the first two hours after posting, then twice more that day. Four reply types only — qualified stuck comment (one mechanism plus one question back, never pitch), boundary-violating comment (public, calm, once: "I do not answer dosing or sourcing questions here," then no further engagement), evangelist/mystic pushback (grant what's correct, isolate the disputed claim, return to evidence), distress signal (do not diagnose or counsel in public, do not DM an offer, short public reply pointing to licensed local support, then stop — never move an acute conversation into DMs, that becomes a private duty of care Vlad isn't licensed to hold).

## DM and lead-generation system

**Never DM first with an offer.** Inbound only, or a reply to a Story reaction. Four inbound types, exact response:

- **Type 1, stable and stuck (this is the client).** Do not pitch in message one. Ask one question: "Thanks for writing. Before anything else, one question so I understand the situation properly: what specifically did you want to be different after, and what has actually happened instead?" If the answer describes a stable life with a stalled change, second message offers the 60-minute session and adds: "If it turns out this is not a coaching problem, I will tell you that instead." That closing sentence closes more executives than any credential list.
- **Type 2, pre-experience.** Do not advise. Redirect to the self-assessment ("I do not advise on whether or what to take, and I'd be careful with anyone who does over DM. What I can send you is a self-assessment covering the screening questions worth answering honestly first.") — primary funnel for this segment.
- **Type 3, acute or clinical.** No offer, no funnel, no link to anything commercial: "What you're describing is outside what coaching is for, and I would not want to be your only support here. The right next step is a licensed clinician in your country, ideally with experience in this area. If it gets worse or you stop being able to function, treat it as urgent and go to local emergency services." Then stop — do not continue the thread into a sale later, ever.
- **Type 4, boundary-testing.** One line, no discussion: "Not something I answer. Good luck."

**Lead magnet stack.** Keep the existing safety self-assessment as the primary funnel asset for the pre-experience group. Add one new asset for the primary (Stalled) audience, which currently has nothing to convert into: **"The 14 Day Return"** — a short, platform-safe PDF/page covering what to do in the two weeks after an experience, structured as questions and one behavioral commitment rather than instructions. A third asset — a free Saturday Barcelona group — later, not now, mentioned only occasionally and geographically framed.

**Link architecture.** One link in bio to a simple page with three destinations only: self-assessment, The 14 Day Return, work with me. Every additional option costs conversion.

## Platform risk rules (non-negotiable — applies to script, audio, on-screen text, cover frame, caption, hashtags)

1. No substance names spoken or written. The Series 01 "microdosing" exception is flagged as conscious and not extended to new content.
2. No dose, quantity, route, combination, or timing.
3. No sourcing, vendors, legal-jurisdiction naming, retreat names, or facilitator referrals, even in a negative frame — "avoid retreat X" still reads as retreat content to a classifier.
4. No consumption imagery, substance imagery, paraphernalia, or ceremony aesthetics.
5. No encouragement framing — content addresses people who already had an experience or are already deciding, never people being persuaded.
6. No claims of treatment, cure, healing, or outcome guarantee, including implied.
7. No diagnosis of a viewer/commenter in video, comment, or DM — "what you're describing sounds like something worth having assessed," never "you have."
8. No sales CTA on any acute-risk content, including soft CTAs, link mentions, or "DM me." The referral video ends with the referral.
9. No profanity — reads as recreational-adjacency to moderators and as unserious to the client profile.
10. Every difficult-experience video carries a spoken or on-screen scope line: education, not medical advice, not a substitute for licensed care.
11. Contraindication content, when relevant, names personal/family history of psychosis, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and current psychiatric instability, and states legal status varies by jurisdiction.
12. Keep a second account or full local archive of every published video — assume a strike or removal happens and make it a delay, not a loss.

## Metrics dashboard (weekly)

**Leading:** save rate (saves/1,000 views) — 15+ IG, 8+ TikTok target. Share rate (shares/1,000 views) — 10+. Qualified comment rate (comments describing a real stuck state, per video) — 3+. Profile visit rate (per 1,000 views) — 20+. Follow-through (link clicks per 100 profile visits) — 8+. Retention at 3 seconds (platform native) — 65%+.

**Lagging (target by day 90):** qualified inbound DMs/week — 3 to 5. Discovery calls/month — 4 to 8. Paid clients/month — 1 to 3. Disqualification rate — 25–40% of inbound, deliberately (near-zero is a warning sign that boundary content isn't reaching the people it needs to filter out).

**Two diagnostic ratios worth more than the rest.** Save-to-like ratio below 0.10 means the content is producing agreement, not usefulness — correct toward mechanism and question-set formats. DMs per 1,000 followers below 0.5/week means the audience is the wrong audience regardless of growth — correct toward pillars 1, 2, and 5.

## First 10 strategic moves

1. Rewrite bio/profile positioning on both platforms this week ("Insight is not yet change. I help stable adults turn what they saw into what they actually do. Education, not medical advice. Not therapy. Not a provider. Barcelona · EN/RU").
2. Publish three pinned videos, leave pinned 90 days: "Three weeks later, nothing changed" (recognition/hook), "What I do, what I do not do, and when to see a clinician instead" (qualification/boundary), "Before you book another one" (re-doser argument) — together these do the work of a landing page.
3. Build The 14 Day Return asset — one weekend of work, the missing conversion asset for the primary audience, entirely platform-safe.
4. Convert existing Series 01/02 scripts into the seven-pillar taxonomy above before writing anything new — likely finding: heavy weighting toward pillars 1 and 4, almost nothing in pillar 3.
5. Fix the cover-frame system before anything else — every video, cover text compressing the hook, consistent placement, never a mid-sentence frame. Highest return per hour on this whole list.
6. Record five referral-literacy videos (pillar 3) in one session — the trust engine and risk shield, also the pillar most likely to be avoided because it has no CTA; batch it so avoidance isn't an option.
7. Set the comment operating protocol as a saved text-snippet — 15 minutes a day, not more.
8. Start the Instagram Stories rhythm — three per week, one a question sticker; cheapest DM-warming mechanism available, no production cost.
9. Run the A/B hook pairs already built into Series 02 and log them (one row per video: series, hook type, cover text, 3-second retention, save rate, qualified comments) — 30 videos in, patterns become decisions.
10. Do not build anything else for 90 days — no new lead magnet beyond The 14 Day Return, no email rebuild, no second account, no paid promotion, no Reels-to-Shorts expansion. Failure mode: adding surface area before the first system produces qualified DMs.

**Two things flagged as real risks, not caveats.** The audience that grows fastest on TikTok in this niche is not the audience that pays — expect weeks 4–10 where numbers rise and the inbox stays quiet; that's the cost of refusing evangelist content, not the strategy failing. The correction is more pillar 1/2, not more reach. Referral content will feel commercially irrational — publishing videos that end by sending people away, with no CTA, while cashflow is the priority, will be the hardest part of this to sustain, and it's also what separates this account from every psychedelic coach on the platform.
