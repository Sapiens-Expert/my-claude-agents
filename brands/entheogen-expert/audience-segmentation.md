# Audience segmentation — entheogen.expert

Ten-segment deep map from the "INSTA Strategy" document (2026-08-30). Supersedes the brief version in `audience.md` as the operational reference for who to attract, tolerate, educate, and repel. Working principle throughout: what someone says they want is rarely what they're actually buying — the gap between the stated problem and the underlying need is where every content angle and DM response comes from.

## The ten segments

| # | Segment | Internal name | Stated problem | Real underlying need | Coaching readiness | Risk | Commercial relevance | Disqualification signals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Curious/preparing | The Researcher | "I need to know what to expect" | Permission and a way to assess personal risk honestly | Low now, high in 3–12 months | Medium | Medium, delayed | Asks what to take, where, how much; wants a recommendation; shopping for approval |
| 2 | Immediate aftermath | The Returner | "I need to hold onto this before it fades" | Slowing down, and structure for the next 14 days | Medium, but decision quality is poor | Medium | High if handled patiently | Euphoric certainty; wants to quit job or leave partner this week |
| 3 | Difficult experience | The Shaken | "Something is wrong with me" | Accurate assessment of whether this is coaching or clinical territory | Depends entirely on severity | High | Low, deliberately so | Any acute symptom cluster; functional collapse; escalating over weeks |
| 4 | Meaning-making | The Destabilized | "I don't know what is real anymore" | A framework for holding significance without requiring certainty | Medium to high | Medium | High | Fixed metaphysical certainty; wants confirmation, not examination |
| 5 | Behavior-change | The Stalled | "I understood something and nothing changed" | A behavioral structure, and relief from the shame of being stuck | High | Low | **Highest — the core client** | Almost none — this is the core client |
| 6 | Long-tail integration | The Faded | "That was two years ago and I'm back where I started" | To stop treating the experience as the event and start treating the return as the work | High | Low | High | Nostalgia loop with no willingness to change anything present-day |
| 7 | Repeat/escalation | The Re-doser | "The last one wasn't enough" | To have the dose-versus-structure question named without shame | Medium to high | High | High | Frequency increasing while function decreases — then it becomes segment 9 |
| 8 | Explicit support seeker | The Buyer | "I need an integration coach" | Confidence they're choosing a competent person, not a guru | Highest | Low | Highest per unit of effort | Wants a provider, a sitter, or someone to sit with them |
| 9 | Clinical, not coaching | The Wrong Room | "Coaching will help me" | Licensed local clinical care | None | Highest | Zero, permanently | Suicidality, psychosis-like symptoms, persistent derealization, inability to function, psychiatric instability, medical risk |
| 10 | Enthusiast/nonbuyer | The Audience | "I love this topic" | Identity and belonging | None | Low | Zero direct, useful as reach | Debating in comments, evangelism, no personal stuck state |

**Two notes on the table.** Segment 5 is the business. Segments 4, 6, 7, and 8 are adjacent enough that the same content reaches them. Segments 1, 2, 3, 9, and 10 are served for ethical and trust reasons, not commercial ones. **Segment 9 is not a difficult lead — it is not a lead. Treating it as one is the single largest reputational and legal risk in this entire operation.**

## Pain and emotion map

1. **The Researcher.** Frustration: every source is either selling something or warning about everything, no calibrated middle. Fear: that they're the person this goes badly for and have no way to know. State: anticipatory, over-informed, quietly anxious, slightly ashamed of how much time they've spent reading. Misconception: that more research reduces risk — past a point it becomes avoidance with a productive costume.
2. **The Returner.** Frustration: the vividness is already fading and they can feel it going. Fear: this becomes another thing they felt strongly about and did nothing with. State: open, flooded, often euphoric, poor judgment, high suggestibility. Misconception: that intensity of feeling predicts durability of change — it doesn't correlate well.
3. **The Shaken.** Frustration: nobody around them can tell the difference between "this is normal and settling" and "this needs a professional." Fear: they broke something permanent. State: frightened, isolated, often hiding it from partners and colleagues, googling symptoms at 2am. Misconception: that a coach or a community can assess them — they cannot, and should say so.
4. **The Destabilized.** Frustration: people closest to them treat what happened as either a hallucination or a revelation, neither usable. Fear: taking it seriously makes them irrational, dismissing it makes them a coward. State: unsettled, intellectually agitated, lonely in a specific way. Misconception: that they must decide whether it was true — significance and accuracy are separate questions.
5. **The Stalled.** Frustration: they know exactly what they should do and aren't doing it, and can no longer explain that to themselves. Fear: they're the problem, they lack something other people have, they're now the person who had the experience and wasted it. State: quietly embarrassed, self-critical, functional on the outside, mildly resentful of everyone posting about their transformation. Misconception: that this is a motivation or willpower failure — it's usually an absence of structure and a mismatch between insight and any specific next behavior.
6. **The Faded.** Frustration: they can narrate the experience beautifully and can't point to one thing it changed. Fear: it was never real, or they had the one chance and missed it. State: nostalgic, slightly deflated, a little cynical about the whole field now. Misconception: that the window closed — old material is workable, the insight doesn't expire, it just stopped being acted on.
7. **The Re-doser.** Frustration: each round produces less than the last and costs more, in money, time, and social capital. Fear: they're becoming the thing they judged, and stopping means admitting the first ones didn't work. State: hopeful on a loop, defensive when questioned, tired underneath. Misconception: that the variable is dose, setting, or venue — the variable is what happened in the fourteen days after.
8. **The Buyer.** Frustration: the market is full of people with a weekend certificate and a mystical vocabulary; they can't assess competence. Fear: paying someone who's either unqualified or evangelical. State: decisive, comparing, checking credentials, ready. Misconception: that credentials guarantee fit — method and boundaries matter more.
9. **The Wrong Room.** Frustration: clinical systems that dismiss the substance context entirely, and communities that dismiss the clinical need entirely. Fear: being pathologized, hospitalized, or not believed. State: acute, sometimes desperate, often seeking a non-clinical route specifically because the clinical one felt unsafe. Misconception: that a coach is a gentler clinician — a coach is not a clinician at all.
10. **The Audience.** Frustration: not really in pain — mild frustration at stigma and at being misunderstood. Fear: minimal. State: enthusiastic, identity-invested. Misconception: that interest in the topic is the same as work on themselves.

## Hidden-demand questions (never typed into search, never said out loud — each is a video)

- **Researcher:** Am I disqualified because of my family history? How do I know if I'm doing this for the right reason? Is it worse to do it alone or with a group I don't trust? What if I have a bad time and nobody can help me?
- **Returner:** How long until this feeling goes away? Should I be making decisions right now? Is it normal to want to tell everyone? Why do I feel like I can't explain it in words?
- **Shaken:** Is this permanent? How do I tell if this needs a doctor? Can I say this to a therapist without consequences? Why does the world still look slightly wrong three weeks later?
- **Destabilized:** Was that real or was it my brain? Does it matter which? How do I take this seriously without becoming someone I would have mocked? Why can't I explain this to my partner without sounding unwell?
- **Stalled:** Why did nothing change? Did I waste it? Is something wrong with me that it didn't stick? Why can I remember the insight and not feel it?
- **Faded:** Is it too late to do something with that? Was it just a nice night? Do I need a new experience or do I need to go back to the old one properly?
- **Re-doser:** How many is too many? Am I using this to avoid something? Why does each one do less? Is wanting another one a red flag?
- **Buyer:** How do I tell a serious practitioner from a mystic? What actually happens in these sessions? Is coaching worth this money if therapy is cheaper? What happens if something comes up that they can't handle?
- **Wrong Room:** Will they lock me up if I tell them what I took? Is there anyone who understands both sides? Am I going to be like this forever?

**The three highest-value questions across the entire audience, by commercial and content leverage:** "Why did nothing change." "Do I need to do it again." "Is this something a coach can help with, or do I need a doctor."

## Buying motivation map

| Segment | Trigger moment | What they're actually buying | Desired outcome, in their words | Price sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Researcher | A date gets booked | Risk reduction and permission | "I want to go in prepared, not naive" | High — wants free resources |
| Returner | Week 2, the fade becomes noticeable | Structure before the window closes | "I do not want to lose this" | Medium — emotional-spending risk, slow them down |
| Shaken | 2am symptom search | Assessment and reassurance | "I want to know if I am okay" | Not applicable — do not sell |
| Destabilized | A conversation goes badly with someone close | A framework and a competent interlocutor who won't laugh or worship it | "I want to think about this with someone who will not laugh or worship it" | Low — values intellectual quality |
| Stalled | Month 2 or 3, when the gap is undeniable | Relief from self-blame plus a concrete plan | "I want my life to actually look different" | Low — employed, decisive, pays for outcomes |
| Faded | An anniversary, or someone else's story | Permission that it's not too late | "I want to finally do something with it" | Low to medium |
| Re-doser | Booking or budgeting the next one | A reason to pause without shame | "I want the next one to count, or not to need it" | Low — already spending significant money on experiences |
| Buyer | Active search | Confidence in the practitioner | "I want someone competent and boundaried" | Lowest, fastest close |
| Wrong Room | Escalating symptoms | Care | Not applicable | Do not sell |
| Audience | None | Nothing | Nothing | Zero |

**Core commercial insight:** segments 5, 6, 7, and 8 are all buying the same thing — structure plus a competent person who won't evangelize. That's one offer, not four. **Second insight:** segment 7 (Re-doser) has the highest immediate willingness to pay of any segment, since they're already spending four figures on experiences — reallocating one experience budget into an integration container is the cleanest commercial argument available, and the honest one.

## Objection map

| Objection | Who says it | Response line |
|---|---|---|
| "I should be able to do this myself" | Stalled, Faded | "You probably can. Most people who could, already would have. The gap is usually structure, not capability." |
| "Coaching is just expensive talking" | Stalled, Destabilized | "Fair concern. The test is whether you leave with a specific behavioral plan or with a nice conversation. Ask any coach that before paying them." |
| "Should I not just see a therapist?" | Most segments | "Sometimes yes, and I will tell you when. Therapy works on why. Coaching works on what you do on Monday. They are not competitors." |
| "How do I know you're not another guru" | Buyer, Destabilized | "Reasonable. I do not provide experiences, I do not tell people what to take, and I refer out when it's not my scope. Judge me on the refusals, not the claims." |
| "Would it not be cheaper to just do another one" | Re-doser | "It might be. It's also the thing you already tried, twice. What would be different about this one?" |
| "It's been too long, the window closed" | Faded | "There's no evidence for an expiring window on meaning. The material is still there. What stopped was the acting on it." |
| "My situation is too unusual" | Destabilized, Shaken | Grant it, then narrow. "Possibly. Tell me what specifically you've tried to change and what happened." |
| "I don't want to be told what to do" | Stalled, executives generally | "I don't do that. You'll make the decisions. My job is to name the mechanism and hold the structure." |
| "I cannot afford it" | Faded, Researcher | Do not discount. Point to the free asset and the free group — discounting to this segment produces low-completion clients. |
| "I'm not sure I need help, I'm functioning fine" | Stalled | "Functioning is not the bar you came here about. You came here because something you understood is not showing up in your life." |

## Language bank

**Words they use that are safe to use back:** integration, nothing changed, it didn't stick, I went back to normal, the window, the comedown after the comedown, insight, ontological shock, made sense at the time, I cannot explain it to my partner, flat, numb, disconnected, not myself, everything looked slightly off, I felt everything and did nothing, I keep telling the story, set and setting, preparation, contraindications, screening, harm reduction, aftermath, difficult experience.

**Words they use that should NOT be adopted — mirroring these moves the brand toward the evangelist category and toward platform risk:** ceremony, plant medicine, medicine work, facilitator, sitter, shaman, journey, download, awakening, rewiring, trauma release, ego death (as a claim rather than a description), mother, "the medicine told me," called to it, held space, sacred, activation, transmission. When someone uses this vocabulary in a comment or DM, answer the content, not the vocabulary — do not correct it, do not adopt it.

**Exact phrases worth using as hooks, because they're how people actually phrase it:** "It has been three weeks and I am exactly the same." "I understood everything and I do not know what to do with it." "Do I need to do it again?" "I don't know if that was real." "My partner thinks I have lost it." "Was it worth it if nothing changed?"

**Likely comments, by type.** Qualified: "This is exactly where I am." "Six months later and I still talk about it more than I act on it." "How do you tell the difference between processing and avoiding?" Boundary-testing: "What did you take." "How much." "Where can I do this legally." "Can you recommend a place." Evangelist pushback: "You are overintellectualizing it." "The medicine gives you what you need." "This is Western reductionism." Distress: descriptions of ongoing symptoms, weeks or months out, sometimes with escalating detail. Noise: emojis, one-word agreement, tagging friends.

**Likely DMs, by segment:** Researcher: "I have a retreat booked in October and I'm reading everything. Is there anything you'd tell someone in my position." Returner: "I got back four days ago. I feel like I need to change everything. Is that normal?" Shaken: "Something happened and it hasn't gone away. I don't know who to ask." Destabilized: "I don't know how to talk about this without sounding insane. My wife thinks I'm having a breakdown." Stalled: "I did this eight months ago. I understood a lot. Nothing in my life is different. Is that something you work with?" Re-doser: "I've done this three times. Each one feels less useful. I was about to book a fourth." Buyer: "Do you take clients. What does the process look like and what does it cost." Wrong Room: acute symptom description, often with a request to talk urgently.

**The Stalled DM and the Buyer DM are the two the entire content system is optimized to produce.**

## Post angles by segment (production-ready openers — first line as spoken, tension in the first three seconds)

Full per-segment opener lines are preserved in the source document; representative examples per segment: **Researcher** — "You have researched this for four months. That is not preparation. That is a different activity." **Returner** — "You got back this week and you want to change everything. Wait fourteen days." **Shaken** — "If it has been weeks and the world still looks slightly wrong, this is not a coaching question." **Destabilized** — "It meant something. That does not mean it was true. Both can hold." **Stalled** — "Three weeks later, nothing has changed. That is the most common outcome and almost nobody says so." **Faded** — "That was two years ago and you still tell the story. What did it change?" **Re-doser** — "Before you book another one, answer this: what did you do differently after the last one?" **Buyer** — "Here is how to tell a serious practitioner from someone with a weekend certificate and a vocabulary." **Wrong Room** (no CTA, ever) — "Some of what people bring me is not a coaching problem, and I say so. Here is where that line is." **Audience** — "That study you keep seeing quoted had twenty-four participants and no control group. It's still interesting. It's not what you think it proves."

## Commercial priority ranking

**Tier 1 — build the content system around these:** (1) The Stalled — highest volume, highest fit, lowest risk, clearest offer; everything else is secondary. (2) The Buyer — smallest segment, fastest close, near-zero acquisition cost; serve with two pinned assets and a clean DM path, not volume. (3) The Re-doser — highest disposable budget already committed to this category; reachable with one specific argument delivered without judgment.

**Tier 2 — serve well, convert patiently:** (1) The Faded — same offer as the Stalled, longer decision cycle, needs permission more than persuasion. (2) The Destabilized — high engagement, strong share behavior, converts when intellectual quality earns respect.

**Tier 3 — serve for trust, not revenue:** The Returner — real future clients, but selling into week one produces bad decisions and bad clients; delay deliberately. The Researcher — free asset only, converts in six to twelve months if at all.

**Tier 4 — no commercial relationship:** The Shaken — serve with referral content; some resolve into segment 4 or 5 later and can be worked with then, never sold to while acute. The Audience — reach layer, zero conversion expectation, don't shape content for them. **The Wrong Room — refer, always, no exceptions, no later follow-up, no funnel.**

**Ratio check for the content calendar:** about 50% of output aimed at Tiers 1–2, 25% at Tier 3, 25% at referral literacy/evidence content that serves Tier 4 and protects the brand.

## Segments to avoid selling to

**Segment 9, The Wrong Room. Absolute.** Triggers, any one of which ends the commercial conversation permanently: suicidality or self-harm, psychosis-like symptoms, persistent derealization/depersonalization, severe dissociation, inability to work or care for themselves, escalating symptoms over weeks, existing psychiatric instability, current medication interactions, or medical risk. Response is a single message — outside coaching, licensed and local is the right next step, treat it as urgent if it worsens — then stop. No link, no offer mention, no follow-up in a month, no email list. If they return later, stable and functioning, that's a new conversation.

**Segment 3, The Shaken, while acute.** The temptation is real, because they're distressed, reaching out, and coaching sounds gentler than psychiatry — exactly why it's a trap. Distress is not a qualification. Refer, and mean it.

**Segment 2, The Returner, in week one.** Not an ethical prohibition, a quality one. Someone who signs up on day four is buying a feeling — they cancel by week three or complete the program in a state that produces no durable outcome. Tell them to wait two weeks; most serious ones come back, and they come back better clients.

**Segment 10, The Audience.** Not a risk, a leak. Discounting or chasing this group converts enthusiasm into low-completion clients and reshapes content toward the wrong signal.

**One structural rule covering all of the above:** no sales CTA appears on any content addressing acute distress, difficult experiences, or referral — not soft, not implied, not "DM me." The referral video ends with the referral. This constraint will feel commercially irrational, and it's the thing that makes every other piece of content in the system credible.
