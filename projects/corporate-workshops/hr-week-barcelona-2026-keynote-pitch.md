# HR Week Barcelona 2026 — Keynote Opportunity Brief

## Revised recommendation

Pitch one keynote:

# What If the Work AI Removes Is the Work That Lets Us Recover?

## When Every Human Task Becomes High-Value, Every Hour Becomes High-Strain

The belief to challenge is:

> Removing repetitive and low-value work automatically makes human work better.

Routine and lower-intensity tasks can also create natural valleys between cognitively or emotionally demanding peaks. When AI removes those tasks, it can compress the human role into a continuous sequence of judgment, ambiguity, creativity, exception handling, and relational work. An employee may perform fewer tasks, or even work fewer hours, while experiencing a more biologically demanding day.

This is not an argument for preserving pointless administration. It is an argument that HR must examine the **intensity pattern left behind after automation**, rather than counting only hours saved or tasks eliminated.

## Why this topic fits the event

HR Week Barcelona takes place on 10 November 2026 at Fira Gran Via. Its central theme is Human x AI Teaming, alongside the future of work, leadership, and organizational design.

The published program already contains strong sessions on:

- organizational resilience;
- the cognitive cost of AI and digital wellbeing;
- attention loss and the corporate “brain drain”;
- the effect of the workplace on the brain, energy, and performance;
- leadership without certainty;
- human agency and independent judgment in AI-enabled work;
- skills, hiring, inclusion, and AI adoption.

Therefore, a broad talk called “Decision Quality Under Load,” “The Neuroscience of Performance,” or “Performance Without Collapse” would risk sounding duplicative. The opportunity is to shift the unit of analysis from individual coping to **the distribution of intensity across the working day**:

> If AI takes the routine work and leaves humans with only the hardest human work, where do recovery, consolidation, and cognitive closure now happen?

The agenda still shows topics to be confirmed, states that it may be updated, and says that more speakers are coming. The program is mature, so this should be treated as a specific gap-filling proposal or backup-speaker opportunity, not an open-ended speaker application.

## Revised proposed abstract

HR has largely accepted a compelling promise: let AI remove repetitive, low-value tasks so people can concentrate on meaningful, high-value work. But what if some of those routine tasks also functioned as the lower-intensity intervals between demanding cognitive and emotional peaks?

As AI takes over drafting, searching, summarizing, and administration, the human role can become a continuous sequence of judgment, ambiguity, creativity, exception handling, and emotional labour. People may complete fewer tasks, or even work fewer hours, while experiencing a more biologically demanding day.

This keynote challenges HR to look beyond tasks eliminated and time saved. Vlad Andreev offers a practical way to map the intensity pattern left behind: decision density, context switching, emotional demand, and opportunities for recovery and cognitive closure. The goal is not to preserve busywork or slow AI adoption. It is to design Human x AI work that remains humanly sustainable when every task left to people is supposedly “high value.”

## Audience takeaways

Participants will leave with four practical questions:

1. **What intensity did the removed task have?** Not all tasks consume attention and emotional capacity equally.
2. **What now sits next to what?** Did automation turn a mixed-intensity day into consecutive high-demand work?
3. **What is the decision density?** How many consequential judgments, exceptions, and context shifts now occupy the same period?
4. **Where does cognitive closure happen?** What allows people to consolidate, reset, and prepare for the next demanding interval?

The keynote should end with an HR action model:

- audit load transfer, not only adoption and hours saved;
- map task intensity and sequencing, not only task value;
- protect human judgment where consequences and ambiguity are high;
- redesign decision cadence, handoffs, and availability norms;
- treat recovery as part of performance architecture, not an employee benefit;
- evaluate repeatable output rather than isolated productivity peaks.
