# Atomic voice rules

- VOICE-001: Correct misattribution before giving advice.
- VOICE-002: Mechanism precedes recommendation.
- VOICE-003: Return agency to the reader.
- VOICE-004: Use short sentences when stating a well-supported conclusion.
- VOICE-005: Prefer structural metaphors that clarify rather than dramatize.
- VOICE-006: Never substitute intensity for evidence.
- VOICE-007: Distinguish evidence, hypothesis, observation, and opinion.
- VOICE-008: Speak as an expert peer, not a guru.
- VOICE-009: Do not use em dashes in public copy.
- VOICE-010: Avoid motivational endings.
- VOICE-011: One piece carries one central idea.
- VOICE-012: Personal data demonstrates practice, not universal efficacy.
- VOICE-013: Name uncertainty where it changes the decision.
- VOICE-014: Do not diagnose the reader through a hook.
- VOICE-015: Useful action must remain inside the stated scope.
- VOICE-016: Rank and cut. State which one or two points matter and why; do not give every point equal weight.
- VOICE-017: Do not repeat a sentence template within one piece ("not just X, but Y," "whether X or Y," "by doing X, you can Y," "from X to Y").
- VOICE-018: Avoid participial significance tails ("…, highlighting/underscoring/ensuring…"). State the finding directly or cut the clause.
- VOICE-019: Use the number of list items the evidence actually requires. Exactly three is a tell, not a target.
- VOICE-020: Hedge only the uncertain part of a claim, and name why it is uncertain. A blanket disclaimer protects the writer, not the reader.
- VOICE-021: Every load-bearing paragraph carries one situated detail: a date, threshold, population, mechanism, constraint, or named source.
- VOICE-022: Match structure to the platform. Headings, tables, or bullet stacks copied from another format are themselves a tell.
- VOICE-023: Cut any sentence that could appear unchanged in any other piece on the same topic.
